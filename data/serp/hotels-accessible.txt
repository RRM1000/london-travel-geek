# Rob's rule for the hotel corpora: evidence comes from guides and especially
# BLOGGERS. Booking engines are not sources - they rank by inventory and
# commission, so a listing says nothing about whether anyone rates the place.
# Brand sites are out for the same reason. Hotels.com is a validator only.
#
# EXCLUDED as AI/aggregator template farms - see hotel-corpus.md for the tells:
#   lamplitlondon.com, explorecities.com, luxuryhotel.guide, boutiquehotel.guru,
#   new-hotels-guide.com, where-stay.com, choosewhere.com, hotelradar.co.uk,
#   pmahotels.org, taketravelinfo.com, myboutiquehotel.com, dyme.earth,
#   bestboutiquehotelsworldwide.com, theluxuryeditor.com, blog.hotelslash.com
#
# THIS TOPIC HAS A HIGHER BAR THAN THE OTHERS. An accessibility claim is one a
# reader acts on and gets stranded by. A name appearing in a list is not
# evidence a hotel is accessible - only a specific, attributable claim is
# ("18 accessible rooms, two with ceiling track hoists"). Loose wording
# ("accessible rooms available", "consistently praised") is not usable and
# must not be repeated on the site. Nothing goes on a page from one source's
# say-so where that source did not say what it actually checked.
#
# DROPPED IN THIS PASS
#   absolutemobility.co.uk - a mobility-equipment retailer's blog, and a
#     substantive piece of desk research (2,712 words, it even criticises
#     Euan's Guide for thin coverage). Dropped anyway because of where its
#     hotel names come from. The Premier Inn and Travelodge sections restate
#     those brands' own access statements - brand sites, barred by the rule
#     above. The "Other Accessible London Hotels" section says outright: "Our
#     booking.com accessible hotel search returned the following hotels",
#     i.e. eleven names pasted straight out of a Booking.com accessibility
#     filter. Counting it would launder a brand site and a booking engine into
#     the corpus as if they were an editorial voice. Author is "Sarah", first
#     name only; the piece never claims anyone visited; unedited throughout
#     ("ofter", "accomadation", "woudl", "acutually").
#   disabilityid.co.uk - the National Disability Card's marketing blog. Fails
#     all three of the README's farm tests at once: no named writer (the
#     schema author is the Organization "National Disability Card"), no
#     evidence of a visit, and the page exists to sell the card. The rest of
#     the blog is PIP freebies, discounted gift cards and card-partner news -
#     no travel reporting at all. Its hotel claims are attributed to nobody
#     ("often highlighted as a benchmark", "consistently praised") and the
#     star ratings beside each name are lifted aggregate booking scores, not
#     an accessibility measure. Its specifics (4 accessible rooms at
#     Bermondsey Square, 8 at Novotel Greenwich, a pool hoist at Hilton
#     Wembley) are recorded in the run report as unverified leads, but the
#     domain is not counted as an independent voice.
#
# KEPT AFTER VETTING
#   blog.wheeltheworld.com - Wheel the World is a booking platform, and its
#     blog is kept anyway. Named author (Ethan Godard, with a photo and an
#     author page), and the London piece carries measured, checkable access
#     data rather than adjectives: bed heights in inches per property, turning
#     space, roll-in shower, shower seat, grab bars, and a bed hoist called
#     out at Pan Pacific. That is the opposite of ranking by commission. Two
#     caveats to carry: the list can only contain properties Wheel the World
#     has mapped and sells, so it is inventory-bounded and says nothing about
#     what it omits; and it was published January 2024 and last touched March
#     2024, which is old for facilities claims.
#
