British alternative comedy started in a former Soho strip club in 1979, and the interesting thing about London comedy now is how little it costs. **The best-value night out in this city is a comedy night**, and several of the best ones are free.

The trade-off is not quality — it is certainty. A £30 ticket buys you a booked bill of professionals. A free ticket buys you five acts, two of whom are working something out, one of whom is about to be famous.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **The Comedy Store** is where all of this began and still the benchmark. **Angel Comedy** runs free stand-up seven nights a week across two pubs and is the best value in London. **Top Secret** is the cheap central option where big names drop in unannounced. **Always Be Comedy** in Kennington gets the surprise guests. And the **Comedy Store Players** have been improvising on the same stage since 1985.

## Where it started

### The Comedy Store, Soho

*££ · 3 min from Piccadilly Circus*

Don Ward opened it in **May 1979**, in the old Gargoyle nightclub on Dean Street, after seeing what the new clubs in New York and Los Angeles were doing. What came out of it was British alternative comedy — observational, political, and aimed squarely at the Establishment.

It has moved since, and it is still the room every British comedian measures themselves against.

**The Comedy Store Players** have performed here since **October 1985** — Paul Merton, Josie Lawrence, Neil Mullarkey, Richard Vranch, Lee Simpson and others. *Whose Line Is It Anyway?* took their short-form improv to Channel 4 in 1988, and **Guinness confirmed in 2010 that it is the world's longest-running comedy show with the same cast**.

> 💡 **The Players play Sundays and Wednesdays.** If you only see one comedy show in London, this is the one with the strongest claim on your evening — it is improv rather than stand-up, and it has been running for forty years.

### Top Secret Comedy Club, Covent Garden

*£–££ · 4 min from Covent Garden*

A basement in the middle of the West End running shows most nights at prices well below what the location suggests. Its reputation rests on **unannounced drop-ins** — established comedians working out material in a room of 100 people who paid a few pounds.

The Thursday and Sunday nights are the cheapest, and no worse for it.

### Backyard Comedy Club, Bethnal Green

*££ · 5 min from Bethnal Green*

A purpose-built club rather than a room above a pub, founded by the comedian Lee Hurst. Proper sightlines, a proper stage, and booked bills — the east London answer to The Comedy Store.

## Free, and genuinely good

This is the part of London comedy that surprises visitors. These are not open-mic nights in the pejorative sense; they are free rooms with professional acts.

### Angel Comedy at The Camden Head, Islington

*Free · every single night*

**Free stand-up every night of the week**, with no exceptions, in a pub near Angel. The **RAW** nights on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Sunday are where new material gets tested; Friday and Saturday are the polished bills.

Between three and six consecutive shows run each night, so you can stay for one or stay for the evening.

> ⚠️ **Free does not mean easy to get into.** At weekends, arrive up to **ninety minutes early** to collect a stamp. This is the single most common way people miss out.

### Angel Comedy at The Bill Murray, Islington

*Free seats · seven nights*

Angel Comedy's second venue, a refurbished pub a short walk from the first, running **one to three shows a night, seven nights a week** — new-material nights, Edinburgh previews, and professional bills. **At least a few free seats at most shows.**

The Edinburgh preview season, roughly June to early August, is when this room is at its best: full-length shows for a few pounds before they go north and cost thirty.

### Comedy Virgins

*Free · four pubs across London*

Free nights running at **Signature Brew in Haggerston, The Railway in Putney, The Gipsy Queen in Kentish Town and The Wheatsheaf in Tooting Bec**. Open-mic in structure but with a long-standing reputation, and the most geographically spread of the free options — there is usually one near wherever you are staying.

### Comedy Cabin, Hoxton

*Free Thursdays · around £5 otherwise*

A small Hoxton room mixing established acts with new ones. **Free on Thursdays**, and rarely more than a fiver the rest of the time.

## Where the locals actually go

Cheap neighbourhood nights with line-ups that embarrass their ticket prices.

### Always Be Comedy, Kennington

*£ · a pub back room*

Widely rated one of the best comedy nights in London, in a Kennington pub, and known for **big-name special guests turning up unannounced** alongside the booked bill. If you want the story of having seen someone famous in a room of eighty people, this is where it happens.

### The Harrison, King's Cross

*£ · around £4*

**Around four pounds** for a bill mixing up-and-coming acts with comedians you will recognise from television. The cheapest good comedy in central London, in the back of a King's Cross pub.

### Happy Mondays Comedy, New Cross

*£ · around £5*

Some of the biggest names in British stand-up alongside newcomers, in New Cross, for **about five pounds**. A Monday-night institution in south-east London.

### Knock2Bag at MOTH Club, Hackney

*£–££ · Valette Street*

Big names and hot new acts in the **glittery ex-servicemen's club** that is one of the best small rooms in London for anything. Tickets cost a little more than the pub nights and the line-ups justify it.

MOTH Club's tinsel-and-Artex ceiling is worth seeing on its own account.

> **Knock2Bag is sometimes listed as free. It is not** — mailing-list subscribers occasionally get free or heavily reduced tickets, which is probably where the confusion comes from.

## The rest of the circuit

### Up the Creek, Greenwich

*££ · around 275 · Thu–Sun*

Founded in **1991 by Malcolm Hardee** in an old church hall, and still the most gleefully hostile room in London — **the heckling is part of the format** rather than an interruption of it, and comedians either handle it or are eaten. It won the **first ever Chortle Award for Best Large Venue** in 2002.

Not the room for a nervous first-timer, and exactly the room if you want to see what stand-up is actually like without a net.

### 2Northdown, King's Cross

*£ · 100 seats · 2 min from King's Cross St Pancras*

Opened in **June 2017** and now one of the industry's favourite rooms for previews and works-in-progress — the place comedians take a show that is not finished yet. A hundred seats, step-free from the street, and two minutes from the station.

### Soho Theatre, Soho

*££ · Dean Street*

Less a comedy club than the room where comedy becomes a show. New theatre, cabaret and full-length stand-up hours, and the London stop for most Edinburgh transfers.

**There is now a second venue, Soho Theatre Walthamstow**, on Hoe Street — a restored art deco cinema, and worth the Victoria line trip for the building alone.

### Camden Comedy Club, Camden Town

*£ · above a pub*

Above the Camden Head — not the Islington one, confusingly — with a consistent programme of touring acts and previews.

### Covent Garden Comedy Club, Covent Garden

*££ · central*

A long-running central club that trades on convenience and a reliable weekend bill. Not the most adventurous room in London, and the easiest to fit around a theatre trip.

### Big Belly Comedy Club, Waterloo

*£–££ · a vaulted cellar*

In a brick cellar bar near Waterloo, with late shows at weekends. The room is a large part of the appeal.

## What to know

* **The free nights are the best value in London**, and they are not a lesser product — Angel Comedy books professionals.
* **Arrive early for anything free.** Ninety minutes at The Camden Head on a Saturday is not an exaggeration.
* **June to early August is Edinburgh preview season.** Full shows, small rooms, a few pounds, before the same show costs three times as much at the Fringe.
* **Front-row seats get spoken to.** At every club on this page. Sit further back if that is not what you want.
* **Most pub nights are cash-light but card-friendly**, and the ticket price does not include a drink.
* **Check the line-up the same week.** Comedy bills change constantly and a name announced a month out is not a guarantee.
* **Angel Comedy is free seven nights a week at The Camden Head**, but The Bill Murray also runs paid shows — the two venues are not the same offer.
