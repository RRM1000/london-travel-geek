Eight published guides to halal food in London name 132 restaurants between them. Only thirteen are named by more than one, and the three most-cited are Gymkhana, Pizza Pilgrims and Dishoom.

None of the three is a halal restaurant in the sense a reader searching this phrase almost certainly means. Pizza Pilgrims is a pizza chain that offers halal pepperoni. Dishoom serves pork. Gymkhana does not describe itself as halal.

So rather than count mentions, this guide checked each restaurant's halal status **at the restaurant** — its own website, FAQ and menu — and groups them by what it found. Someone is relying on the answer for a religious dietary requirement.

Three things get printed as "halal" in list articles and they are not the same:

1. **Certified.** A third-party body such as the Halal Monitoring Committee has audited the kitchen.
2. **Halal, on the kitchen's own word.** The restaurant sources halal meat and says so. Common, and not an audit.
3. **Halal options.** Part of the menu, or in the weakest cases merely the absence of pork.

The sections below follow that order. Alcohol is recorded where the restaurant makes it clear; whether a licensed bar is acceptable is for you to decide.

## The register almost nobody cites

The **Halal Monitoring Committee** (HMC), a UK halal certifier, publishes its entire list of certified outlets. It covers **over 700 outlets across the UK**, of which around **240 are in London** — and around **155 of those are restaurants and takeaways**.

Cross-referencing the register against all 132 venues named by the eight published halal guides produces **no matches at all.** The restaurants London's food media calls the best halal restaurants and the restaurants that have been audited are two separate populations.

They are separate geographically too. Of the certified restaurants, **about half are in E postcodes and about two dozen are in Ilford**, with clusters in Norbury, Thornton Heath, Stoke Newington and Wembley. **Two are in central London** — Oodles Wok in Holborn and Tikkas at Oxford Circus.

Some of the better-known certified rooms: **Aziziye** on Stoke Newington Road (N16 8BU), the Turkish restaurant beneath the tiled mosque; **Dilara Uyghur Restaurant** in Finsbury Park (N4 2JF); **Samarkand** in Stoke Newington (N16 7XJ) for Central Asian cooking; **Anatolia** in Leyton (E10 5QN); and **Toro's Steakhouse** in Tooting (SW17 7BA).

**Read it yourself at [halalhmc.org/outlets-by-name](https://halalhmc.org/outlets-by-name/)**. Certification lapses and is withdrawn, so check it shortly before you go.

The **Halal Food Authority** is the UK's other major certifier, but it publishes no consumer-facing list of certified restaurants: it certifies food production, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals for businesses. HMC's register is the only one a diner can check.

## Halal throughout, on the kitchen's own word

The restaurant's own statement that the whole kitchen is halal is the evidence here. For most halal kitchens in London that is what there is.

### Souk — North African, for large groups

*££ · Covent Garden · WC2H 9NJ*

Asked "Is your food Halal?" in its own FAQ, Souk answers: **"Yes it's 100% Halal HMC certified."** If an independently audited kitchen is what you need, ask when you book.

It has cooked in central London **since 1998**: **tagines, couscous and charcoal grills**, in a low-lit basement fitted out with lanterns, rugs and cushioned banquettes.

There is a **set group menu** alongside the à la carte, and the room is built for long tables and noise rather than a quiet dinner.

### The Great Chase — halal fine dining with an alcohol-free bar

*£££ · Islington Square · N1 1WL*

Its page title reads **halal fine dining and an alcohol-free bar**, its sourcing statement reads **"High-welfare, and fully Halal,"** and the site has a page called *Why Halal?*

**Binchotan-grilled steak, dry-aged beef, Beef Wellington, and a Sunday roast** with a lamb roast alongside the beef. The bar runs on **rare teas and botanical dry cocktails**.

**Closed Mondays.** Tuesday 5.30pm to 9pm, Wednesday to Friday 5.30pm to 10pm, Saturday noon to 10pm, Sunday noon to 9pm (roasts only). The Sunday roast needs booking, and the largest table seats six.

### HS&Co — a British steakhouse where the beef is halal

*££ · Stratford · E15 4QZ*

**"Proudly fully Halal"**, on its own homepage, from a restaurant that has been running since **2016** and calls itself Stratford's original British steakhouse. It describes its kitchen as **hand-cut, dry-aged halal prime steaks**, with British classics on the menu alongside.

It is a few minutes from the Elizabeth line.

### Cue Point — Afghan-led barbecue

*££ · Notting Hill · The Garden Bar, Grill & Smokehouse, 41 Bramley Road, W10 6SZ*

Its site says all its food is halal, and that alcohol is served. Director **Mursal Saiq's Afghan heritage runs through the branding, the ethos and the recipes**, and head chef **Joshua Moroney** brings smoked barbecue technique drawn from Argentinian, Texan, Afghan, Guyanese and Brazilian traditions. In practice that means **brisket burnt ends and hot link sausages served alongside torshi**, the Afghan pickles. Time Out opens its halal guide with it.

Saiq and Moroney also run the business as a **training route for refugees**, with professional catering qualifications alongside language teaching.

After years of moving between residencies it has a permanent site in Notting Hill. **Open Tuesday to Saturday noon to 10pm and Sunday noon to 8pm; closed Monday.** Book.

### Berenjak — Persian kababs, halal at every London site

*££ · Soho and Borough*

Asked in its FAQ whether it serves halal meat, Berenjak answers: **"We do serve halal meat at all our London restaurants."** That confirms the meat, and confirms it **across every branch**.

The cooking is Tehran hole-in-the-wall food: **kababs over charcoal, mahi and jujeh, flatbread baked in the room, and the salads and torshi that go with them.** The Soho original is a narrow counter-led space where you watch the grill work; Borough is the larger room. It is a JKS restaurant.

This is a statement about the meat rather than the whole operation: the restaurants are licensed.

## Halal for part of the menu

These are restaurants with a halal component, not halal restaurants.

### Honest Burgers — the most detailed published policy of any chain here

*£ · Branches across London*

Honest Burgers publishes its full policy:

**All chicken in its restaurants is halal.** The supplier's halal certificate **can be requested from a server** on arrival. Halal products are **stored separately**, with processes intended to stop cross-contamination. Halal chicken is **handled with separate utensils and gloves**. Fried halal chicken burgers and wings **do not share fryers** with non-halal items.

**The beef is not halal**, and the restaurants serve beer from local breweries: a halal chicken burger in a licensed restaurant that is not halal throughout.

### Dishoom — halal-certified suppliers, and a bacon naan on the same menu

*££ · Branches across London*

Halal round-ups often describe Dishoom as verified or fully halal. It is not.

Dishoom states that **its lamb, chicken and turkey are sourced from halal-certified suppliers**, and that it **holds copies of the certificates**.

It also serves **pork**, including the bacon naan, and it is licensed. The certification covers the supply of certain meats rather than the kitchen. Whether that is acceptable is your call.

### Pizza Pilgrims — halal pepperoni, and a per-branch answer

*£ · Branches across London and beyond*

Named by four of the eight lists in this pass.

The company says **halal pepperoni is available at no extra cost**, describes the spicy turkey pepperoni it sources as its halal solution, and says **all its cheese is halal**, which rules out animal rennet. **"Halal Options Available" is flagged branch by branch** on its locations list rather than applied to the company as a whole, so check the branch you are going to. The pizza is Neapolitan.

### Rasa Sayang — Malaysian and Singaporean hawker food in Chinatown

*£ · Chinatown · W1D 6AY*

Rasa Sayang describes itself as born out of the hawker scene of Malaysia and Singapore, and its site refers to **its halal offerings**, which points to part of the menu rather than the whole kitchen.

**Hawker staples, quick and cheap**, in a loud room.

**Monday to Friday noon to 10pm, Saturday 11.30am to 10pm, Sunday 11.30am to 9pm.**

## What to do with this

Check the restaurant and the register, not the list.

**The register is free.** HMC publishes around 155 certified restaurants and takeaways in London and not one of them appears in any published best-of list. If you want independent verification, start there.

**Multi-branch restaurants answer per branch.** Pizza Pilgrims flags it site by site; Berenjak confirms that all its London restaurants are covered. Never assume a brand answer applies to the branch you are walking into.

**Certification, sourcing and options are three different claims.** A kitchen audited by a named body, a kitchen giving you its word, and a menu with a halal item on it are not interchangeable. Everything on this page tells you which of the three you are getting.
