The NFL plays three games in London in October 2026, on three consecutive Sundays, at two stadiums that have almost nothing in common. The Jacksonville Jaguars play twice, a week apart, at both grounds. Every one kicks off at **2.30pm BST**.

The part visitors get wrong is the ticketing. There is no single NFL London box office: the two Tottenham games are sold by the league, the Wembley game is sold by the Jaguars, and they use different accounts, different price bands, different child rules and different delivery. An account for one is no use for the other.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Colts v Commanders, Sunday 4 October** and **Eagles v Jaguars, Sunday 11 October**, both at **Tottenham Hotspur Stadium**; **Texans v Jaguars, Sunday 18 October**, at **Wembley**. All **2.30pm**. The Tottenham games are sold by the NFL and need an **NFL Ticketmaster account**, six tickets maximum per account per game, **£79.13 to £280.65** depending on the game and the seat, all fees included. The Wembley game is sold by the **Jaguars** on their own site, **£70 to £297**, with **under-16s at £51**. **Gates open two hours before kick-off**; the free **Gameday Experience** in N17 runs **10am to 2pm**; there is **no tailgating**. You **can** drink in your seat — the football alcohol ban does not apply — but it is **four drinks per purchase**, **18+ with photo ID**, and both stadiums are **completely cashless**. Bags must be **clear and no bigger than 30.5 x 15.25 x 30.5cm**, with **nowhere on site to leave one that fails**. No ticket? All three are **free to air on 5**.

## Tickets, and the two systems behind them

### The Tottenham games: sold by the NFL

![Miami Dolphins and Jacksonville Jaguars players in action on the synthetic turf at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium](../../assets/articles/nfl-london-games/dolphins-jaguars-tottenham.jpg)

You need an **NFL Ticketmaster account** to buy any 2026 NFL game in Europe. Set it up at [nfl.com/international/ticketaccount](https://www.nfl.com/international/ticketaccount) before you try to buy, not during. Selling opened in two stages: **hospitality from midday BST on Friday 5 June**, **general admission from noon BST on Tuesday 7 July**. Both games are handled through the [NFL's official Tottenham ticket site](https://www.eticketing.co.uk/nfl-tottenham).

2026 is the first season the NFL has used all-in pricing on its international games, so the figure you see when you start choosing seats is the figure you pay. There is no fee added at the end.

Three things follow from that table. **Child tickets exist in two categories only** — 4 and 9 — so a family sitting together is choosing between the £200-a-seat band and the cheapest one, with nothing in between. **Category 9 costs the same for both games** while the expensive end is £35 dearer for the Eagles, which tells you where the demand is. And **the limit is six tickets per account, per game**; the NFL cancels transactions it reads as bot purchases or as duplicate accounts opened to get round the cap.

**Accessible seating is not sold online.** Wheelchair and ambulant seats are deliberately kept off the public map, and you apply through the NFL's enquiry form; the league's stated turnaround is a response within five working days, and proof of eligibility may be asked for in advance or at the ground. Do not leave this to the last fortnight.

**Your ticket arrives digitally, and only to the person who booked.** There are no paper tickets for the Tottenham games. Download links go to the lead booker's email no later than 72 hours before kick-off, and every person attending needs their own ticket saved to Apple Wallet or Google Pay on their own phone — the lead booker forwards the email and tells each person which seat number to download. Get this wrong and two people arrive holding the same QR code.

> ⚠️ **One of the two Tottenham games is gone.** As of 21 September 2026, **Eagles v Jaguars on 11 October has no availability** on the official site, in general admission or in the accessible allocation. **Colts v Commanders on 4 October is still on sale.** Hospitality is sold separately, including a Premium Sideline Central package with a lounge next to the designated team locker room.

The child rules are tighter than Tottenham's. **Under-16 tickets exist only in categories 5, 9 and 10**, every child ticket has to be bought alongside an adult ticket **in the same price category**, and the ratio is capped at **three under-16s to one adult**. Wembley also operates a **flat ban on under-2s** — no babies in arms at all, where the NFL lets an under-2 in free on a ticket collected from the Tottenham ticket office on the day. If you are travelling with a baby, the 18 October game is not open to you.

Delivery is different too: Wembley tickets go out roughly **ten working days before the game**, not 72 hours.

### What you are allowed to do with a ticket you cannot use

**Sell it at face value, through the official channel, or not at all.**

The NFL's ticket terms for Tottenham are unusually blunt about this. Commercial resale is reserved to the NFL and Ticketmaster; you may not put the ticket on an auction site, list it on a third-party exchange, sell it above face value beyond genuine transaction costs, or pass it to a ticket dealer. Private redistribution is permitted through the **NFL Ticket Exchange**, and a ticket found to have moved any other way can be cancelled, with the buyer refused entry and nobody refunded. For Wembley, the Jaguars point ticket holders at **Ticketmaster's Last Available Ticket service** inside their eTicketing account as the only official route; the date it opens has not been announced.

The legal position is worth understanding before you buy from a stranger. **Ticket touting is a criminal offence in the UK only for football**: section 166 of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 covers designated football matches and nothing else. An NFL ticket sold at four times face value on a resale site breaks no law — it just breaks the contract, and the contract is enforced at the turnstile by cancelling the barcode. You would have no realistic way to get your money back.

## The day itself, end to end

A 2.30pm kick-off sounds like an afternoon out. It is most of a day.

| Time | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| **10.00** | **NFL Gameday Experience** opens in the streets around the ground — free, no ticket |
| **12.30** | **Gates open** for general admission ticket holders, two hours before kick-off |
| **14.00** | The NFL asks you to be **in your seat**, half an hour out, for the pre-game build-up |
| **14.30** | **Kick-off** |
| **early evening** | Final whistle, then a controlled walk back to the stations |

*Timings from the [NFL's 2026 London Games gameday guide](https://www.nfl.com/news/2026-nfl-london-games-gameday-guide), checked 21 September 2026. Wembley has not published its entry times for 18 October.*

The **Gameday Experience** is what London has instead of a car park full of grills: a free street event from 10am to 2pm with the McCoy's Quarterback Challenge, DJs, cheerleaders, mascots, the official NFL shop selling London Games merchandise, and food and drink stalls. It closes as the gates fill.

**The game runs far longer than a football match, and not because of the rules.** Rule 4 of the NFL rulebook gives the game 60 minutes of clock in four 15-minute quarters, with a 13-minute halftime and two-minute breaks between the other quarters — 73 minutes on paper against a football match's 105. But the clock stops on every incomplete pass, every change of possession and every score, and the broadcast takes its breaks in those gaps. A football crowd that goes in at 3pm is out by a quarter to five. An NFL crowd that goes in at 12.30 is out in the early evening.

Two rules catch people at the gate:

- **There is no re-entry.** The NFL's ticket for the Tottenham games is a licence to enter once. Stepping out for air, or to find a cash machine, ends your afternoon.
- **Both stadiums are entirely cashless.** No kiosk at either ground takes notes or coins. At Tottenham a physical contactless card is capped at £100 a tap, with chip and PIN above that; Apple Pay and Google Pay have no such ceiling.

> ⚠️ **The bag rule is the one that ruins the morning.** The **NFL Clear Bag Policy** applies at both grounds: a clear plastic, vinyl or PVC bag no larger than **30.5 x 15.25 x 30.5cm**, or a one-gallon clear freezer bag, plus one small clutch up to **11.5 x 16.5cm**. Anything else is refused, and **neither stadium will store it for you** — there is no cloakroom and no bag drop. This is stricter than Tottenham's normal A4 rule for football, so a bag that got you into a Spurs match will not get you into this one. Sort storage before you travel; our [luggage storage guide](/articles/luggage-storage-london/) covers the staffed options near the main stations.

No food, drink or liquid of any kind comes in with you. The one exception is an **empty, transparent, reusable plastic bottle up to a litre** for the water points. Fixed-shaft umbrellas are banned at any size; a collapsible one under 61cm may come in but may not be opened inside. The ground is smoke-free throughout, vapes included — you may carry one, you may not use it.

## What is different from a game at home

**There is no tailgating.** The NFL says so itself, in its own gameday guide for Tottenham: there is no traditional tailgate. The streets around the ground are residential and the roads close hours before kick-off. The Gameday Experience from 10am is the sanctioned substitute.

**You can drink in your seat, which British fans cannot at the football.** The Sporting Events (Control of Alcohol etc.) Act 1985 makes it an offence to have alcohol in any part of a ground from which the event can be directly viewed — but only at a *designated* sporting event, and the 2005 designation order lists association football and nothing else. An NFL game is not designated. So alcohol is sold at concession stands inside the bowl and goes to your seat with you. The conditions: **18 or over**, **government-issued photo ID** under a Challenge 25 policy, and a hard limit of **four alcoholic drinks per purchase**. Being drunk inside a ground is itself an offence under the same 1985 Act, and ejection is without refund.

**Bring photo ID you can hand over.** A Challenge 25 bar wants something official with your date of birth on it. A passport does the job; leaving it in the hotel safe and hoping does not.

**Your kick-off is everyone else's lie-in.** A 2.30pm London kick-off is 9.30am on the US east coast, which is why it sits alone on the Sunday card. The rest of the slate starts at 6pm UK time, so the game finishing in the early evening lands you straight into the main Sunday programme — most sports pubs in London will be turning the sound up on it as you arrive.

## Getting home on a Sunday evening

Tottenham has put more than 120,000 spectators through the gates across back-to-back NFL weekends in 2019, 2022 and 2023 — roughly 60,000 leaving at once — and Wembley can put up to 90,000 onto three stations. On a Sunday that lands on the weakest transport night of the week.

**The Night Tube runs on Friday and Saturday only**, so neither ground has it on a game day. Nor does a big event buy you a later train: the Sunday timetable is the Sunday timetable, whatever time the final whistle goes. Check the line you need against your date before you commit to a late dinner in town.

Beyond that, the two grounds are different problems, and the detail belongs with each:

- **Tottenham**: four stations, White Hart Lane closest at five minutes, no Night Tube equivalent on the Overground line that serves it, and a queue-management system that sends you back by a different route than you came. The station-by-station breakdown and the last trains are in our [Tottenham Hotspur Stadium guide](/articles/tottenham-hotspur-stadium-travel-guide/). Taxis and private hire cannot reach the ground: the NFL's own advice is to arrange a pick-up at least a 15-minute walk away.
- **Wembley**: Wembley Park's 33 ticket gates absorb most of the crowd, Chiltern's last train to Marylebone goes around midnight whatever the event, and the official car parks are £50. The queue windows, the car parks a stop out and the hotel prices are in our [Wembley guide](/articles/wembley-stadium-arena-guide/).

If you are planning around step-free routes rather than speed, our [step-free London guide](/articles/step-free-london/) covers which of these stations actually deliver it.

## What else is on those weekends

October is the busiest month in the London calendar, and all three NFL Sundays fall inside it.

- **The BFI London Film Festival runs 7 to 18 October**, so the 11th and the 18th are both festival days, with a release of held-back tickets at 10am on 1 October. Prices, and the four routes into a sold-out screening, are in our [London Film Festival guide](/articles/london-film-festival/).

- **The Jaguars run a free draw for two passes to their practice session on Friday 16 October**, open to UK and Ireland residents aged 18 or over. [Entries close on 25 September](https://jaguars.co.uk/join-the-practice-session-draw/) and winners are told on 30 September.

## Before you fly

**Get your UK ETA first.** Americans, Canadians, Australians and New Zealanders all need one for a visa-free visit now, it costs **£20**, and it must be approved before you board — most decisions arrive within a day, but the official window is up to three working days. Apply only at gov.uk or through the official UK ETA app; the copycat sites charge more and do nothing you cannot do yourself. The detail, including what happens if you are refused, is in our [UK ETA guide](/articles/uk-eta-guide/).

**Sort payment before you land.** Both stadiums are entirely cashless and so is most of London transport. A contactless card or a phone wallet is all you need; changing dollars into notes for a game day is money you cannot spend inside the ground. [How the Oyster and contactless system works](/articles/oyster-card-guide-london/) explains the daily caps, which matter on a day when you are crossing the city twice.

**Charge your phone.** Your ticket lives in it, there is no paper fallback, and the walk back to the station is long enough that you will want the map.
