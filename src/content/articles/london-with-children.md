London is unusually good with children and most of the best of it is free. There are **eight city farms** you can walk into for nothing, a museum built entirely around children, and a thousand fountains in the paving at King's Cross.

The expensive attractions are fine. They are just not where the good stuff is.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Young V&A** is free and built for children. The **city farms** are all free. **Granary Square fountains** are the best free thing in London on a hot day. **Babylon Park** is three underground floors of rides in Camden. And **Mail Rail** puts you on a train inside real Post Office tunnels.

## Free city farms

All eight are free to walk into. This is the single most underused thing on this list.

### Mudchute Park and Farm, Isle of Dogs

*Free · 32 acres*

**Thirty-two acres of open farmland with Canary Wharf's towers visible over the fence** — genuinely disorienting, and the largest city farm in Europe.

### Kentish Town City Farm

*Free*

The farm that **started the city farm movement in Britain in 1972** — cows, pigs, a duck pond and a café.

### Hackney City Farm

*Free*

Pigs, goats, donkeys and chickens on a scrap of land off Hackney Road, with a good café attached.

### Spitalfields City Farm

*Free*

A working farm on reclaimed railway land **minutes from Brick Lane** — sheep, goats and a farm shop.

### Surrey Docks Farm, Rotherhithe

*Free*

Right on the Thames Path — pigs, goats and beehives with river views.

### Also free: **Stepney City Farm** (with a working pottery and a weekend market), **Freightliners Farm** in Islington, and **Vauxhall City Farm**.

## Museums built for children

### Young V&A, Bethnal Green

*Free*

The V&A's museum **built entirely around children**, reopened in 2023 after a redesign that put play first. Three galleries, a stage, and things to climb.

### The Postal Museum and Mail Rail, Bloomsbury

*Ticketed*

A **miniature train ride through the actual tunnels** of the Post Office's own underground railway.

### Natural History Museum and Science Museum, South Kensington

*Free*

The classic pair, next door to each other. The Science Museum is better with older children; the Natural History Museum's building does half the work on its own.

### London Transport Museum, Covent Garden

*Ticketed*

Real Tube carriages and horse buses **you can climb into**.

## Animals

### ZSL London Zoo, Regent's Park

*Ticketed*

Opened in **1828 as the world's first scientific zoo**, and the listed Victorian and modernist buildings are part of the appeal.

### Battersea Park Children's Zoo

*Ticketed · smaller and cheaper*

Meerkats, monkeys, otters and a barnyard, **scaled deliberately for under-tens** — small enough to finish without anyone melting down.

### SEA LIFE London Aquarium, South Bank

*Ticketed*

A walk-through shark tunnel and a rockhopper penguin colony in the basement of County Hall.

### WWT London Wetland Centre, Barnes

*About £14.50*

105 acres of wetland with kingfishers and otters, twenty minutes from Hammersmith.

## Rides and running about

### Babylon Park, Camden

*Free entry, pay per ride*

**Three underground storeys** including the only indoor rollercoaster in London, plus bumper cars, a carousel and VR. **Entry is free** — you only pay for rides, and paying per go is much cheaper than the wristband for a short visit.

![Neon-lit alien-themed rides and a flying saucer carousel inside Babylon Park in Camden](../../assets/articles/london-with-children/babylon-park-camden.jpg)

*Babylon Park, under Camden High Street. It is much bigger than the entrance suggests — the rides run down three floors.*

### Granary Square Fountains, King's Cross

*Free*

Over a thousand jets set into the paving. **The single best thing in London for small children on a hot day.** Bring a towel and a change of clothes.

### Go Ape, Battersea Park

*Ticketed · book ahead*

Zip wires and rope bridges through the trees — the only high-ropes course this close to central London. Minimum heights apply.

### Shrek's Adventure, South Bank

*Ticketed*

A guided walkthrough with live actors and a 4D bus ride, aimed at roughly ages 5–10.

> ⚠️ **The London Dungeon is in the same building** and is emphatically not the same thing — Merlin recommend it for 12+ and it genuinely frightens younger children.

## Free spectacle

* **Changing the Guard**, St James's — guards in bearskins and a full regimental band, free to watch. Check the schedule; it does not run daily.
* **Greenwich Foot Tunnel** — walk **under the Thames** through a tiled Victorian tunnel and come up on the Isle of Dogs. Free, and reliably a hit.
* **Word on the Water**, King's Cross — a bookshop on a barge, with a roof deck.
* **The Crystal Palace Dinosaurs** — the first dinosaur sculptures ever made, from 1854, and magnificently wrong.
* **Covent Garden street performers** — three licensed pitches, free, and the West Piazza takes the big circle acts.

---

## What to know

* **The city farms are free and almost never busy.** They are the best-kept secret on this page.
* **Check age guidance.** The London Dungeon is 12+; Shrek's Adventure is 5–10; several competitive-socialising venues are over-18 after 6pm.
* **Babylon Park charges per ride** if you want it to — much cheaper than a wristband for an hour's visit.
* **Under-11s travel free** on the Tube, DLR and buses with a fare-paying adult.
* **Museums are quietest in the last two hours** before closing.
