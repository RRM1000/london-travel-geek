Eurostar quotes fares to Brussels from £39 for a promotional-fare seat; £51 one-way in Standard is the steadier advance fare. Treat the range as what to expect, not a guarantee for your date.

### Eurostar Standard, Plus or Premier

The classes are **Eurostar Standard, Plus and Premier**, replacing the old Standard, Standard Premier and Business Premier.

| | Eurostar Standard | Eurostar Plus | Eurostar Premier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Exchange** | Free up to 1hr before | Free up to 1hr before | Free up to **48hrs after** departure |
| **Refund** | Up to 7 days before, £25 fee | Up to 7 days before, £25 fee | Free, up to 48hrs after departure |
| **Luggage** | 2 bags + 1 daypack | 2 bags + 1 daypack | **3 bags** + 1 daypack |
| **Seat** | Standard | Extra spacious, meal included | Extra spacious, hot meal, lounge |
| **Lounge** | — | — | St Pancras, Paris, Brussels |

For one day in Brussels, Standard does the job: it is refundable up to a week out for a £25 fee and free to change up to an hour before your train. Plus adds a meal; Premier adds the lounges and a flexible ticket.

Bookings open **10 to 11 months ahead**, and the price climbs as the train fills, so book early.

## Check-in and the border

UK exit, Belgian entry and EU biometric registration all happen at St Pancras before you leave, not on arrival in Brussels.

| Station | Class | Recommended arrival |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **St Pancras** (outbound) | Standard / Plus | **75 min before** |
| St Pancras (outbound) | Premier | 45 min before |
| **Brussels-Midi** (return) | Standard / Plus | **75 min before** |
| Brussels-Midi (return) | Premier | 45 min before |

> ⚠️ **Allow 75 minutes, not the 20 Eurostar recommends between Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam and Cologne.** Any route that starts or ends at St Pancras carries the longer figure.

**You need a passport, not an ID card.** Your passport must have been **issued less than 10 years before** the date you enter Belgium and stay **valid for at least 3 months after** the day you leave the Schengen area; a passport that fails either rule is turned away at the gate.

**The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES)** has applied to this route since **12 October 2025** and replaced the manual passport stamp in full on 10 April 2026. It means a passport scan, a photo and, on your first crossing, fingerprints, done at self-service kiosks in three zones of St Pancras before the ticket gates. It's free, needs no advance booking, and your digital record lasts three years.

**ETIAS isn't live yet.** The UK government's guidance says the EU expects to start it from autumn 2026, with the date still to be announced, so there is nothing to apply for today and any site charging for one is not official.

**Luggage:** one handbag or daypack plus two further items, none over 85cm at their widest point, for Standard and Plus — three bags plus the daypack for Premier.

## Is Brussels actually doable as a day trip?

Yes, and more comfortably than Paris, because the train is shorter.

The first Brussels train leaves St Pancras at **06:16** and reaches Brussels-Midi at **08:13**, but that means checking in by around 5am. The more realistic early option is the **08:16**, arriving **10:13**. The last service back leaves Brussels around **19:56–20:56**, arriving at St Pancras roughly two hours later.

The 08:16 out and the last train back give you from **10:13 to around 18:40–19:40** in Brussels before the 75-minute check-in at Brussels-Midi: **eight and a half to nine and a half hours**. The 06:16 stretches the day past **eleven hours**. Paris gives roughly seven on the equivalent pattern.

## Getting into Brussels: from Midi to the centre

Brussels-Midi's Metro and premetro (underground tram) station sits directly beneath the platforms, on lines 2, 4, 6 and 10. A **premetro ride of about 10 minutes** takes you to De Brouckère, by the Bourse and a short walk from the Grand-Place. Walking the whole way takes about 20 minutes.

## What to do with the day

### The Grand-Place and Manneken Pis

The Grand-Place is where nearly every route starts: a market square ringed by guildhalls, with the Town Hall's 96-metre spire, topped with a gilded statue of St Michael, rising over it. Most of what you see was rebuilt within four years of a French bombardment in 1695, and UNESCO listed the square as a World Heritage Site in 1998.

**Manneken Pis** is a five-minute walk south-west of the square: a bronze fountain figure of a small boy, **55.5cm tall**. The statue on the street is a 1965 replica of the 1619 original, which is in the Brussels City Museum on the Grand-Place. The figure is dressed several times a week from a wardrobe of roughly a thousand costumes, on a schedule posted at the railings; retired costumes are in the GardeRobe MannekenPis museum close by.

### The comic-strip walls

Brussels has painted comic-book murals on blank gable walls since 1991, a joint project between the city and the Belgian Comic Strip Center. There are now more than 50, most inside the Pentagon (the ring road that marks the old city walls), so a walk between two or three sights will usually cross one. Look for Tintin's rocket, Gaston Lagaffe on the Rue de l'Écuyer, and the Smurfs.

The indoor **Comic Art Museum** (also known as the Belgian Comic Strip Center) is worth the admission if you want the history behind the murals. It is in a former textile warehouse designed by Victor Horta in 1906. Adult admission is €14.

> ⚠️ **The Comic Art Museum is closed on Mondays, except in July and August.** The murals are outdoors and always viewable.

![Inside the Belgian Comic Strip Center, a wrought-iron staircase and glass roof under Victor Horta's 1906 Art Nouveau ironwork, with exhibition panels below](../../assets/articles/brussels-day-trip/comic-strip-center-interior.jpg)

### The Atomium question

The **Atomium**, nine steel spheres 102 metres tall built for the 1958 World's Fair, is a detour, not a stop on the way to anything. It sits at Heysel, north of the centre, a **direct 15-minute Metro line 6 ride from Brussels-Midi**, no change. Five spheres are open to visitors; the top-sphere panorama is temporarily closed. Adult admission is **€17**, which also covers the Design Museum Brussels 150 metres away. It is open every day, 10am to 6pm (last entry 5:30pm), and closed from 18 January to 5 February 2027. Tickets are sold at the ticket office only, with waits of 45 minutes to two hours.

![The Atomium's nine steel spheres and connecting tubes against a blue sky, seen from the avenue leading up to it](../../assets/articles/brussels-day-trip/atomium.jpg)

*The Atomium, at Heysel.*

If you are on one of the early trains and can give it 90 minutes to two hours there and back, plus the queue, it is an easy add-on. On the later 08:16, with the centre still to see, drop it: the Grand-Place, Manneken Pis and the comic walls sit close together, and the Atomium doesn't.

### Waffles, frites and beer

**Waffles** come in two shapes: a Brussels waffle is light, crisp and rectangular, made from a yeasted batter and usually dusted with icing sugar or piled with toppings; a Liège waffle is smaller, denser and oval, made from a brioche-like dough studded with pearl sugar that caramelises as it cooks. Both are sold from stands across the centre.

**Frites** come in a paper cone with mayonnaise rather than ketchup as the default, fried twice, from a **frietkot** (a fries stand, sometimes just a hatch in a wall) rather than a restaurant.

**Beer:** the Delirium Café, in the Impasse de la Fidélité alley just off the Bourse, held the Guinness World Record for the longest beer list, **2,004 different beers**, when it opened in December 2003.

### Chocolate shops

**Neuhaus**, in the Galeries Royales Saint-Hubert a few minutes from the Grand-Place, is where the filled praline began: Jean Neuhaus II created it in 1912, and his wife Louise Agostini invented the ballotin, the small box that stops pralines crushing each other. Other names worth the walk: **Mary**, official supplier to the Belgian royal court since the 1930s, and **Pierre Marcolini**, the more experimental end of the trade.

## Onward to Bruges and Ghent

## Where to stay

Stay near Brussels-Midi only if you need the five-minute walk to a morning train; the streets around the station are known for pickpocketing and feel rough after dark. Otherwise stay in the old centre, a short premetro ride from Midi.

- **[Jill Hotel Brussels](hotelscom:h6715906)** — Avenue Fonsny, a two-minute walk from Gare du Midi's platforms and premetro; a 4-star boutique fit-out behind a plain façade, with a garden terrace. **££**, worth booking for the early train, not an evening in the area.
- **[Hotel Amigo](hotelscom:h808295)** — Rue de l'Amigo, between the Grand-Place and Manneken Pis; a five-star with its own Italian restaurant and live jazz at Bar Magritte. **£££**.
- **[Hotel Le Plaza Brussels](hotelscom:h54732)** — Boulevard Adolphe Max, a ten-minute walk from the Grand-Place past the Rue Neuve shops; a traditional, formal hotel. **££**, quieter than staying on the square.
- **[ibis Brussels off Grand Place](hotelscom:h16260)** — Rue du Marché aux Herbes, two minutes from the Grand-Place; a modern budget-chain room with a breakfast buffet that includes Belgian waffles. **£**, the cheapest of the four.

## What people get wrong

- **Treating check-in like a UK domestic train.** Eurostar wants 75 minutes at St Pancras for Standard and Plus.
- **Bringing an ID card instead of a passport.** UK nationals need a passport.
- **Fitting in the Atomium on a short afternoon.** The Metro alone is a 30-minute round trip, before the queue.
- **Turning up at the Comic Art Museum on a Monday.** The outdoor murals are always there; the museum isn't.
- **Bolting Bruges or Ghent onto the same day.** Each needs its own hour or so of travel from Brussels-Midi.
