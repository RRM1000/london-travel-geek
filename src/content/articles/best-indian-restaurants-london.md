London does not have one Indian restaurant scene — it has a dozen running in parallel. A two-Michelin-starred club room in Mayfair and a Peshawari grill in Tooting are not two versions of the same meal; they are different cuisines that a British menu convention files under one word.

So this guide is arranged by **what each kitchen actually cooks**: Awadhi, Keralan, Bengali, Gujarati, Kenyan-Punjabi, Himalayan, Peshawari. Once you know you want appam and coconut rather than a tandoor, half the famous names stop mattering.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Trishna** is the single most-recommended Indian restaurant in London and the safest booking on this page. Go to **Gymkhana** for a milestone and book months out; **Veeraswamy** for a hundred years of Regent Street; **Dishoom** or **Kricket** for a good central meal without a plan; **Tayyabs** for the best £25 you will spend on food in this city; and **Rasa** for South Indian vegetarian cooking that shames most of the Mayfair vegetable courses.

> 📘 **How we choose these (editorial note)**
> No paid placements. These are drawn from a wide spread of sources rather than any single list - the food and travel press, the big listings sites, independent blogs and specialists, video, and what Londoners recommend themselves - and cross-referenced against each other. Prices and booking rules change; check before you travel.

## What every guide agrees on

These seven turn up on four or more of the independent restaurant guides — the strongest agreement in the whole set. If you only want one Indian meal in London and do not want to think about it, take one of these.

### Trishna, Marylebone

*£££ · Marylebone · 8 min from Bond Street · book weeks ahead · 6 sources - the strongest Indian consensus; Michelin star*

More published guides name Trishna than any other Indian restaurant in London, and the agreement is not really about the star — it is about how little the room asks of you. The cooking is south-west coastal, built on seafood and sour, bright spicing rather than tandoor smoke, and the hariyali bream is the dish people come back for.

If Gymkhana feels too formal, or is simply gone for the next three months, this is the substitution to make. It is quieter, half the price, and no less serious.

**1 minute from Jikoni.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/explore/trishna/reservations/create/search?venues=ambassadorsclubhouse%2Ctrishna)

### Gymkhana, Mayfair

*££££ · Mayfair · 3 min from Green Park · book months ahead · 4 sources; two Michelin stars*

Two Michelin stars, and the only Indian kitchen in Britain holding that many. The room takes its cues from the colonial-era gymkhana clubs — ceiling fans, dark wood, a bar that behaves like a members' club — and the kitchen leans hard into game and grilling. The wild boar biryani is the order.

Book as far ahead as the window allows, which in practice means months rather than weeks. This is not a meal you slot in before a show; it is the evening.

▶ **In the videos:** [TOPJAW visits from 14:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AHUnqeRroYk&t=855s).

**4 minutes from Benares.**

### Veeraswamy, Piccadilly

*££££ · Piccadilly · 4 min from Piccadilly Circus · book weeks ahead · 4 sources; est. 1926, Michelin star*

Open since 1926, which makes it the oldest surviving Indian restaurant in Britain and puts its centenary in 2026. It would be easy for that to be the whole story, but Veeraswamy holds a Michelin star as well, and the dum biryani is cooked with the seriousness of a kitchen that is not coasting on its own history.

> ⚠️ **Check the rules before you book.** Veeraswamy runs a smart-casual dress code, age guidance for evening service, and a minimum spend at dinner outside the [pre-theatre menu](https://www.veeraswamy.com/whats-on/pre-theatre-and-post-theatre-dining/). None of that is unusual at this level, but all of it is easier to know in advance.

**3 minutes from Kricket.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.veeraswamy.com/reservations/)

### BiBi, Mayfair

*££££ · Mayfair · 6 min from Bond Street · book weeks ahead · 5 sources; Michelin star*

Chet Sharma's room, and the one on this page that most rewards paying attention. The menu works through regional Indian dishes with fine-dining technique and British produce, and the drinks list was built alongside the food rather than bolted on afterwards.

It is small and the seating is scheduled, so allergies, deposits and timings are worth settling when you book rather than on the night.

▶ **In the videos:** [TOPJAW visits from 7:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AHUnqeRroYk&t=440s).

**7 minutes from Jamavar.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/bibi)

### Dishoom, Covent Garden

*££ · Covent Garden · 3 min from Covent Garden · 4 sources; also a 3-source pick on the breakfast pass*

The Irani cafés of old Bombay, rebuilt in enough detail that the reproduction is half the pleasure. It is the most accessible thing on this list — no formality, branches next to most of the places you were going anyway — and the bacon naan roll turned it into a breakfast destination as much as a dinner one.

> 💡 **Getting a table**
> * **6pm is the cut-off, not the party size.** Any number of people can book before 6pm. After 6pm, bookings open only for groups of six or more — which is where the widely repeated "Dishoom doesn't take reservations" comes from, and why a table for two books perfectly well at lunch.
> * **Most tables are held for walk-ins** at every hour, so a booking page showing nothing does not mean a full restaurant.
> * **Breakfast and lunch barely queue.** After 6pm and at weekends, expect thirty minutes to an hour, with chai brought to you while you wait.

**4 minutes from Punjab.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/explore/dishoom/reservations/create/search?venues=dishoombattersea%2Cdishoomcarnaby%2Cdishoomcanarywharf%2Cdishoomcoventgarden%2Cdishoomkensington%2Cdishoomkingscross%2Cdishoomshoreditch%2Cdishoombirmingham%2Cdishoomedinburgh%2Cdishoomglasgow%2Cdishoomleeds%2Cdishoommanche)

### Brigadiers, City of London

*£££ · City of London · 1 min from Cannon Street · 5 sources; JKS Restaurants*

Built on the idea of an Indian army mess bar: several large rooms, barbecue over coals, a serious whisky list, and live sport on the screens. It is loud, and that is a design decision rather than an accident — nobody at Brigadiers is having a quiet conversation.

Which makes it the obvious answer for a group of six or more, and the wrong answer for almost everything else. The tandoori lamb chops are what to order first.

▶ **In the videos:** [TOPJAW opens here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AHUnqeRroYk&t=0s), and so does [Harrison Webb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6mDJrJFvllo&t=0s).

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/explore/brigadiers/reservations/create/search?venues=brigadiers%2Cambassadorsclubhouse%2Ctrishna)

### Jamavar, Mayfair

*££££ · Mayfair · 8 min from Bond Street · book weeks ahead · 4 sources; Michelin star*

Named after the handwoven Kashmiri shawl, and the menu takes the same approach — cooking across the whole subcontinent rather than committing to one region of it. That breadth is unusual at this level, where the fashion is to specialise, and it makes Jamavar the easiest starred room here to bring a table of people who want different things.

**4 minutes from Benares.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.opentable.co.uk/r/jamavar-reservations-london?restref=110892&lang=en-GB&ot_source=Restaurant%20website)

## Fine dining the listicles under-count

Five more rooms at the top end. Two of them were found through the awards rather than the guides, which is the whole argument for reading both.

### The Cinnamon Club, Westminster

*££££ · Westminster · 7 min from St. James's Park · 3 sources*

The old Westminster Library, Grade II listed, with the bookshelves still in place and the gallery still running round the room. It is the most striking dining room of any Indian restaurant in London by a wide margin, and it sits close enough to Parliament that the lunch trade tells you something about the day's politics.

**9 minutes from Quilon.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://cinnamonclub.com/)

### Benares, Mayfair

*££££ · Mayfair · 4 min from Green Park · book weeks ahead · Asian Curry Awards - Best Fine Dining, London*

Named Best Fine Dining Restaurant in London at the Asian Curry Awards, and a Berkeley Square fixture for two decades — which between them make a stronger case than its near-absence from the published guides would suggest. This is exactly the kind of restaurant that the listicle circuit stops mentioning once it is no longer new.

**4 minutes from Gymkhana.**

### Tamarind, Mayfair

*££££ · Mayfair · 5 min from Green Park · book weeks ahead · 2 sources; Michelin star*

One of the first two Indian restaurants anywhere in the world to win a Michelin star — it and Zaika were awarded jointly in 2001 — and it holds one today. It is still a Mayfair basement doing tandoor cooking, and it has never tried to become anything else — no reinvention, no small plates, no counter.

**6 minutes from Benares.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](http://www.tamarindrestaurant.com/)

### Oudh 1722, Borough

*££££ · Borough · 5 min from Borough · book weeks ahead*

Aktar Islam — who holds two Michelin stars elsewhere — cooking Awadhi food, the courtly tradition of Lucknow, across three floors of a Victorian townhouse near London Bridge. Awadhi means biryani and kebabs treated as the centre of a menu rather than as line items on one.

It is the newest arrival on this page and the one least represented in the published guides, simply because it opened after most of them were written.

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/oudh1722)

### Amaya, Knightsbridge

*££££ · Knightsbridge · 6 min from Knightsbridge · 2 sources; Michelin star*

An open kitchen of tawa, sigri and tandoor — griddle, charcoal grill and clay oven, all three working in front of you — and a Michelin star awarded essentially for grilling rather than for saucing. Dishes arrive as they come off the fire rather than in courses, which suits two people and frustrates six.

**14 minutes from Kutir.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](http://www.amaya.biz/reservations/)

## Regions almost nowhere else in London cooks

This is the section that justifies the whole page. Each of these is the only serious representative of its regional tradition in the city, or close to it.

### Kanishka, Mayfair

*£££ · Mayfair · 4 min from Oxford Circus · 2 sources*

Atul Kochhar cooking the north-eastern states — Assam, Nagaland, Sikkim — which almost nothing else in London attempts at any price. The flavours run smokier, more fermented and less obviously "curry" than anything else in Mayfair, and that is the point of going.

**7 minutes from Benares.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/explore/kanishka/reservations/create/search/)

### Quilon, Westminster

*££££ · Westminster · 5 min from St. James's Park · book weeks ahead · 2 sources; Michelin star*

Kerala and the Malabar coast: appam, coconut, curry leaf, and seafood treated as the main event. Quilon has held its star since 2008 and remains the one starred kitchen in London working the south-west rather than the north. Order the Mangalorean chicken.

Practically, it sits inside the St James' Court hotel and is a short walk from Buckingham Palace and Westminster Abbey, which makes it an unusually easy dinner to attach to a sightseeing day.

**9 minutes from The Cinnamon Club.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/thequilon)

### Rasa, Stoke Newington

*£ · Stoke Newington · book days ahead*

The same coast as Quilon for a fifth of the price. An entirely vegetarian Keralan kitchen behind a shocking pink shopfront on Stoke Newington Church Street, built on coconut, curry leaf and mustard seed rather than cream and tomato — which is what south Indian vegetarian food actually tastes like and what most of London's vegetarian curry does not.

Cheaper than almost anything in this guide and better than most of it.

### Kokum, East Dulwich

*£££ · East Dulwich · 13 min from East Dulwich · 3 sources*

Named for the sour, dark coastal fruit used along India's western shore, and the menu follows it — Goan and Konkani seafood, tamarind and coconut, very little cream. Three independent guides name it, which is a strong showing for a neighbourhood restaurant thirteen minutes' walk from a suburban station.

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://order.kokumlondon.com/reservations)

## Modern rooms

Kitchens doing something deliberately unlike a curry house — small plates, counters, and one restaurant that is a period piece.

### Kricket, Soho

*££ · Soho · 1 min from Piccadilly Circus · 2 sources*

Started life in a shipping container in Brixton and grew into several sites without losing the format: Indian cooking served as small plates rather than as curries, designed to be ordered across the table. The Keralan fried chicken is the one to start with.

One minute from Piccadilly Circus makes it the most practical pre-theatre booking on this page.

▶ **In the videos:** [Harrison Webb visits from 7:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6mDJrJFvllo&t=476s).

**3 minutes from Darjeeling Express.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/kricketbrixton)

### Empire Empire, Notting Hill

*£££ · Notting Hill · 8 min from Westbourne Park · 2 sources*

A love letter to the Bombay dining rooms of the 1970s, from the group behind Gymkhana and Trishna — velvet, brass, mirrored panels and a disco-era swagger that is played entirely straight. The food is north Indian and unfussy; the room is the reason it gets written about.

▶ **In the videos:** [Harrison Webb visits from 18:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6mDJrJFvllo&t=1095s).

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/empireempirelondon)

### Darjeeling Express, Covent Garden

*£££ · Covent Garden · 3 min from Piccadilly Circus · book weeks ahead · 3 sources*

Asma Khan's restaurant, staffed entirely by women, cooking the food of Calcutta alongside the royal Mughlai dishes she grew up eating. The kitchen is the story here and Khan has never pretended otherwise — the recipes are family ones and the cooks were largely trained at home rather than in professional kitchens.

Tables go weeks out. Go with a group and share.

▶ **In the videos:** [TOPJAW visits from 11:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AHUnqeRroYk&t=677s).

**3 minutes from Kricket.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.darjeeling-express.com/book-a-table)

### Kutir, Chelsea

*£££ · Chelsea · 6 min from Sloane Square · 3 sources*

A Chelsea townhouse fitted out as an Indian hunting lodge, with a menu built around game and the outdoors to match. Three guides name it, and it is the most comfortable room in this section — small, warm, and much closer to a private house than a restaurant.

**14 minutes from Amaya.**

### Jikoni, Marylebone

*£££ · Marylebone · 8 min from Bond Street · book weeks ahead*

Ravinder Bhogal cooks across her Kenyan, Indian and British background without picking one of them, which is why Jikoni is hard to file and easy to love. Ottolenghi has said it is the restaurant he wishes he had opened, and the comparison is fair: this is food built on borrowing rather than on authenticity.

**1 minute from Trishna.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/jikoni)

## Grills, curry houses and the East End

> 🌶️ **A note on what "Indian" means here.** Tayyabs is a Pakistani restaurant, and we have said so rather than quietly filing it under Indian the way most London guides do. Punjabi Lahori grilling is its own tradition — and it is also, unambiguously, where you go for the best-value serious meal in this city. Leaving it out of a guide to eating curry in London to keep a label tidy would be the wrong call.

### Tayyabs, Whitechapel

*££ · Whitechapel · 7 min from Whitechapel · walk-in only · est. 1972; video pass*

The sizzling lamb chops arrive still spitting on the plate and they are the reason the queue forms outside before service. Trading since 1972, and nothing about the place has been softened for anybody — it is loud, it is packed, and you will not linger over coffee.

It is also BYOB, which is where the real saving is: a full meal with your own wine comes in around the price of two courses almost anywhere else on this page.

▶ **In the videos:** [TOPJAW visits from 3:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AHUnqeRroYk&t=228s).

**10 minutes from City Spice.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://booking.resdiary.com/widget/Standard/Tayyabs/13658)

### City Spice, Brick Lane

*££ · Brick Lane · 11 min from Aldgate East · video pass; Brick Lane*

Brick Lane's reputation has been coasting for twenty years and most of the street knows it. City Spice is the room that still gets named when people argue about which of the curry houses is worth eating at, and it is the one to pick if you want the street's history with dinner attached.

Be clear-eyed about the trade, though: Tayyabs is a ten-minute walk away and it is the better meal.

**10 minutes from Tayyabs.**

**Book:** [Reserve a table](https://cityspice.co/book-now/)

### Punjab, Covent Garden

*££ · Covent Garden · 2 min from Covent Garden · est. 1946; landmark*

Open since 1946, claimed as the oldest North Indian restaurant in Britain, and still run by the same family four generations in. It predates Britain's entire curry-house boom, which makes the menu a genuine historical document as much as a dinner.

**4 minutes from Dishoom.**

## Cheap, fast and excellent

Everything here comes in under £20 a head and none of it takes a booking.

### Shree Krishna Vada Pav, Fitzrovia

*£ · Fitzrovia · 4 min from Oxford Circus · walk-in only · 3 sources*

Vada pav — a spiced potato fritter in a soft bun with chutneys, Mumbai's answer to the burger — done properly, from a counter rather than a dining room. Three independent guides name a place where nothing costs more than a sandwich, which tells you how good it is.

**7 minutes from Kanishka.**

### Horn OK Please, Borough

*£ · Borough · 4 min from London Bridge · walk-in only*

An all-vegetarian stall in Borough Market whose samosa chaat is built in front of you: the samosas crushed under hot chickpea curry, then yoghurt, then tamarind, eaten standing up. Order it and eat it immediately — it does not survive being carried anywhere.

### Gujarati Rasoi, Borough

*£ · Borough · 4 min from London Bridge · walk-in only*

Mother-and-son Gujarati cooking from family recipes, a few steps from Horn OK Please and a completely different tradition — thalis, bhajia and dhal, gently sweet where the other stall is sharp. Two Indian regional cuisines within thirty seconds of each other, at a market where nearly everything else is European.

### Kolkati, Covent Garden

*£ · Covent Garden · 3 min from Covent Garden · walk-in only*

Kati rolls the Kolkata way, inside Seven Dials Market: paratha cooked with egg through it, wrapped around spiced chicken or paneer with pickled onion, coriander chutney and lime. The best thing you can eat in Covent Garden for a fiver.

## Booking and dietary essentials

* **Book months ahead** for Gymkhana. **Weeks ahead** for BiBi, Veeraswamy, Jamavar, Benares, Tamarind, Quilon, Oudh 1722, Darjeeling Express and Jikoni.
* **You cannot book at all** at Tayyabs, Shree Krishna Vada Pav or any of the market stalls.
* **Dishoom takes any party size before 6pm**, and groups of six or more after it. It holds most tables for walk-ins regardless.
* **Fully vegetarian:** Rasa, Shree Krishna Vada Pav and Horn OK Please. Everywhere else here has a substantial marked vegetarian menu.
* **Halal** preparation varies by branch and by dish even within one restaurant. Confirm directly with the venue rather than assuming from the cuisine.
* **Bring your own alcohol** at Tayyabs. It has no licence and charges little corkage, which is a large part of why it is such good value.
* **Service charge** of 12.5% is discretionary and standard on London restaurant bills.
