Hampton Court Palace is two palaces at one address on the Thames in East Molesey: Henry VIII's redbrick Tudor courts on one side, Christopher Wren's Baroque state apartments for William III and Mary II on the other. Historic Royal Palaces, the charity that also runs the Tower of London, prices tickets by season, not a flat rate.

Allow longer than you think. The Tudor Kitchens cover half an acre, the gardens run to 60 acres, and the hedge maze has been catching visitors out since the 1690s. As a two-hour add-on to a day in London you will rush past most of it.

## Getting there

### By train

**Hampton Court station** is a five-minute walk from the palace gates. South Western Railway runs **two direct trains an hour from London Waterloo**, a **35-minute journey** with no changes. The station is in **Zone 6**, so contactless and Oyster pay-as-you-go work as anywhere else in London: a single off-peak fare is around £3.50, peak around £5.70, and the Zone 1–6 daily cap is £11.80. There are no barriers at Hampton Court, so you must **touch in at Waterloo and touch out here** (and the reverse on the way back), or you'll be charged the maximum fare. Return trains leave Hampton Court at 24 and 54 minutes past the hour Monday to Saturday, and 05 and 35 past on Sundays.

### By river (summer)

In summer, a **riverboat from Westminster or Richmond** is the slower, more scenic way in. Westminster Passenger Services sails from **Westminster Pier**, calling at Kew and Richmond, with the full run to Hampton Court taking around **3.5–4 hours** depending on the tide. Turks Launches covers the **Richmond–Hampton Court** stretch in about **1 hour 45 minutes**, and the **Hampton Court–Kingston** leg in around 35 minutes. ### By bus and car

Bus **R68** runs directly between Richmond and Hampton Court. From Kingston, routes **111, 216, 411, 461 and 513** all stop at the palace. Drivers pay **£1.80 an hour** by card in the on-site car park (10 Blue Badge bays); Hampton Court Green, 500 metres away, charges the same, and the Walton Road car park is a 20-minute walk.

## What to see

Historic Royal Palaces' suggested routes run to two hours, three hours or a full day, adding rooms in roughly the order below.

![The red-brick Tudor West Front and Great Gatehouse of Hampton Court Palace with its twisted chimneys under a clear blue sky](../../assets/articles/hampton-court-palace-guide/hampton-court-west-front.jpg)

*The West Front and Great Gatehouse.*

### Henry VIII's Apartments and the Great Hall

Enter through Clock Court, beneath the **Hampton Court astronomical clock**, installed in 1540 and still showing the hour, the zodiac and the time of high water at London Bridge. **Henry VIII's Apartments** lead into the **Great Hall**, built between 1532 and 1535, with a carved hammerbeam roof, Henry's tapestries and stained glass.

![Looking up at the carved wooden hammerbeam roof and stained-glass windows of the Great Hall at Hampton Court Palace, with tapestries and antlers on the walls below](../../assets/articles/hampton-court-palace-guide/hampton-court-great-hall-roof.jpg)

*The Great Hall's hammerbeam roof.*

Beyond the Great Hall, the **Haunted Gallery** and the **Wolsey Rooms** hold the Tudor World exhibition, tracing the palace back to Cardinal Wolsey, who built it before Henry VIII took it from him.

### The Tudor Kitchens

Built before 1508 by Sir Giles Daubeney and **quadrupled in size by Henry VIII in 1529**, the Tudor Kitchens fed a royal household of more than a thousand people a day. Copper pots still sit on the brick ranges, and on busier days costumed cooks demonstrate Tudor cooking over an open fire.

![Rows of copper cooking pots set on the brick ranges inside the Tudor Kitchens at Hampton Court Palace](../../assets/articles/hampton-court-palace-guide/hampton-court-tudor-kitchens.jpg)

*The Tudor Kitchens.*

### William III's Apartments and the Chapel Royal

Cross into the Baroque half of the palace for **William III's Apartments**, built by Christopher Wren for William III and Mary II in the 1690s, and the **Chapel Royal**, still in regular use for services.

![Christopher Wren's red-brick and stone range at Hampton Court Palace, seen across a formal garden of clipped yew cones and a gravel path](../../assets/articles/hampton-court-palace-guide/hampton-court-wren-front.jpg)

The **Georgian Story** and **Queen's Apartments** round off a three-hour visit, and a full day adds the **Chocolate Kitchens**, a surviving Georgian royal kitchen built solely for preparing chocolate, plus the short **Palace Host Talks**.

### The maze and the gardens

North of the palace, the **hedge maze** is the oldest surviving example in Britain: about a third of an acre, planted in the 1690s for William III and designed by George London and Henry Wise. It's included in standard admission.

![Visitors walking between tall clipped hedges inside the Hampton Court Palace maze](../../assets/articles/hampton-court-palace-guide/hampton-court-maze.jpg)

*Inside the hedge maze.*

The wider gardens run to 60 acres, Grade I listed on the Register of Historic Parks and Gardens. The **Privy Garden** is a 1992 recreation of the 1702 garden laid out for William III; the **Long Water** canal was excavated in 1662 for Charles II. The **Magic Garden**, a children's play area themed around palace legends, and the **Kitchen Garden**, which supplies the Tudor Kitchens' demonstrations, are included in standard admission too.

### The Great Vine

In a glasshouse near the Thames-facing side of the palace stands the **Great Vine**, planted in **1768** by Lancelot "Capability" Brown from a cutting. It's a Black Hamburg dessert grape, with a trunk over two metres around and a canopy stretching some 30 metres. **The Great Vine and the Sunken Garden are closed 26 October–23 November 2026.**

![The white-framed glasshouse built around the Great Vine at Hampton Court Palace, seen from the garden path](../../assets/articles/hampton-court-palace-guide/hampton-court-great-vine.jpg)

*The Great Vine's glasshouse.*

The vine is harvested every **September**, and the grapes (an average crop of around 270kg) are sold in the palace's Garden Shop and Barrack Block shop while stocks last.

## Christmas at Hampton Court

**The Winter Palace**, an immersive Christmas walkthrough themed around mythical creatures, runs **28 November 2026 – 3 January 2027** and is included in admission at the Winter Palace prices above. Historic Royal Palaces members get in free with pre-booking. On 9–11, 16–17 and 19–30 December the palace stays open until 7.30pm for it.

> ⚠️ **The ice rink is a separate ticket from palace admission.** It runs on the East Front lawn from **20 November 2026 to 3 January 2027** (closed Christmas Day), 10am to 8pm daily in 45-minute sessions. Adult tickets are **£19.50 off-peak / £21.50 peak**, child (3–12) £14.50/£16.00, and a family ticket for four is £59.50/£65.50. Book through the [rink's own site](https://hamptoncourtpalaceicerink.co.uk/); a palace ticket does not include skating, and a rink ticket does not include the palace.

## Food and Garden Festivals

Hampton Court hosts two big annual events on the grounds, run by outside organisers.

The **Hampton Court Palace Food Festival** usually runs over the August bank holiday weekend; festival tickets include free entry to the palace and gardens on the day.

The **RHS Hampton Court Palace Garden Festival** last ran at the palace **1–6 July 2025**. There is no Hampton Court edition in 2026.

## Combining with Richmond or Kew

The **R68 bus** runs directly between **Richmond** and Hampton Court, and in summer **Turks Launches'** river cruise covers the same stretch in about 1 hour 45 minutes. Westminster Passenger Services' longer summer sailing also calls at **Kew Pier** and Richmond on its way to Hampton Court, so one ticket can cover all three in a single direction if you give most of a day to the river.

Hampton Court and Kew Gardens sit on different rail lines with no direct connection, so treat them as separate trips from central London. Our [Richmond area guide](/articles/richmond-area-guide/) and [Kew Gardens guide](/articles/kew-gardens-guide/) cover both in full.

## Practical information

### Food and drink

The palace has cafés inside the gate, and picnics are allowed on the lawns in Home Park, outside the ticketed gardens.

### What people get wrong

1. **Assuming the maze and gardens cost extra.** They're part of standard admission.
2. **Turning up on a Monday or Tuesday in term time.** The palace is closed those two days outside school holidays; check the calendar before you travel.
3. **Missing the last off-peak train back.** Hampton Court has no barriers, so forgetting to touch out means the maximum fare, not a fine.
4. **Booking the ice rink and the palace as if they're the same ticket.** They aren't; you need both if you want to do both.
