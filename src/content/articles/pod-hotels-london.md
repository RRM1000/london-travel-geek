A capsule berth at Piccadilly Circus is **£33 a night**. A windowless private room across the road, under the same brand, is **£97**. Both are sold as pods, and Zedwell calls both of them *Cocoon*.

That is the whole problem with this category in one sentence, and it is why most guides to it are wrong. They quote the capsule price and describe the room.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **£33** buys a capsule in a shared dormitory. **£97** buys a windowless private room with an en-suite. The word that separates them is **"Capsule" in the property name** — not "pod", and not "cocoon", because Zedwell uses "cocoon" for both. The rooms only become cheap when you fill them: a **Cocoon 4 at Greenwich is £31 a head**, and that beats a capsule.

*Every rate below was read off the room cards on each property's own Hotels.com page for a midweek night, 4–5 October 2026, one adult, on **7 September 2026**. Room dimensions and bed counts come from the same listings and from Zedwell's own room pages.*

## Two products, one word

### The hotel cocoon: a windowless private room

Zedwell's five hotels — Piccadilly Circus, Tottenham Court Road, Greenwich, Knightsbridge and Park Lane — sell **private rooms with no window**. The smallest is 7 sq m. You get your own door, an **en-suite with a walk-in rainfall shower**, soundproofed walls and doors, filtered and temperature-controlled air, a Hypnos mattress and Egyptian cotton sheets.

![A Zedwell cocoon room: a bed on a lit wooden plinth, a full-length mirror, hooks and a private door](../../assets/articles/pod-hotels-london/zedwell-cocoon-room.jpg)

*A cocoon room. Small, no window, and a door of its own.*

This is a small hotel room that happens to be interior. It is not a bunk, it is not shared, and **it is not, for one person, especially cheap**.

### The capsule cocoon: a berth in a dormitory

Zedwell runs **two separate Capsule properties** — Piccadilly Circus Capsule and Leicester Place — and these are a different product in a different building. Their own description is unusually straight about it: *"your own private space within a shared dormitory"*.

A capsule is roughly **four feet high and four feet wide**, closed with a roller shutter rather than a door. Same mattress, same sheets, same windowless philosophy. **Bathrooms are shared** — private shower stalls with their own changing areas, on communal floors. You bring your own padlock, lockers are bought separately, and there is a **women-only floor at £40 against £33** for the mixed one.

![Inside a sleeping capsule, a mattress and pillow in a wood-lined berth with hooks and a shelf, too low to stand in](../../assets/articles/pod-hotels-london/zedwell-capsule-interior.jpg)

*Inside a capsule. Hooks, a shelf, a shutter, and no room to stand up.*

**Otherwander Soho** and **The GreenHouse Capsules** are this format too.

## Why the mistake is so easy to make

Zedwell sells both products **under the same brand, at Piccadilly Circus, minutes apart**. Two listings, near-identical names, and the room type on the cheap one is called "Capsule Cocoon" while the room types on the expensive one are called "Cocoon 1" and "Cocoon 2".

![A corridor of capsules with signage pointing to Cocoon 5.163-5.164 in one direction and Toilets and Showers in the other](../../assets/articles/pod-hotels-london/zedwell-capsule-corridor.jpg)

*The capsule floors sign the way to the toilets and showers, because they are down the corridor rather than in your berth. The rooms do not need that sign.*

**The only reliable signal is the word "Capsule" in the property name.** Zedwell uses it consistently. Nothing else on the booking page does the job.

## What the Cocoon numbers mean

The number is how many people the room sleeps. This is the part almost nobody explains, and it changes the maths completely.

Zedwell Piccadilly Circus, the largest of them, on the night we checked:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cocoon 1** | 1 | 7 sq m | 1 single | £97 | £97 |
| **Cocoon 2** | 2 | 10 sq m | 1 double | £102 | £51 |
| **Cocoon 3** | 3 | 12 sq m | 1 double, 1 single | £132 | £44 |
| **Cocoon 4** | 4 | 14 sq m | 2 doubles, bunked | £163 | £41 |
| **Cocoon 6** | 6 | 18 sq m | 3 doubles, bunked | £203 | £34 |
| **Cocoon 8** | 8 | 20 sq m | 4 doubles, bunked | £244 | £30.50 |
| **Cocoon 12** | 12 | 62 sq m | 6 doubles | £356 | £30 |

There is also a **Family Room** (4 people, 14 sq m, £173), a **Cocoon 10** and a **Premium Family Cocoon** — both sold out on our date.

**A Capsule Cocoon at the same postcode is £33.** So the per-person price of a private room crosses under a dormitory berth somewhere around **Cocoon 6**, and by Cocoon 8 the room is comfortably cheaper.

### The catch, which is a real one

**From Cocoon 4 upwards, these rooms are built from double beds.** A Cocoon 4 is two doubles in a bunk, not four berths. A Cocoon 6 is three doubles. A Cocoon 8 is four.

So the £30-a-head figure assumes **you are sharing a bed with someone**. Two couples in a Cocoon 4 at £41 each is a genuinely good deal in Zone 1. Four friends who each want their own bed are better off with four capsules at £33 — cheaper in total, and everyone gets a shutter to close.

The second thing the table shows is space. **A Cocoon 8 is 20 sq m for eight people — two and a half square metres each**, against seven for a solo Cocoon 1. The price falls because the room does not grow. Cocoon 12 is the exception and a different sort of room: 62 sq m, with a sitting area.

**Knightsbridge sells one room type only** — a Cocoon 2 at £89 — so there is no solo rate to drop to and no family option at all. As a couple it is the cheapest bed in SW1 by a wide margin.

## The things Zedwell does not put on the booking page

These come from Zedwell's own room pages and FAQ rather than a review, and several of them will decide whether the format suits you.

**The room is cleaned every four days, not daily.** Fresh towels come with that clean. You can ask for more at reception for a fee, or collect clean towels and bedding yourself at any time.

**There is no kettle, no mini bar and no television**, deliberately. There is also no hairdryer and no iron in the room — those are **£10 to have delivered**, though Piccadilly Circus and Tottenham Court Road have free beauty and ironing rooms.

**There is no breakfast.** Zedwell's answer to the question is a list of nearby restaurants. Budget for a café.

**Check-out is 10am**, which is early, and check-in is 3pm. Both are moveable for money: early check-in from 9am is **£60** at Piccadilly Circus, Knightsbridge, Park Lane and Tottenham Court Road, **£25** at Greenwich and **£20** at the Capsule site. Late check-out is charged the same way.

**Foreign nationals have to upload a passport copy online before arrival**, via a secure link emailed after booking. UK residents can check in with any government photo ID. This catches people out at 11pm.

Wi-Fi is free, unlimited devices, and Zedwell quote over 50 Mbps. Reception is staffed 24 hours.

## The windowless question

Every Zedwell is windowless on purpose. The pitch is sleep rather than stay, and the design follows from it: soundproofing on walls, floors and doors, purified air, blackout by default, and a bathroom mirror with circadian-rhythm lighting that mimics a daylight cycle.

**Two of the seven are entirely underground.** Tottenham Court Road is carved out of a disused car park beneath Great Russell Street — 206 rooms with no surface at all — and Park Lane, the newest, is the same idea in Mayfair.

**Reviewers split harder on this than on anything else in London accommodation.** One group calls it the best sleep they have had in the city: no traffic, no dawn, no thin curtains on a Soho street. Another finds a windowless room genuinely oppressive, and the published reviews include some blunt ones — a capsule guest at Piccadilly Circus called it a "depressing prison". Both reactions are common enough that neither is the outlier.

Two practical notes from guest reviews rather than the marketing. **Rooms run warm**, and the climate control is less individual than it sounds. And there is **no daylight cue at all**, so you wake to an alarm or you do not wake.

If you have never slept in a windowless room, book one night before you book four.

## The luggage problem

This one applies to the capsules rather than the rooms.

A capsule's storage is **two hooks and a narrow shelf**, with room for a slim bag between the mattress and the wall. Lockers are bought separately rather than included. Guests report leaving larger suitcases outside the pod overnight and pulling them in during the day, which is workable but not what most people picture.

**Zedwell charges £15 per piece, non-refundable,** for the secure luggage room by reception. On a two-night capsule stay with one big case that is nearly a quarter of the price of the bed.

If you are arriving from a flight with a full-size case, this is an argument for booking a room rather than a capsule — or for a hostel, where lockers are standard.

## The properties

### Zedwell Piccadilly Circus — the flagship, and the priciest

*From £97 a Cocoon 1 · Windowless private room · Piccadilly Circus*

Inside the Grade II London Pavilion, directly on Piccadilly Circus, with nearly a thousand rooms. The address is the whole story: the middle of the West End, no window, and a rate that is ordinary London money for one person and very good money for six.

The busiest and least peaceful of the Zedwells despite the soundproofing, purely because of where it is. **This is the one where the booking mistake happens** — the separate Capsule property is minutes away under an almost identical name.

### Zedwell Tottenham Court Road — the underground one

*From £81 a Cocoon 1 · Windowless private room · Two minutes from the British Museum*

**London's first hotel entirely below ground**, in a disused car park beneath Great Russell Street, with 206 rooms. Here the windowless format stops being a design choice and becomes a fact of the building.

**About £16 a night cheaper than Piccadilly for the same product**, two minutes from the British Museum, and on the Elizabeth line, Northern and Central lines — which makes it the best-connected of the seven if you are coming from Heathrow. Rooms stop at Cocoon 4, so larger groups have to go to Piccadilly.

### Zedwell Greenwich — the value one

*From £70 a Cocoon 2 · Windowless private room · Riverside*

The same format at Greenwich prices, by the river, within reach of the Cutty Sark and the Observatory, and **the obvious base for a gig at the O2**.

**The cheapest bed in the estate per head.** A Cocoon 4 is £124 for four — £31 each, which undercuts a Zone 1 capsule berth. Early check-in is £25 here against £60 in the West End, too. The pick of them if you want the format without the West End around it.

### Zedwell Knightsbridge — the couples one

*From £89 a Cocoon 2 · Windowless private room, en-suite · SW1*

At 127–139 Knightsbridge, two minutes from the station, between Hyde Park, Harrods and South Kensington. **One room type only** — a Cocoon 2, 7 sq m, sleeping two — so there is no single rate and no family option.

For two people at £45 each it is the cheapest bed in SW1 by a distance, and the postcode is doing none of the usual work on the price. For one person it is poor value against Tottenham Court Road.

### Zedwell Park Lane — the new one

*Not currently bookable on Hotels.com · Windowless private room · Mayfair*

77 Park Lane, W1K 7TP: underground like Tottenham Court Road, and the brand's newest and most design-led property. The listing exists and carries fifteen guest reviews, but **no room card returned a rate on either date we tried**, so book direct or expect to.

We have not stayed in or priced it, so it is listed rather than recommended.

### Zedwell Capsule Piccadilly Circus — the actual bargain

*From £33 a capsule · Shared dormitory · Piccadilly Circus*

The cheapest way to sleep in the middle of the West End, and the row that makes this whole guide necessary. A berth with a roller shutter, the same Hypnos mattress as the rooms, shared bathrooms of private shower stalls, keycard access to the sleeping floors.

**£33 for a mixed floor, £40 for the women-only one.** Bring a padlock. Opened September 2025.

### Zedwell Leicester Place Capsule — the second capsule site

*Book direct · Shared dormitory · Leicester Square*

The newer of Zedwell's two capsule properties, steps from Leicester Square, in the same format. **It is not sold on Hotels.com at all** — a search of the whole brand there returns six properties and this is not one of them — so go to Zedwell directly. Expect something near the £33 Piccadilly rate until someone checks it.

### Otherwander Soho — the true capsule, at a Soho price

*From £62 a single nest, £98 a double · Capsule · Dean Street*

A genuine capsule hotel on Dean Street, adults only, with air conditioning, lighting and sockets in each pod, steps from Soho's centre.

Sold as **"nests", stacked upper and lower** — and the booking page charges a pound more for a lower one, which tells you which people prefer. **You cannot stand up inside and the bathrooms are communal.** They are clean and room-like rather than hostel-grim, but they are shared, and reviewers report hearing corridor conversation and neighbouring pods.

At £62 for one it is nearly double a Zedwell capsule and within £20 of a private room at Tottenham Court Road. You are paying for Dean Street, and it is worth being clear with yourself that that is what you are doing.

### The GreenHouse Capsules — the East London one

*From £42 a capsule · Capsule · Roman Road, Bethnal Green*

An eco-conscious capsule hostel on Roman Road, a working East London market street rather than a tourist strip, a short walk from Victoria Park and about ten minutes from Brick Lane.

**£42 against £33 in Zone 1** — so on price alone the walk east buys you nothing, which was not true when we first listed it. Rated **7.3 out of 10 across 41 guest reviews**, the most modest score here and worth taking at face value. The solar panels and sustainable materials are the operator's own framing and we have not independently verified them.

## Who this format suits

**Book a capsule if** you are alone, you are in London to do things and need somewhere to sleep between them, and you want a Zone 1 address for hostel money without the dorm.

**Book a cocoon room if** there are two or more of you. It is the only configuration where the format is clearly good value, and at four or more sharing beds it becomes the cheapest private accommodation in central London by some distance.

**Do not book either if** you want to spend time in your room; you are claustrophobic or unsure about windowless sleeping; you are travelling with a full-size suitcase; or you want the social side of a hostel. Pods are private and deliberately not sociable.

**On accessibility:** the Zedwell hotels list accessible rooms and lifts, and Piccadilly Circus and Tottenham Court Road both sell a Cocoon 2 Accessible. The capsules are limited, and a berth you climb into is not step-free by any definition. Check directly if this matters, and do not rely on the booking-site filter to tell the two products apart.

The row people get wrong is the third. **A windowless private room for one is not a bargain** — £97 at Piccadilly Circus is what a room costs in London, and what you have given up is the window.

The row that makes the format worth knowing about is the fourth. **Fill the room and it collapses to a third of that**, and you are still behind your own door.
