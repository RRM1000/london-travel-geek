London's Italian restaurants stopped being one thing about a decade ago. There is now a Sicilian room in Fitzrovia, a Milanese one in London Fields, a Florentine steak house in Clerkenwell and a Ligurian kitchen at The Langham — and they cook genuinely different food.

So this guide is arranged by **region and by what you are paying**, because "Italian" tells you almost nothing useful and the price range here runs from a £3.95 pizza to a £150 dinner.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **River Café** is the famous one and worth it for the terrace. **Trullo** is what London chefs actually recommend. **Bancone** and **Padella** are the value picks that people queue for. **Norma** for Sicilian, **Brutto** for Florentine steak, and **Café Murano** for Michelin-standard cooking at a third of Michelin prices.

> 📘 **How we choose these (editorial note)**
> No paid placements. These are drawn from a wide spread of sources rather than any single list - the food and travel press, the big listings sites, independent blogs and specialists, video, and what Londoners recommend themselves - and cross-referenced against each other. Where the critics disagree we say so rather than picking a side. Prices change; check before travelling.

## The fine dining

### River Café, Hammersmith

*££££ · book months ahead · Michelin star*

Michelin-starred simplicity on the Thames, where both Jamie Oliver and Hugh Fearnley-Whittingstall trained. Informal in atmosphere, upmarket in cooking, and the riverside terrace tables are the ones everyone wants and book furthest ahead.

### Murano, Mayfair

*££££ · 5 min from Green Park · book weeks ahead · Michelin star*

Angela Hartnett's Mayfair dining room, starred within four months of opening in 2008. Northern Italian cooking in a formal room, named after the Venetian glass-making island.

If the price is the obstacle, **Café Murano** in Covent Garden is the same kitchen thinking at roughly a third of it.

![A charcuterie board with olive oil and grissini](../../assets/articles/best-italian-restaurants-london/murano.jpg)

*Angela Hartnett's Mayfair dining room. The pasta course is the one to build the meal around. Photo: [Ewan-M](https://www.flickr.com/photos/55935853@N00/5210809193), [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/).*

### Langosteria London, Westminster

*££££ · 3 min from Embankment*

The Milan seafood group's first London site, and it did not pick a modest address: the **Old War Office on Whitehall**, the building Churchill worked in, now a hotel.

The cooking is Ligurian-leaning seafood — spaghetti with clams, sea urchin, raw fish plates — done with the precision the room demands and priced accordingly. This is the one for an anniversary rather than a Tuesday.

Book the dining room rather than the bar if the occasion is the point.

### Sale e Pepe Mare, Marylebone

*££££ · 3 min from Oxford Circus*

Ligurian coastal cooking inside **The Langham** on Portland Place — lighter and more seafood-led than the Knightsbridge original, with the dining room to match the hotel.

It carries over the trick the original is known for: **cacio e pepe finished inside a hollowed pecorino wheel** at the table, tossed in front of you. It is theatre, and it also happens to make the pasta better.

Book it for lunch if the dinner price is steep — same kitchen, same wheel.

### Sale e Pepe, Knightsbridge

*£££ · 3 min from Knightsbridge*

The original, and a different proposition from its Marylebone offshoot — **trading off Sloane Street since 1974**, with trolley service and tableside flambé still intact.

The kind of room London stopped building decades ago, and the reason the Marylebone site has a name worth borrowing.

## By region

The section that justifies the page. Each of these cooks somewhere specific rather than "Italy".

### Norma, Fitzrovia — Sicilian

*£££ · 5 min from Goodge Street*

Sicilian cooking with the Moorish influence kept in rather than smoothed out, across four floors of a Fitzrovia townhouse.

**Worth knowing that the critics split on this one.** It is in the Michelin Guide and holds two AA rosettes, but Time Out gave it three stars out of five, and The Infatuation found it inconsistent between visits. Go for the fried starters — the spaghettini fritters are the dish everyone agrees on — and treat the rest as less certain.

### Campania & Jones, Bethnal Green — Campanian

*£££ · a converted dairy*

Cooking from the Campania region — the stretch of coast around Naples and Amalfi — in a **converted Victorian dairy** a minute from Columbia Road flower market.

Pasta is made by hand every day and the menu is short enough that the kitchen clearly means it. The room is half the reason to go: white tiles, marble, and the original dairy fittings left where they were.

**Go on a Sunday** and do the flower market first — it runs 8am to about 3pm and the two together make an afternoon.

### Brutto, Clerkenwell — Florentine

*££ · 4 min from Farringdon*

Deliberately unfussy Florentine cooking from the late Russell Norman, who did more than anyone to make London restaurants stop trying so hard.

**The bistecca is the order** — an 800g Florentine T-bone for sharing, priced by weight at around £8.25 per 100g, so a big one lands near £80 between two. The rest of the menu is cheap, short and knows exactly what it is there for: crostini, a few pastas, and negronis at prices that belong to another decade.

Walk-ins are taken at the bar.

### Ornella, London Fields — Milanese

*£££ · 7 min from Hackney Central*

Milanese cooking on Wilton Way from the team behind Roman-influenced Lupa in Highbury — so the two restaurants deliberately cook different halves of the country.

A **butter-yellow room** and a menu short enough to read in a minute: risotto alla milanese, cotoletta, and whatever the kitchen has decided on that week. Milanese food is richer and more butter-and-rice than the tomato-and-olive-oil south, which is the whole point of coming.

It is a neighbourhood restaurant rather than a destination, and better for it.

### Macellaio RC, South Kensington — Piedmontese

*£££ · 5 min from South Kensington*

A butcher's counter that became a restaurant, and still behaves like one — **Piedmontese Fassona beef hung in the window**, dry-ageing in full view, cut to order in front of you.

Fassona is a lean, low-fat breed that tastes almost nothing like a dry-aged British sirloin: cleaner, more mineral, and usually served rare or raw. The **carne cruda**, hand-chopped raw beef, is the dish that explains the place in one plate.

Order by weight, share it, and let them choose the cut.

### Bar Etna, Newington Green — Sicilian-American

*£££ · 8 min from Canonbury*

**Joe Beddia** — whose Philadelphia pizzeria was once called the best in America — in his only restaurant outside the US, on a Newington Green corner most visitors never reach.

Sicilian-American rather than Neapolitan: thick, square, generous, and closer to what an Italian family in Brooklyn would recognise than anything in Naples. **The tomato pie is the thing**, sauce over cheese, served at room temperature.

Worth the trip north, and quieter midweek.

## Pasta worth queueing for

### Trullo, Highbury

*£££ · 3 min from Highbury & Islington · book weeks ahead*

Tim Siadatan, ex-St John and Moro, cooking a frequently changing seasonal menu of hand-made pasta and charcoal-grilled meat. The **pappardelle with eight-hour beef shin ragu** is the dish, and this is the Italian restaurant London chefs name most.

![A grilled steak with mashed peas and gravy](../../assets/articles/best-italian-restaurants-london/trullo.jpg)

*Islington. The hand-rolled pasta changes daily and the beef over charcoal is the constant. Photo: [Ewan-M](https://www.flickr.com/photos/55935853@N00/14194656052), [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/).*

### Padella, Borough

*£ · walk-in only*

A short menu of hand-rolled pasta at low prices, which is why the queue exists. Original site at Borough Market takes no bookings — use the virtual queue app. Also in Shoreditch and Soho.

![The interior counter of Padella with cooks working behind it](../../assets/articles/best-italian-restaurants-london/padella.jpg)

*Sit at the counter and watch the pasta being rolled. The queue moves faster than it looks. Photo: [Bex.Walton](https://www.flickr.com/photos/7831824@N04/28035454561), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).*

### Bancone, Covent Garden

*££ · 3 min from Charing Cross*

Watch the pasta being rolled at the counter, at prices well below what a **Michelin Bib Gourmand** normally costs.

The **silk handkerchiefs with walnut butter and confit egg yolk** are the signature, and genuinely originated here — the dish has since been copied across half of London, which tells you enough. Around £12 a plate.

The Covent Garden original takes no bookings for small tables. The Borough and Golden Square sites do, and the pasta is the same.

### Manteca, Shoreditch

*£££ · 10 min from Old Street · Bib Gourmand*

**Whole-animal butchery** feeding everything else on the menu — the salumi is cured in house, the bread is wood-fired, and nothing much is wasted.

The signatures are the **house coppa and the pig-skin crackling**, and the brown-butter cacio e pepe is the most-photographed plate in the building. Pastas rotate constantly: spicy lumache in tomato vodka sauce, fazzoletti with duck ragu and duck-fat pangrattato.

The focaccia is the thing people talk about afterwards, and it does run out — order it when you sit down.

## Best value

### Mucci's, Chelsea

*££ · every starter £10, every main £20*

**Every starter £10, every main £20.** Owner Giuseppe Mucci built the menu that way after listening to guests who wanted to eat well on the King's Road without watching the bill climb, and it is the single best-value proposition in this postcode.

The tuna tartare and the **200g sliced sirloin** are the picks — a Chelsea steak for £20 is not a thing that otherwise exists. Portions are generous rather than clever.

Giuseppe is usually in the room, which is most of why people go back.

### ICCO, Fitzrovia

*£ · 46 Goodge Street · from £3.95*

**A twelve-inch pizza from £3.95**, made to order — roughly a third of what anywhere else in central London charges for the same thing.

"The People's Pizzeria" has been on Goodge Street since 1999, which in this part of town is close to a miracle. Thin, crisp, unfussy, handed over fast. There is seating but most people take it away.

The bloggers found this one long before the magazines did, and it is still the cheapest proper meal in Fitzrovia.

### Al Dente, Fitzrovia

*£ · 3 min from Goodge Street · pasta under £15*

Handmade pasta rolled in front of you, a few doors from ICCO on the same street. **Every plate is under £15**, which is why it fills with students at lunch and solo diners at night.

The cacio e pepe is the one to order. There are further branches in South Kensington and by Monument.

### Lina Stores, Soho

*££ · Brewer Street*

The **mint-green deli on Brewer Street since 1944**, opened by an Italian couple for Soho's Italian community, and still selling to it.

The restaurants came seventy years later and the shop is still the point: fresh pasta cut to order, salumi, cheese and tinned things worth carrying home. Eat at the counter for a plate of ravioli in the room where the queue is buying dinner around you.

**Take the pasta home** — it is a fraction of the restaurant price and the best souvenir on this page.

### Café Murano, Covent Garden

*££ · 5 min from Covent Garden*

Angela Hartnett's everyday counterpart to Murano — **the same kitchen thinking at roughly a third of the price**, and the strongest value-to-quality trade on this page.

Northern Italian small plates and pasta rather than a tasting menu, in a room designed to be used on a normal Tuesday. The **set lunch** is where the maths becomes genuinely silly for cooking of this standard.

Ideal for pre-theatre — it is minutes from most of the Covent Garden houses and used to people who have a curtain to catch.

## The rooms people book for

### Ave Mario, Covent Garden and Circolo Popolare, Fitzrovia

*££ · Big Mamma group · book weeks ahead*

Two rooms from the Big Mamma group, and **the rooms are the product**. Ave Mario has stripy humbug walls and bright red seats across a Covent Garden basement; Circolo Popolare has the wall of thousands of bottles and the lemon meringue pie that arrives the size of a fin.

The food is good rather than remarkable — big portions, fair prices, all-Italian menu. What you are booking is the spectacle, and on that it delivers completely.

**Book weeks ahead**, or take a walk-in seat at the bar early in the evening.

### Forza Wine, South Bank

*£££ · 5 min from Waterloo*

A terrace on top of the **National Theatre** looking straight up the Thames — arguably the best free view attached to any restaurant in London, and no ticket required.

The food is Italian small plates rather than a full menu: cauliflower fritti with aioli, burrata with pickled onions, whatever is seasonal. The wine list is the real draw — natural, low-intervention, heavy on chilled reds and orange.

**Go for the golden hour and go early.** It is first-come on the terrace and everyone else has had the same idea.

### Al Boccon di'vino, Richmond

*£££ · no menu*

There is no menu. You are brought course after course of whatever is being cooked that night, the way an Italian family would feed you. Turn up hungry and without a plan.

## What to know

* **Region beats rating.** Choosing between Sicilian, Florentine and Milanese will change your meal more than a star will.
* **Padella and Bancone do not take bookings** at their original sites. Both run virtual queues.
* **Big Mamma rooms** (Circolo, Ave Mario, Jacuzzi, Barbarella) book out weeks ahead and are loud by design.
* **The pizza is a separate question** — see our dedicated guide below.
* **Service charge** of 12.5% is discretionary and standard on London restaurant bills.
