Covent Garden and Soho form London's most convenient entertainment district. Theatres, Chinatown, Trafalgar Square, the National Gallery and dozens of restaurants are close enough to walk.

> **Best for:** a first visit, theatre, restaurants, nightlife and travellers who dislike changing trains. **Think twice if:** you need quiet streets, large rooms or easy coach parking.

## Best things to do

- Explore Covent Garden Piazza before the busiest afternoon period.
- Visit the National Gallery and National Portrait Gallery.
- Walk through Seven Dials, Soho and Chinatown.
- Book a West End performance.
- Browse independent shops around Neal Street, Carnaby and Berwick Street.

## Hotels

| Tier | Hotel | Why consider it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | NoMad London | Dramatic design opposite the Royal Opera House |
| Mid-range | The Resident Covent Garden | Exceptionally central with useful in-room facilities |
| Value-focused | Z Hotel Soho | Small rooms but a strong location for late evenings |

Ask for a room away from street-facing nightlife if sleep matters. Air conditioning and glazing are important.

Bocca di Lupo, Chinatown and the restaurants of Seven Dials provide further choice. Reserve a pre-theatre table and tell the restaurant when the performance begins.

For a broader comparison, see [the best Indian restaurants in London](/articles/best-indian-restaurants-london/).

## Transport and airport fit

Covent Garden station can become crowded and has lifts with stairs at the final section. Leicester Square, Tottenham Court Road, Charing Cross and Holborn may be easier depending on the hotel.

Tottenham Court Road gives direct Elizabeth line journeys from Heathrow and fast connections across London.

## Theatre strategy

Book the performance before dinner. Check the exact theatre—not merely “West End”—and aim to eat within a ten-minute walk. Collect tickets and locate the entrance before sitting down if the schedule is tight.
