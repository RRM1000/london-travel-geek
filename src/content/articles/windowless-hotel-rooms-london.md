**Six operators in London sell rooms with no window at all.** Zedwell runs five hotels where every room is windowless by design. **Every one of Z Hotels' twelve London branches sells a windowless room**, and at nine of them it is the cheapest rate in the building. hub by Premier Inn, Point A, easyHotel and The Corner sell them too.

Below: what the rooms are like, how each brand discloses them, and what daylight costs, which runs from £5 to £35 for the identical room.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** A windowless room is a **small, private, en-suite hotel room with no daylight**, and it is not a capsule. **Zedwell** is the purpose-built version: for two people the second guest adds about £5 a night. **Z Hotels** sells a windowless "Inside" grade at all twelve London branches — cheapest at **Shoreditch and Victoria, £50** — but the premium for daylight varies wildly, from £5 at Tottenham Court Road to £35 at Shoreditch. **Read the grade name before you pay.**
>
> **Three picks:** [The Z Hotel Shoreditch](hotel:z-hotel-shoreditch) (£50 windowless, two minutes from Old Street) · [Zedwell Greenwich](hotel:zedwell-greenwich) (£66–£145, Cutty Sark DLR) · [Zedwell Piccadilly Circus](hotel:zedwell-piccadilly-circus) (£97–£287, one minute from the Tube).

## Two different things get called a windowless room

**Windowless by design** is Zedwell: there is no room with a window to upgrade to, and two of the five hotels are entirely below ground.

**Windowless as a grade** is everyone else. Z Hotels, hub by Premier Inn, Point A, easyHotel and The Corner sell their interior rooms, usually cheaper. **Disclosure ranges from a word in the room's name to a footnote behind an asterisk**.

Neither is a capsule: every room in this guide has its own door and shower. Our [London capsule hotels guide](/articles/pod-hotels-london/) covers capsules.

## What a purpose-built windowless room is like

![A Zedwell cocoon room: a bed on a lit wooden plinth, a full-length mirror, hooks and a private door](../../assets/articles/windowless-hotel-rooms-london/zedwell-cocoon-room.jpg)

*A cocoon room.*

**Your own room, your own door, your own bathroom.** The smallest Zedwell is 7 sq m. The bed sits on a lit oak plinth, there is a full-length mirror, hooks, not a wardrobe, and an **en-suite with a walk-in rainfall shower**.

**Soundproofing** on walls, floors and doors, and purified, temperature-controlled air instead of an opening window. The bathroom mirror runs circadian-rhythm lighting that mimics a daylight cycle.

**Hypnos mattress, Egyptian cotton sheets, Wi-Fi over 50 Mbps.** Reception is staffed 24 hours.

hub lights its window-free rooms with a self-dimming back-lit box and what it calls circadian light wells; The Corner builds the bed and the bathroom into one moulded cube with blank walls. None gives a daylight cue in the morning.

## What you give up, beyond the window

**Zedwell cleans the room every fourth night, not daily**, with fresh towels. You can pay for extra cleans or collect towels and bedding from reception at any time. easyHotel Victoria cleans free every sixth night and charges **£15** for an extra clean.

**Zedwell has no kettle, mini bar or television.** No hairdryer or iron either — those are **£10 to have delivered to the room**. Piccadilly Circus and Tottenham Court Road have free beauty and ironing rooms; Greenwich, Knightsbridge and Park Lane do not. Z Hotels and The Corner both put a TV in the room; hub has no kettle but free tea and coffee all day.

**There is no breakfast at Zedwell.** Point A includes breakfast if you book direct; Z Hotels charges for it outside its Club grade.

**Zedwell check-in is 3pm and check-out 10am.** Both ends move for a fee:

| | Early check-in from 9am | From noon | Late check-out to noon | To 2pm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Piccadilly Circus, Knightsbridge, Park Lane, Tottenham Court Road** | £60 | £40 | £50 | **£80** |
| **Greenwich** | £25 | £15 | £25 | £50 |

**After 2pm you are charged a full night** at every one. On a quiet Sunday, when a Cocoon 1 at Piccadilly Circus is £97, an £80 late check-out is most of a second night's price. Greenwich is less than half the price at both ends.

**Foreign nationals must upload a passport copy before arrival** at Zedwell, through a secure link emailed after booking. UK residents can check in with any government photo ID.

## The Zedwell pricing, which works backwards

A windowless room for one person is not a bargain. Across five sample dates — a quiet Sunday, a February midweek, an October Saturday, the second Saturday of December and a Saturday in April — a single room at Zedwell Piccadilly Circus ran from **£97 to £287**, ordinary money for central London.

**The value is in filling the room.** The Cocoon number is how many people it sleeps, and the price barely moves with it:

| Date sampled | Room for one | Room for two | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet Sunday | £97 | £102 | **+£5** |
| February midweek | £113 | £117 | **+£4** |
| October Saturday | £253 | £258 | **+£5** |
| December Saturday | £287 | £291 | **+£4** |
| April Saturday | £168 | £172 | **+£4** |

A second guest adds £4 or £5 on a room whose price swings by nearly £200 across the year. Tottenham Court Road adds £8 or £9.

**A Cocoon 8 sleeps eight for about two and a half times the price of a single**, and that ratio held to within about three per cent on every sample date.

**The limit is the beds.** From Cocoon 4 upwards the rooms are *double* beds in bunks: a Cocoon 4 is two doubles, a Cocoon 8 is four. The per-person price assumes people share a bed, so two couples suit a Cocoon 4 and four friends who each want their own bed do not.

Floor space works the same way: a Cocoon 8 is 20 sq m for eight people, against 7 sq m for one.

## Z Hotels: all twelve London branches sell one

Z Hotels has **twelve London branches**, and every one publishes at least one room grade with no window.

Z's own word for them is **"Inside"**, and the room page says an Inside room has all the features of the standard bedroom, "just with no curtains to draw". Most cards also carry a small **"no window"** tag beside the occupancy.

**At nine of the twelve branches the cheapest published rate in the hotel is a room with no window.** At Piccadilly it ties with the single; only at City and Soho does the lead rate come with daylight.

## The accessible room is often the windowless one

**Four Z branches sell a wheelchair-accessible room with no window**: Holborn at £60 (15 sq m), Strand at £65 (16 sq m), Trafalgar at £70 (14 sq m) and Leicester Square at £80 (16.4 sq m). At Holborn, Strand and Trafalgar there is also an accessible room with a window, at £75, £80 and £90 — so the step up is £15 to £20 and the choice is real.

**At Z Leicester Square the choice is much harder.** The only standard accessible room is the windowless one at £80; the next accessible grade is a Club Queen at £135. That is a £55 step to get daylight.

And on two of those four — Holborn's Z Inside Accessible and Leicester Square's Inside Queen (accessible) — **the word "Inside" in the name is the only signal on the page**. Neither carries the no-window tag, and neither description mentions a window at all.

## The other four operators

### hub by Premier Inn — the "ambient room", and where it actually is

*16 London sites · Interior grades published at Westminster Abbey and Paddington · 11–14 sq m*

hub's brand page carries a section headed "Our ambient rooms" stating plainly that they are **window-free**, lit instead by a self-dimming back-lit box, "circadian light wells" that mimic daylight, or frosted glass. Beneath it sits the double-asterisk footnote: the estate operates a mix of sites, and **this includes rooms that are window-free**. Neither line says which hotel.

hub's room FAQ says some rooms have a window and some are window-free, and that the ones **underground or on lower ground levels** have no natural daylight. The rule is a floor, not a hotel.

Of the sixteen London hub sites, **two publish the grade on the hotel page**. hub London Westminster Abbey, 21 Tothill Street SW1H, sells a Standard room – Interior at 11 sq m and a Bigger room – Interior at 14 sq m, both described as having no windows.

## How to tell before you pay

How each operator discloses it:

| Operator | What it calls the room | Where you find out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Corner** | Snug Room **(windowless)** | In the room's name |
| **Point A** | "windowless rooms" | Property description, plain English |
| **Zedwell** | Cocoon | The whole hotel; unavoidable |
| **Z Hotels** | **"Inside"**, "no curtains to draw" | Grade name plus a small tag |
| **hub by Premier Inn** | "ambient" on the brand page, **"Interior"** on the hotel page | Two different words, two different pages |
| **easyHotel** | "no window" option | A FAQ answer near the foot of the page |

Three rules. **Read the grade name, not the price**: Inside, Interior, Snug and ambient all mean the same thing. **Check the floor area against the next grade up**; when two rooms are the same size and different prices, the difference is usually the window. And **at Z Hotels, check the gap**: £5 at Tottenham Court Road, £35 at Shoreditch.

## Who should not book one

**Do not book a windowless room if** you want to spend daytime in your room, you are claustrophobic, or you are unsure how you react to sleeping without daylight. Guest reviews report that **the rooms run warm** and that there is **no daylight cue**, so you wake to an alarm or you do not wake. Reviewers split: some call it the best sleep they have had in the city, others find it oppressive. Book one night before you book four.

To test it cheaply, take **the £70 Inside Double at Z Tottenham Court Road**; the same room with a window is £75.

## Not the same thing as a capsule

Zedwell also runs two **capsule** properties and calls the sleeping unit there a "Capsule Cocoon", so *cocoon* appears on both products. A capsule is a berth in a shared dormitory with shared bathrooms, at roughly a third of the price of a room. **Look for "Capsule" in the property name.**

Every room in this guide has its own lockable door and en-suite shower. Our [London capsule hotels guide](/articles/pod-hotels-london/) covers the five capsule properties.

For a longer stay at a lower price, compare an [aparthotel](/articles/aparthotels-london/): a kitchen changes the arithmetic more than a missing window. To keep the window, [the best budget hotels in London](/articles/best-budget-hotels-london/) lists hotels whose typical night came to £150 or less.
