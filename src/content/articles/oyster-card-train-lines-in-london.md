London has several rail systems that appear together on Transport for London maps. They do not all have the same name, trains or fare boundaries, but Oyster pay as you go works on most journeys within London.

> **The short answer:** Oyster is accepted throughout the London Underground, Docklands Light Railway and London Overground. It is accepted on the Elizabeth line as far west as West Drayton, but not between Iver and Reading. It is also accepted on National Rail at stations inside London's Oyster pay-as-you-go area. Contactless payment extends beyond the Oyster boundary on some routes.

If you are choosing between Oyster and your own bank card, start with the [complete Oyster card and contactless guide](/articles/oyster-card-guide-london/). If you are new to the network, [How to Use the London Underground](/articles/how-to-use-the-london-underground/) explains touching in and out, fare zones, interchanges and common first-time mistakes.

## What the ratings mean

The comfort and crowding ratings in this guide are **editorial estimates, not official TfL scores**. They are intended to help a visitor compare a typical journey:

- **Comfort:** 1/5 means older, hotter or more cramped trains; 5/5 means spacious, smooth and air-conditioned.
- **Crowding:** 1/5 is usually quieter; 5/5 can be extremely busy on central sections or at commuter times.

Conditions vary by section, direction, disruption and time of day. A train rated 3/5 for crowding can still be packed after a major event.

## London Underground

Oyster pay as you go is accepted at every London Underground station. All 11 Tube lines are included below; the Elizabeth line, DLR and London Overground are separate railway systems and follow in their own sections.

| Underground line | Oyster | Typical crowding | Comfort | Air-conditioning | Useful to know |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Bakerloo | Yes | 3/5 | 2/5 | No | Older trains and a warm central section; useful between Paddington, the West End and Waterloo. |
| Central | Yes | 5/5 | 2/5 | No | Very fast east-west route, but frequently hot and crowded through Zone 1. |
| Circle | Yes | 3/5 | 4/5 | Yes | Modern walk-through trains; shares tracks with other lines and is not a simple closed loop. |
| District | Yes | 4/5 | 4/5 | Yes | Spacious trains and many branches, so check the destination before boarding. |
| Hammersmith & City | Yes | 3/5 | 4/5 | Yes | Modern walk-through trains; useful across the northern edge of central London. |
| Jubilee | Yes | 5/5 | 3/5 | No | Fast and frequent; the modern eastern stations are roomy, but trains get very busy. |
| Metropolitan | Yes | 3/5 | 4/5 | Yes | Comfortable trains and faster outer-London services; check whether a train is fast, semi-fast or all-stations. |
| Northern | Yes | 5/5 | 2/5 | No | Has several branches. Check both the central route and the final destination. |
| Piccadilly | Yes | 5/5 | 2/5 | Existing fleet: no. New fleet: yes | Direct to Heathrow but awkward with luggage at busy times. Air-conditioned replacement trains are due to begin entering service during 2026. |
| Victoria | Yes | 5/5 | 2/5 | No | Extremely frequent and usually fast, but hot and intensely busy at peak times. |
| Waterloo & City | Yes | 5/5 at peak | 2/5 | No | A two-stop commuter shuttle between Waterloo and Bank; check its operating days and hours. |

The Circle, District, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan lines use modern S-stock trains with walk-through carriages and air-conditioning. The deep-level lines generally use forced ventilation rather than passenger air-conditioning. Piccadilly line fleet replacement is beginning in 2026, so the train you receive will depend on the rollout.

## Elizabeth line

The Elizabeth line is shown in purple. It crosses London from Shenfield and Abbey Wood in the east through the central tunnels to Heathrow and Reading in the west.

- **Oyster:** Yes between Shenfield or Abbey Wood and West Drayton, including Heathrow. **No from Iver to Reading.**
- **Contactless:** Accepted across the full line to Reading.
- **Typical crowding:** 4/5, rising to 5/5 on the central section at peak times.
- **Comfort:** 5/5.
- **Air-conditioning:** Yes.
- **Other advantages:** Long walk-through trains, generous luggage space and step-free access from street to platform at every Elizabeth line station. Boarding is level at the new central stations and Heathrow.

The train in the main photograph is at Reading, which is deliberately useful here: you may use contactless for that journey, but **not Oyster**. If you are going to Heathrow, Elizabeth line and Piccadilly line fares and journey times differ, so use the [complete Heathrow Airport transport guide](/articles/heathrow-airport-to-london/) alongside TfL's Single Fare Finder.

## Docklands Light Railway

The DLR serves the City, Canary Wharf, the Royal Docks, London City Airport, Stratford, Woolwich and Lewisham. Its trains are automated, although a passenger service agent is normally on board.

- **Oyster:** Yes across the whole DLR.
- **Typical crowding:** 3/5 overall and 4/5 around Canary Wharf at commuter times.
- **Comfort:** 3/5 during the fleet transition.
- **Air-conditioning:** Older trains: no. New B23 trains: yes.
- **Other advantages:** Every DLR station has step-free access from street to platform. The front seats give an excellent driver's-eye view when available.

TfL is replacing the older fleet with spacious, walk-through B23 trains. The new trains have air-conditioning and charging points, but the proportion available can change during the rollout.

## London Overground

London Overground is the orange network that circles and crosses inner and outer London. Its six routes now have individual names. Oyster is accepted at every station on every London Overground line.

| Overground line | Main route | Oyster | Typical crowding | Comfort | Air-conditioning |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Liberty | Romford–Upminster | Yes | 2/5 | 4/5 | Yes |
| Lioness | Euston–Watford Junction | Yes | 4/5 | 4/5 | Yes |
| Mildmay | Stratford–Richmond or Clapham Junction | Yes | 4/5 | 4/5 | Yes |
| Suffragette | Gospel Oak–Barking Riverside | Yes | 3/5 | 4/5 | Yes |
| Weaver | Liverpool Street–Enfield Town, Cheshunt or Chingford | Yes | 4/5 | 4/5 | Yes |
| Windrush | Highbury & Islington–New Cross, Clapham Junction, Crystal Palace or West Croydon | Yes | 5/5 | 4/5 | Yes |

All London Overground trains are walk-through and air-conditioned. Several lines divide into branches, so the new line name does not remove the need to check the train's destination.

## National Rail services within London

National Rail is not one single London line. Several operators run suburban and regional trains through the Oyster area. Oyster is valid only for journeys where both stations and the permitted route are inside the pay-as-you-go boundary; it does **not** become valid across an operator's entire network.

The current TfL rail map shows services from these operators:

| Operator | London examples | Oyster guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chiltern Railways | Marylebone, Wembley and routes towards Amersham | Yes within the marked Oyster area; outer destinations are not covered. |
| c2c | Fenchurch Street and east London towards Upminster | Yes within the marked Oyster area. |
| East Midlands Railway | St Pancras and limited north London calls | Check the exact journey; contactless extends farther than Oyster on this corridor. |
| Gatwick Express | Victoria–Gatwick Airport | Oyster and contactless can be used to Gatwick, but airport fares are not ordinary zonal fares. |
| Great Northern | Moorgate or King's Cross and north London | Yes within the marked Oyster area. |
| Great Western Railway | Paddington and west London | Yes only where the station and service are inside the Oyster area. |
| Greater Anglia | Liverpool Street, Stratford and routes into east London | Yes within the marked Oyster area. |
| Heathrow Express | Paddington–Heathrow | Oyster and contactless are accepted, with a premium Heathrow Express fare. |
| London Northwestern Railway | Euston and northwest London | Yes within the marked Oyster area, including Watford Junction. |
| South Western Railway | Waterloo and southwest London | Yes within the marked Oyster area. |
| Southeastern | Charing Cross, Cannon Street, Victoria and southeast London | Yes within the marked Oyster area. |
| Southeastern high speed | St Pancras International–Stratford International | Check the exact fare before travel; special fares apply. |
| Southern | Victoria, London Bridge and south London | Yes within the marked Oyster area, including Gatwick Airport. |
| Thameslink | Cross-London services via St Pancras, Farringdon and Blackfriars | Yes within the marked Oyster area; contactless continues farther on some routes. |

This is why operator names alone are not enough. For example, a Southern train can accept Oyster for one journey but continue beyond Oyster territory later in its route.

## How to check a journey before travelling

1. Find both stations on the [official rail and Tube map](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/london-rail-and-tube-services-map.pdf).
2. Look for an Oyster boundary note or a section marked **contactless only**.
3. Enter the exact stations in TfL's [Single Fare Finder](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/single-fare-finder).
4. Use the same Oyster card throughout the journey and touch in and out, including at standalone pink route validators when your route requires one.
5. If either station is outside the Oyster area, use contactless or buy the correct rail ticket instead.

Contactless is often more flexible at the outer edge of London, but foreign card fees and card acceptance can make Oyster preferable for some visitors. Never assume the same geographic boundary applies to both.

## Best choices for comfort

For a visitor who can choose between routes:

- **Elizabeth line** is generally the most comfortable option for longer east-west journeys and airport luggage.
- **Sub-surface Tube lines** are cooler than the deep Tube because their modern trains have air-conditioning.
- **London Overground** is comfortable and air-conditioned, but the Windrush and Mildmay lines can be exceptionally busy.
- **DLR** is scenic and accessible; seek the front seats outside peak time.
- **Deep-level Tube lines** are often fastest for central journeys, but can be hot and crowded in summer.

Speed, step-free access and interchange walking time can matter more than the train itself. Check the whole journey rather than choosing purely by the ratings above.
