> 💡 **The Short Version:** The year's biggest ticketed shows are **Monet: Painting Time** at Tate Modern (**25 February to 27 June**, £26), **German Expressionism** at the National Gallery (**18 March to 1 August**), **Mary Cassatt** at the National Portrait Gallery (**11 March to 13 June**), **Rembrandt** at the Royal Academy (**9 April to 19 September**, £17), **Thomas Gainsborough** at Tate Britain for his 300th anniversary (**20 May to 10 October**), and **David Hockney** at Tate Britain from **7 October**. The **Bayeux Tapestry** stays at the British Museum until **11 July**, sold out to 31 March. **Most shows from May onwards have no price yet.** The **National Gallery's Catharina van Hemessen**, **Frankenthaler and Bassano** and **every Serpentine show** are free.

## How tickets work

- **Book the blockbusters.** Van Eyck sells timed slots at the National Gallery, and the Bayeux Tapestry is sold out to the end of March. For Monet, Rembrandt, Gainsborough and Hockney, book as soon as your dates are fixed.
- **Members go free.** Tate, the National Gallery and the V&A let members into their paid exhibitions free; at Tate, members just turn up with their card.
- **Young people pay less.** Tate Collective gives 16 to 25s £5 exhibition tickets, under-18s are free at Van Eyck, and the King's Gallery charges 18 to 24s £15.50 against £24 for adults.
- **Peak and off-peak.** The National Portrait Gallery charges £2 more for its paid shows Friday to Sunday, the Natural History Museum prices Wildlife Photographer of the Year by day, and the Hayward says its prices can change with demand.
- **The permanent collections are free** at the national museums and galleries; only the special exhibitions charge. Our [best museums](/articles/best-museums-london/) and [best galleries](/articles/best-galleries-london/) guides cover what to see without a ticket.
