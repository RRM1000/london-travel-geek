Almost everything written about London tour buses is written by someone earning a commission on the ticket. So here is the thing those pages leave out: **London's ordinary red buses cover much of the same route for £1.75, capped at £5.25 a day**, and the top deck of a normal double-decker has the same view as the top deck of a tour bus.

That does not make tour buses a rip-off. It makes them a specific product for a specific person, and this page is about working out whether that is you.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Big Bus** from **£32** online. **Golden Tours** from **£24.78** online, against **£42** at the kerb. **Never buy from a street seller** — the online discount is 25–40%. The alternative is **route 11** or **route 24** for **£1.75**, capped at **£5.25** all day.

> ⚠️ **Never buy from a seller at a bus stop.** Every operator above discounts heavily online and charges near full price at the kerb. On a Golden Tours day ticket that difference is **£17**. The street sellers are commission-paid and the price they quote is the one you should not pay.

## The honest alternative

A normal London bus costs **£1.75** a journey with contactless or Oyster, and the **daily cap is £5.25** — after that, buses are free for the rest of the day.

Better still, the **Hopper fare** gives you unlimited bus journeys within **one hour** of touching in, for that single £1.75. Hop off, look at something, hop on the next one, and it is all one fare.

### The routes worth riding

**Route 11** is the classic and the one Londoners recommend. It runs past **Liverpool Street, the Bank of England, St Paul's Cathedral, Fleet Street, the Strand, Trafalgar Square, Whitehall, the Houses of Parliament and Westminster Abbey**, and finishes at Victoria. That is most of a tour bus route for £1.75.

**Route 24** goes the other way through London: **Hampstead Heath, Camden Town, Tottenham Court Road, Trafalgar Square, Whitehall, Westminster and Pimlico**. It is the best north-to-south sightseeing run in the city.

**Route 15** covers **Trafalgar Square, the Strand, Fleet Street, St Paul's and the Tower of London** — the eastern half of the tourist map.

**Route 88** links **Camden, Oxford Circus, Piccadilly Circus, Trafalgar Square, Whitehall, Westminster and Tate Britain**.

> **The heritage Routemaster is gone.** Route 15 used to run a limited service with the old open-platform buses, and a lot of guides still send people to find it. TfL's own route 15 page now lists no heritage service. If you want a vintage Routemaster, Golden Tours runs an electric one as a paid tour.

### What you give up

Being fair about it: no commentary, no guaranteed seat, and no guaranteed top-deck front window — which is the seat everyone actually wants. Ordinary buses get crowded, and they do not wait while you photograph anything. You also need to know where you are going, because nobody will tell you.

## Who should buy a tour bus ticket

Genuinely worth it if:

* **You have limited mobility.** This is the strongest case by far. Tour buses are step-free at the door, have set stops right outside the sights, and remove the walking between them.
* **You have one day and no plan.** The commentary and the loop give you orientation you would otherwise spend half a morning acquiring.
* **You are travelling with children who have run out of legs.** A guaranteed sit-down that is still moving past things.
* **The weather is bad and you still want to see the city.** Covered lower deck, and you never wait long.
* **The bundle actually fits.** Big Bus Essential at £46.99 includes a river cruise and walking tours. If you were going to pay for a cruise anyway, the arithmetic changes.

## Who should not

* **Anyone with three or more days.** You will see these places on foot anyway, and the ticket duplicates them.
* **Anyone comfortable using contactless on a normal bus.** Route 11 costs £1.75.
* **Anyone in a hurry.** Tour buses sit in the same traffic as everything else and stop more often. Walking Westminster to Trafalgar Square is fifteen minutes; the bus is not faster.
* **Anyone planning to actually get off and look at things.** The hop-off, queue, hop-on cycle eats a day quickly.

## Traps to watch for

1. **"24 hours" is not always 24 hours.** Some tickets run for a calendar day and expire at the end of it, others run 24 hours from first use. Check which you are buying — this is the most common complaint.
2. **The kerbside price.** Covered above, and worth repeating: buy online, before you arrive if possible.
3. **"Included" extras that need separate booking.** River cruises and walking tours bundled into a ticket often still need a slot reserved, and popular ones fill.
4. **Reduced winter service.** Frequencies drop and last departures get much earlier outside summer. A 4pm start in January may not be the day you paid for.
5. **Similar-looking brands.** Several operators use comparable names, red buses and near-identical livery. Check whose ticket you are buying, because they are not interchangeable between operators.
6. **Live guide versus recorded commentary.** Recorded is multilingual and consistent; a live guide is better on a good day and worse on a bad one. Operators mix both across their routes, so check what your specific ticket gets.
7. **Traffic.** No operator can promise a frequency it does not control. Advertised gaps between buses are optimistic in central London.

## What to know

* **Contactless is all you need for ordinary buses.** No ticket, no Oyster required, just tap the reader with a bank card or phone. You do not tap out on buses. See our [fares guide](/articles/london-public-transport-costs-and-fares/).
* **The bus daily cap is separate from the Tube cap.** £5.25 for buses and trams alone; a mixed day of Tube and bus is capped differently.
* **The river bus is the better-value scenic ride.** Ordinary fares, genuine views, and it is transport rather than a tour. See [how to use London's river boats](/articles/how-to-use-london-river-boats/).
* **A free alternative to commentary:** the [three-day itinerary](/articles/three-days-in-london-itinerary/) walks the same ground with the context built in.
* **Children travel free on London buses** under 11, which changes the family arithmetic considerably against a tour bus child ticket.
