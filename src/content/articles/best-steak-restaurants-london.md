London does three different things with beef and they are not variations on each other. There is the **British steakhouse** with dry-aged native breeds, the **Basque asador** cooking aged dairy-cow chops over coals, and the **butcher's counter** where the animal is the point and the room is incidental.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Hawksmoor** is the British benchmark and **The Guinea Grill** the old-Mayfair one. **Lurra**, **Ibai** and **Sagardi** do Basque asador beef, which tastes nothing like it. **Macellaio RC** hangs Fassona in the window and cuts to order. **Blacklock** is the value pick, and does chops and steaks equally. And **Flat Iron** serves one cut for £15.

> No paid placements. ## British steakhouses

### Hawksmoor, Spitalfields

*££££ · several sites*

The benchmark. **Dry-aged native-breed British beef**, properly sourced, with sides better than most steakhouses bother with. Spitalfields is the original.

Expensive at dinner. **The set lunch and the Sunday roast are considerably cheaper** for the same kitchen and the same beef.

### Blacklock, Soho

*£££ · chops and steaks*

Cooked over coals in a Soho basement, at prices well under the steakhouse average. The name says chops, but **the menu runs both** — skinny lamb and pork chops flattened under vintage Blacklock irons, and a proper steak list of Denver, bavette, rump cap, sirloin, sixth rib-eye and fillet at £16–24, dry-aged up to 55 days.

The **Big Chops** are the sharing cuts on the bone — prime rib, porterhouse, bone-in sirloin, priced by weight at around £10–11 per 100g.

**The All In is the order** if there are two or more of you: pre-chop bites, then beef, pork and lamb piled onto charcoal-grilled flatbreads with a side each, for £28 a head.

The Sunday roast here is one of the best in London.

### Flat Iron, Soho

*£ · £15 · no bookings*

**One cut, one price.** No bookings, and the queue moves. The cheapest good steak in central London by a considerable distance.

![A flat iron steak sliced on a wooden board, with creamed spinach, beef dripping chips and sauces in enamel dishes](../../assets/articles/best-steak-restaurants-london/flat-iron-steak.jpg)

*The whole proposition, in one photograph. The steak comes sliced on a board with a cleaver-handled knife, and the sides are ordered separately — creamed spinach and beef dripping chips are the two worth having.*

Popcorn arrives while you wait and there is free salted caramel ice cream at the end, which is a large part of why the queue is tolerable.

### The Guinea Grill, Mayfair

*££££ · 7 min from Green Park*

A pub in a Mayfair mews that has been grilling since 1952, and the most-recommended steak in London that visitors never find — **five separate guides list it**, from Time Out to The Infatuation to the bloggers.

The beef is **dry-aged grass-fed English**, bought from Godfreys of Finsbury Park, and the kitchen names its supplier because that is the whole point. Order the sirloin.

The other thing to know: **the steak and kidney pie is award-winning** and costs a fraction of the beef. Plenty of regulars come for that and nothing else.

### Goodman, Mayfair

*££££ · 4 min from Oxford Circus*

A New York steakhouse played completely straight — leather booths, a specials chalkboard, and no concept beyond aged beef cooked correctly. It sits at **no. 54 in the World's 101 Best Steak Restaurants**.

The useful quirk is that **USDA and British dry-aged cuts sit on the same menu**, so you can taste the difference in one sitting rather than arguing about it.

Mayfair and the City are the sites to use.

![A grilled steak with a bowl of chips](../../assets/articles/best-steak-restaurants-london/goodman.jpg)

*A Russian-owned New York-style steakhouse, and the dry-aging is done in-house. Photo: [Ewan-M](https://www.flickr.com/photos/55935853@N00/4567552384), [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/).*

### CUT at 45 Park Lane, Mayfair

*££££ · 5 min from Hyde Park Corner*

Wolfgang Puck's first restaurant in Europe, and the most expensive steak on this page by some distance.

What justifies it, if anything does, is the range: **British native breeds, USDA Prime and Japanese wagyu on one menu**, cooked over a konro grill. It is the one place in London set up for a direct side-by-side comparison of three beef traditions.

### Lutyens Grill, City of London

*££££ · 1 min from Bank*

The old bank manager's office inside The Ned, which is exactly as clubbable as that sounds.

Rare-breed Longhorn and Hereford, and **44-day-aged prime rib carved at your table from a trolley** — the kind of service that had almost died out. It made the World's 101 Best Steak Restaurants list for 2026.

## Basque asador

A coal fire and an aged rib chop. Different meat, different technique, different meal.

### Lurra, Marylebone

*££££ · 3 min from Marble Arch*

**Aged Galician txuleta and whole turbot over coals.** The beef comes from old dairy cows rather than young cattle — darker, more mineral, and far more intensely flavoured than a British steak.

### Ibai, Farringdon

*££££ · 4 min from Barbican*

Fire-led Basque cooking and **some of the best beef in the country**, from the same team as Lurra.

### Sagardi, Shoreditch

*£££ · 9 min from Old Street*

London's third proper asador, and **much the easiest to get a table at** — Lurra and Ibai book out weeks ahead, this one often does not.

Same principle: **txuleton from old free-range cows**, cooked over a charcoal grill set in the middle of the dining room. Cheaper than the Marylebone pair, and the gap in quality is smaller than the gap in price.

## Butcher's counters

### Macellaio RC, South Kensington

*£££ · 5 min from South Kensington*

**A butcher's counter that became a restaurant** — Piedmontese Fassona beef hung in the window and cut to order in front of you.

### Brutto, Clerkenwell

*££ · Florentine*

Deliberately unfussy Florentine cooking, and **the bistecca is the order**. Cheaper than anywhere else doing a T-bone at this quality.

## Best value

* **Flat Iron** — £15 for a single 200g cut, no bookings.
* **Blacklock** — the All In at £28 a head, and a dry-aged steak list from £16. The cheapest serious meat on this page.
* **Mucci's**, Chelsea — every main £20, including a 200g sliced sirloin.
* **Hawksmoor's set lunch** — the same beef at a fraction of the dinner price.
* **Brutto's bistecca** — a T-bone for the table at trattoria prices.
* **Zelman Meats**, Knightsbridge — sliced picanha and sharing cuts from the Goodman group, priced well under its Mayfair parent. The Harvey Nichols room is the one to book.
* **The Guinea Grill's pie** — an award-winning steak and kidney pie in a Mayfair grill room, for a fraction of the beef price.

## What to know

* **Ask how it is aged.** Dry-aged and wet-aged are different products and every restaurant here will tell you which.
* **Basque txuleta is ordered rare and sliced** for the table. Asking for it well done defeats the point and the kitchen will tell you so.
* **Steak is priced by weight** at the asadors and butcher counters. Confirm the total before agreeing.
* **Rest matters.** The good places rest the meat properly, which is why it arrives later than you expect.
* **Service charge** of 12.5% is discretionary and standard.
