The hotels below run from about **£110 to £250 a night**, and most of the design hotels sit between £180 and £250.

Shoreditch is also loud. The streets around Great Eastern Street and Curtain Road are a nightlife district, and on a Friday people are still on them at three in the morning.

So this covers two questions: **which Shoreditch hotel**, and **where to stay near Shoreditch** if you would rather not pay the premium or be woken by it.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **[The Hoxton](hotel:the-hoxton-shoreditch)** is the best all-rounder at about £200. **[The Z Hotel](hotel:z-hotel-shoreditch)** is the cheapest at £110, but its lowest rate is a windowless room. **[art'otel Hoxton](hotel:artotel-london-hoxton)** has two rescued Banksys on the outside of the building, free to see. For the area without the price, **Stratford** is fifteen minutes away from about £100, and **the City of London** is often cheapest at weekends.

## Which part of Shoreditch

The name covers about a mile, and the two ends are the difference between a good night's sleep and a bad one.

**Great Eastern Street, Curtain Road and Old Street** are the nightlife core: bars, clubs, queues, and street noise until the early hours at weekends. Several of the hotels are here. Book a room off the street.

**Spitalfields and the Liverpool Street edge**, to the south, is Georgian, quieter, and a ten-minute walk from the Elizabeth line. Stay here for Shoreditch in the evening and sleep at night.

**Hoxton and the north end**, up towards Hoxton Square and Kingsland Road, is more residential, slightly cheaper, and a longer walk to a Tube station.

**There is no Tube station called Shoreditch.** Shoreditch High Street is Overground, Old Street is the Northern line, Liverpool Street is everything including the Elizabeth line.

### The Z Hotel Shoreditch — the cheapest, with a catch

*About £110 · Breakfast is charged separately · [check prices](hotel:z-hotel-shoreditch)*

A compact-room hotel on the Shoreditch edge of the City. Breakfast is bought in the Z Cafe: the free Z Member scheme adds evening cheese and wine and 10% off food and drink, but excludes breakfast, and only the pricier Z Club room grade has breakfast in the rate.

**The cheapest rate is an "Inside" room, which has no window at all**, the same format as the Covent Garden branch, and people book it by accident because the booking page does not lead with it. Pay up a grade unless you do not mind.

### art'otel London Hoxton — the tower

*About £230 · 26 storeys · Two Banksys outside · [check prices](hotel:artotel-london-hoxton)*

A tower wrapped in twisted black fins with the street artist D\*Face's work running through every room and public space. There is a public gallery, a 60-seat screening room, and an indoor pool non-residents can book.

**Two original Banksys are preserved on the outside of the building**, rescued from The Foundry which stood on this site, and free to look at from the street whether or not you stay.

**Breakfast is about £56 extra.** Solaya, the 25th-floor restaurant, is open Tuesday to Saturday evenings only, so check that if the view is why you booked.

## Where to stay near Shoreditch instead

If you have not settled on the area yet, our guide to the [best areas to stay in London](/articles/best-areas-to-stay-in-london/) sorts the whole city by what the trip is for, and covers whether there is a train home at one in the morning.

### Liverpool Street and Aldgate — ten minutes south

Quieter at night, with better transport: **the Elizabeth line puts you at Bond Street in about twelve minutes and Heathrow without changing.** You are a ten-minute walk from Shoreditch's bars.

The streets are commercial rather than residential, so they empty after dark. Liverpool Street has the Elizabeth line, Central, Circle, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan, plus National Rail out to Stansted. **[Batty Langley's](hotel:batty-langleys)** and **[Leman Locke](hotel:leman-locke)** both sit in this pocket, the first a Georgian townhouse and the second an aparthotel with kitchens.

### The City of London — cheapest at weekends

**The City empties out at weekends**, so hotels that charge business rates from Monday to Thursday discount on Friday and Saturday, the opposite of the pattern elsewhere in London.

**[Locke at Broken Wharf](hotel:locke-at-broken-wharf)** is about £190 midweek and worth checking against a Shoreditch room for the same Saturday. It is ten minutes away by bus or twenty on foot, on the river between Blackfriars and the Millennium Bridge, and the rooms are studios with kitchens.

### Whitechapel — east and cheaper

East of Shoreditch, cheaper, and on both the Elizabeth line and the District line: **Bond Street in about fifteen minutes, Heathrow without changing.**

It is a working East End high street rather than a going-out district. **Whitechapel Market runs six days a week** along the main road, and the Royal London Hospital dominates the middle of it. It is busy by day and quiet by night. The walk back from Shoreditch's bars is twenty-five minutes, so the night bus is the realistic way home.

## What you are staying for

Our [Shoreditch area guide](/articles/shoreditch-area-guide/) covers the markets, the bars and what is worth walking to. The [street art](/articles/london-street-art/) is free and the walls change often, so read the guide before you go looking.

## The noise question

Shoreditch is a nightlife district and a residential one at the same time, and the hotels sit in the middle of that. Three things help:

**Ask for a room away from the street**, by name, at booking, not check-in. The design hotels here have deep floorplates and the internal rooms are quieter.

**Ask for a high floor.** Street noise falls off fast above the fourth.

**Treat Friday and Saturday as different products.** A hotel on Great Eastern Street that is calm on a Tuesday is not calm on a Saturday.

If none of that appeals, stay at Liverpool Street or Spitalfields, close enough to walk back and far enough to sleep.

## Getting in and out

**Liverpool Street** is the main station: Elizabeth line, Central, Circle, Hammersmith & City, Metropolitan, and mainline trains to Stansted. Ten minutes on foot from most of Shoreditch.

**Old Street** is the Northern line, and closer to the north end.

**Shoreditch High Street** is Overground only, useful for Hackney, Dalston, Whitechapel and New Cross, and no use for the West End.

**For Heathrow**, walk to Liverpool Street and take the Elizabeth line, with no changes.
