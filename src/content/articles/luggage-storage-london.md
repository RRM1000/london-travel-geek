Nine London terminals have a staffed left-luggage desk. Four of the six airports do too. Two attractions on every first-time itinerary — the Tower of London and the Houses of Parliament — will turn a suitcase away at the door with nowhere on site to leave it. None of that is obvious until the queue is in front of you, so here is where an item actually goes, what it costs, and who is holding it, sourced to the operator that runs each desk.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **King's Cross, Euston, Paddington, Liverpool Street, Victoria, Waterloo, London Bridge, Charing Cross and St Pancras** all have a staffed desk, mostly run by **Excess Baggage Company**, from **£10 for up to 3 hours** and **£40 for up to 72 hours** per item. **Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted and Luton** have the same desks and the same tariff; **London City and Southend don't**. The **storage apps** — Stasher, Bounce, Radical Storage, LuggageHero and Nannybag — undercut every counter, from **£1.49 a bag a day**, booked online for a drop-off at a nearby shop or hotel. And two big-ticket sights will not take a suitcase in at all: the **Tower of London** refuses one outright, and the **Houses of Parliament** caps a bag at 60cm x 40cm, both with no cloakroom.

## Stations: nine desks, one operator, one tariff (Victoria excepted)

**King's Cross, Euston, Paddington, Liverpool Street, Victoria, Waterloo, London Bridge, Charing Cross and St Pancras International** all have a staffed left-luggage desk on the main concourse. **Excess Baggage Company** runs eight of the nine; at London Bridge, Network Rail lists the station's own Left Luggage unit.

**The published tariff is identical at every desk except Victoria's**, per item:

| Time band | Standard tariff | Victoria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 3 hours (6 at Victoria) | £10.00 | £10.00 |
| Up to 24 hours | £15.00 | £15.00 |
| Up to 48 hours | £27.50 | £25.00 |
| Up to 72 hours | £40.00 | £35.00 |
| After 72 hours | £12.50/day | £10.00/day |
| After 7 days | £10.00/day | £10.00/day |

Excess Baggage Company gives a **20% discount on 15 or more items**, and its own FAQ caps its liability for loss, theft or damage at **£200 per transaction**. It publishes no maximum bag size, and won't cover damage to anything over **32kg**, the EU's recommended safety limit.

**Desks open 07:00–23:00.** Network Rail lists Liverpool Street, London Bridge and Charing Cross as **open every day**; for King's Cross, Euston, Paddington, Waterloo and Victoria it lists **weekday hours only**, so ring the desk before a weekend drop-off (each number is on the station's Network Rail page). St Pancras International's desk is in The Circle, on the ground floor.

**Why a staffed counter rather than a bank of coin lockers?** Excess Baggage Company's own FAQ answers it directly: every bag is security-screened **as required under the National Rail Security Programme**, which is why the service is over the counter rather than self-service.

## Airports: four with a desk, two without

**Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted and Luton** all have a left-luggage desk, and all four are run by **Excess Baggage Company** on the **same tariff as the stations** (£10 up to 3 hours, up to £40 for 72). **London City and Southend have neither a desk nor a locker.**

| Airport | Location | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heathrow (all terminals) | Excess Baggage Company | 06:00–22:00 |
| Gatwick North Terminal | Level 1, before security, next to Costa Coffee | 04:00–22:00 |
| Gatwick South Terminal | Level 2, before security, behind check-in zone E | 04:00–22:00 |
| Stansted | Arrivals Hall | 06:00–22:00 |
| Luton | Check-in area | Daily, 04:00–22:00 |
| London City | — | No left-luggage service |
| Southend | — | No left-luggage service |

**Gatwick's own site is explicit about why there's no locker option**: "due to security reasons there are no lockers available for storing your luggage at Gatwick".

**At London City and Southend, book a storage app instead**, with a shop or hotel near the airport or at your first stop in town. Southend's terminal only opens three hours before each day's first departure.

## The storage apps: cheaper than any counter, if you can find one nearby

Five networks book a bag into a nearby shop, hotel or storage room rather than a dedicated counter: **Stasher, Bounce, Radical Storage, LuggageHero and Nannybag**. All five work the same way — pick a location and a date online, get a confirmation code, drop the bag and show the code, then collect it any time before closing with the same code. Most also take a walk-up booking on the day if a slot is free.

**Every figure here beats a station counter on price**, though a shopfront rather than a manned rail concourse is a different kind of trust — each network's own guarantee is the thing worth reading before a valuable bag goes into one. **Nannybag is now run inside the Bounce network**: its own site says partner shops "now sign up and manage bookings on Bounce," while Nannybag keeps its own branding and pricing for customers.

## Hotels, and the day-room alternative

**Most hotels will hold bags for their own guests before check-in or after check-out**, usually free and logged with nothing more than a name tag at the desk — it's worth asking even if a room won't be ready for hours. It's a courtesy rather than a guaranteed service, and it's for guests: a hotel you're not staying at has no obligation to take a stranger's suitcase.

**If the gap is long enough to want a bed rather than just a shelf**, a day-use hotel room is the better trade. About 245 London hotels sell one, from roughly £50 for a seven- or eight-hour window — a bed, a shower and somewhere to sit rather than a locker and a coffee shop. Our [day rooms guide](/articles/day-rooms-london/) covers which hotels sell one, when the windows start, and the ones open early enough for a 6am landing.

## Where you can't take a bag in

Four big venues publish bag limits that turn a suitcase away at the door.

| Venue | Bag limit | If you're carrying one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tower of London | No suitcases, large bags or rolling luggage at all | No cloakroom on site; Historic Royal Palaces' own FAQ points visitors to Stasher |
| Houses of Parliament | Nothing larger than 60cm x 40cm | No cloakroom; airport-style security on entry |
| Wembley Stadium | One small bag per person, no bigger than A4 (297mm × 210mm × 210mm) | Folding a bigger bag down doesn't count; no storage on site |
| The O2 | One bag per person, A4 or a standard handbag | Paid bag store outside the main entrance, £10 (free for American Express cardholders) |

**The Tower of London's own FAQ is unambiguous**: "Visitors must always keep their belongings with them. No left luggage or bag storage facilities are available. Suitcases, large bags, and any rolling luggage will not be permitted inside the Tower." **The Houses of Parliament's own site** is just as direct: "There are no cloakrooms, so you need to keep your belongings with you at all times. Luggage larger than 60cm x 40cm is not allowed." If you are carrying a case, book a storage app near the attraction rather than a desk at the station you arrived at.
