The London Eye stands 135 metres (443 feet) tall on the South Bank of the Thames. Its 32 climate-controlled glass capsules give 360-degree views of up to 40 kilometres (25 miles) on clear days.

About 3.5 million people ride it every year, so turning up without a booking risks a long queue on top of the £39 walk-up price.

## Is Fast Track worth it?

The upgrade is called Flexi Fast Track: it buys a dedicated priority lane on the boarding ramp and, unlike Standard tickets, lets you turn up any time on your chosen day rather than a fixed slot. It costs from £49 online or £54 at the gate, against Standard's from £29 online or £39 at the gate: a gap of roughly £20 online and £15 at the gate.

![The boarding platform of the London Eye on the South Bank with visitors queueing](../../assets/articles/london-eye-guide/london-eye-boarding-platform.jpg)

*The boarding platform beside County Hall. Photo: [Simiprof](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:London_Eye_boarding_platform.jpg), [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).*

The operator's own guidance on wait times: **20 to 30 minutes for Standard and 5 to 10 minutes for Fast Track on a quiet day, rising to an hour or more during peak periods.**

### When Fast Track is worth it
- **Weekends, school holidays and summer afternoons:** the Standard queue is most likely to reach the "hour or more" band, and Fast Track's separate lane keeps its advantage.
- **Sunset flights:** slots within an hour of dusk are the busiest of the day and the standard line bunches up.
- **Young children, or anyone who finds standing difficult:** the standard queue is an outdoor zigzag with no seating.

### When Fast Track isn't worth it
- **Midweek, term-time, off-peak months:** a 20-to-30-minute Standard queue against a 5-to-10-minute Fast Track one saves at most 25 minutes for £15 to £20.
- **Opening slots:** the first flights of the day have the least backlog.

## Timing a sunset flight

Book around sunset and one 30-minute revolution gives you daylight, golden hour and lit-up London.

![The London Eye illuminated in blue and purple at dusk reflected in the River Thames](../../assets/articles/london-eye-guide/london-eye-night-illuminated.jpg)

*The wheel illuminated at twilight. Photo: [David Iliff (Diliff)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:London_Eye_Twilight_April_2006.jpg), [CC BY 2.5](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.5/).*

### The 30-minute booking formula
Look up the sunset time for your date and book a slot **30 minutes before it**.

1. **Boarding (T minus 30 mins):** you join the queue and, after ticket scanning and security, board shortly after.
2. **Ascent in daylight (T minus 15 mins):** the capsule climbs over central London in evening light.
3. **Apex at golden hour (T to T plus 5 mins):** at 135 metres the sun dips to the horizon behind the Palace of Westminster.
4. **Descent into night (T plus 5 to T plus 15 mins):** the lamps on Westminster Bridge, the floodlights of Big Ben and the office towers of the City and Canary Wharf switch on.

### London sunset reference guide

| Month | Approximate Sunset | Target Booking Slot | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **January** | 16:00 – 16:45 | **15:30 – 16:15** | Earliest evening slots; combines well with winter South Bank lights. |
| **April** | 19:40 – 20:20 | **19:15 – 19:30** | British Summer Time begins; clear spring sunsets. |
| **July** | 21:00 – 21:20 | **20:30 – 20:45** | Long summer twilight; check late attraction closing hours. |
| **October** | 16:40 – 18:35 | **16:10 – 18:05** | Clocks go back on the last Sunday, which moves sunset about an hour earlier overnight. |

## Inside the capsule: what you see from the top

Each of the 32 capsules holds up to 25 passengers and is mounted on the outside of the rim rather than suspended beneath it, so the view is unobstructed in every direction.

![Passengers standing inside an air-conditioned glass capsule on the London Eye looking towards the River Thames](../../assets/articles/london-eye-guide/london-eye-capsule-interior.jpg)

*Inside a London Eye capsule. Photo: [LittleRoamingChief](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2024--15_September--London_Eye_Capsule_interior.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).*

Each capsule is air-conditioned and heated, with a wooden bench in the centre so the glass perimeter stays clear for photographs.

Rubber tyres drive the rim, so the ride is smooth even in strong river breezes.

![The Palace of Westminster and Big Ben viewed from high above on the London Eye with the River Thames below](../../assets/articles/london-eye-guide/view-from-london-eye-parliament.jpg)

*The Palace of Westminster, from the apex. Photo: [LittleRoamingChief](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2024--15_September--_Palace_of_Westminster_from_the_London_Eye.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).*

### Key landmarks from the 135-metre apex
- **Looking west and south-west:** Directly opposite are the Palace of Westminster and the Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben). Behind Parliament lie Westminster Abbey, Victoria Tower Gardens, St James's Park, Green Park and Buckingham Palace.
- **Looking north:** The curve of the Thames sweeps past Victoria Embankment towards Charing Cross railway bridge and Somerset House. On clear days the BT Tower in Fitzrovia stands out against Hampstead Heath.
- **Looking east:** Follow the river downstream past Waterloo Bridge towards St Paul's Cathedral and the high-rise cluster of the City of London (including The Gherkin, 22 Bishopsgate, and 20 Fenchurch Street). The jagged silhouette of The Shard rises directly across London Bridge.
- **Distance horizon:** on clear days with high atmospheric pressure, visibility extends 40 kilometres to Windsor Castle to the west and the North Downs to the south.

## Weather, fog and rescheduling

The wheel turns in rain, fog and light snow, closing only for severe storms or sustained gale-force winds.

### Low cloud and mist
The apex is 135 metres up. On rainy days London's cloud ceiling regularly drops below 100 metres, putting the upper third of your rotation inside dense cloud with no view across the river.

### Refunds and rescheduling
Tickets bought direct from londoneye.com are non-refundable and non-transferable. A Standard or Fast Track ticket can be rescheduled for free up to one hour before your arrival slot, through the operator's own [booking portal](https://www.londoneye.com/plan-your-visit/information-and-support/manage-your-booking/). A multi-attraction ticket, such as the Tussauds combo or the five-attraction bundle, can be rescheduled free up to three times, any time up to 24 hours before arrival.

### If you'd rather cancel than reschedule
A <a href="https://www.getyourguide.com/activity/-t170451?partner_id=WWP7I0R&amp;cmp=london-eye-guide" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" data-affiliate="gyg">London Eye timed entry ticket on GetYourGuide</a> matches official advance pricing and adds free cancellation up to 24 hours before your slot.

To plan around the forecast:
1. **Check the 48-hour forecast.** The Met Office two days out shows whether the apex will be in cloud.
2. **Move the slot rather than gamble on it.** If the outlook is poor, reschedule to a clearer time up to an hour before arrival.
3. **In spring and autumn, book 24 to 48 hours out** so you can read the conditions before you commit.

## Getting there, security and luggage

### Nearest stations and walking routes
- **Waterloo Station (Bakerloo, Jubilee, Northern, Waterloo & City lines + South Western Railway):** the most direct route. Take the South Bank exit; it is five minutes through York Road or Jubilee Gardens to the riverside.
- **Westminster Station (Circle, District and Jubilee lines):** exit onto Bridge Street and cross Westminster Bridge, seven minutes, with the wheel across the river ahead of you.
- **Charing Cross / Embankment Stations (Bakerloo, Northern, District, Circle lines):** cross the Golden Jubilee footbridge beside Hungerford Bridge, ten minutes, ending in Jubilee Gardens.

### Security screening
Every passenger goes through security screening in the marquee before the boarding ramp: bags are inspected, with walkthrough metal detectors and handheld wands.

### Luggage and storage
Large suitcases and bulky bags can't ride the capsule; security will ask you to check them in. The ticket hall's storage desk charges **£5 per item**, collected by 7pm the same day, or **£25** if you leave something overnight. Buggies and pushchairs can be folded into the capsule or left at the same desk for free, also collected by 7pm.

To avoid carrying a case to County Hall, use the staffed **Excess Baggage Company** counter on the main concourse of Waterloo station (open daily 07:00 to 23:00) is a five-minute walk away. See our [luggage storage in London guide](/articles/luggage-storage-london/) for pricing and nearby app-based drop points.

### Wheelchair and accessibility access
The London Eye is fully step-free:
- Boarding ramps have gentle gradients, and staff slow or halt the wheel to position the ramp against the capsule threshold.
- Only **two wheelchair users are permitted per capsule**, and a maximum of **eight wheelchairs** may be on the wheel at any one time.
- Wheelchair slots must be booked in advance online to secure an allocated time. Carers receive a complimentary companion ticket when booking.
