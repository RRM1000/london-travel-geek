The O2 calls itself the world's busiest entertainment arena, and getting most of the crowd there and home again runs through one Tube line, one small peninsula, and a couple of scenic-looking alternatives that turn out to be for arriving, not leaving.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** Come by tube — **North Greenwich** is the only station, on the **Jubilee line** only, about **five minutes'** walk from the doors. It's a **single-line dependency**: unlike Wembley's three stations, there's no second tube line to switch to if the Jubilee is disrupted. If you drive, book ahead: The O2's own **Car Park 1** is **£29–£35** pre-booked or **£40** on the day, through JustPark — there's **no residents-only event-day zone** the way Brent runs for Wembley, but the venue's own site is currently warning of **serious post-event traffic** from nearby roadworks. The **cable car and the river boat are for arriving, not leaving**: both stop running before most evening shows end (cable car by **21:00–23:00** depending on the day, last boat **21:45–22:22**). Going home on a **Friday or Saturday**, the **Jubilee Night Tube runs all night**; any other night the last train leaves to the ordinary timetable. On a big night a Greenwich hotel can **triple or quadruple** in price; central London barely moves.

## Getting there by tube — and why one line matters

| Station | Line | Zone | Walk to The O2 | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **North Greenwich** | **Jubilee only** | 2/3 | **About 5 minutes** | It's the only station — there is no second or third option |

North Greenwich was built as part of the Jubilee Line Extension, and the station's ticket hall sits right against The O2's own forecourt: come up the escalators and the dome is in front of you. That's the good news. The catch is the one thing every other major London venue guide has to caveat and this one has to lead with: **The O2 depends on a single Underground line**. Wembley has three stations across two lines (Jubilee, Metropolitan, Chiltern, plus the Bakerloo and Overground at Wembley Central); The O2 has one station on one line. If the Jubilee is suspended or severely delayed on a show night, there is no second tube line to switch to — only the bus, the river, the cable car, or a much longer walk.

That single-line dependency is also why TfL takes crowd management at this station seriously enough to have a standing procedure for it. In response to a Freedom of Information request, TfL confirmed that after O2 events, station staff **"reduce flow and hold event crowds"** outside the station rather than let the ticket hall flood, working **"with the help of O2 security and the police,"** under **"a local event plan"** used to brief the team on how each event is managed. A separate 2026 FOI response confirms this is an established, ongoing practice — staff have logged crowd-control measures through a dedicated app since June 2024, before which it was an electronic and then a paper log book. In practice, this means: on a big night, expect to be held on the concourse or outside the gate line for a few minutes rather than walking straight onto a train, and expect staff and sometimes police presence managing that queue.

North Greenwich has good accessible features built in from that 1999 rebuild: low-level ticket machines, wheelchair-accessible gates (with a dedicated gate to the right of the barriers), an induction loop, step-free access to every platform via lifts, and step-free bus stops.

### Buses and the night bus

Routes **108, 129, 132, 161, 188, 422 and 486** all stop at North Greenwich Bus Station, immediately outside the tube entrance and The O2's doors. The **188 to Waterloo runs 24 hours a day** — the standing fallback if the Jubilee line is down or it's a weeknight and the Night Tube isn't running. There's also a dedicated night route, the **N472**, which starts its journey at North Greenwich Station and runs via Woolwich and Thamesmead to Abbey Wood.

## Driving: no residents' ban, but book ahead and expect delays

Unlike Wembley, Greenwich doesn't run anything resembling Brent's event-day protective parking scheme. There's no borough-wide zone that shuts out visitor parking specifically because there's a show on, no special permit-only hours that only apply on event days, and no equivalent of the £130 penalty for parking in the wrong place on a match night. The Royal Borough of Greenwich's parking rules — its ordinary controlled parking zones and resident permits — apply on a concert night exactly as they do on any other night, no more and no less.

What The O2's own site does warn about, in language that reads like a live operational notice rather than boilerplate, is traffic:

> ⚠️ **Roadworks are affecting the drive in.** The O2's own parking page currently states: "Please note major roadworks in the surrounding area are significantly affecting traffic flow around The O2. Expect long delays at peak times, particularly post event." That's a warning from the venue itself, not a general assumption — build in extra time both ways if you're driving, and especially for the journey home.

The other practical constraint is capacity rather than restriction: Car Park 1, the one bookable for arena and indigo events, is a single car park serving a very large room. Booking ahead is the only way to guarantee a space — turning up and paying on the day depends on what's left.

## Where to actually park

### The O2's own car parks

All event parking is booked through **[JustPark](https://www.theo2.co.uk/visit-us/parking)**, and pre-booking closes 24 hours before the event.

| | Car | Minibus | Coach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-booked, early bird** | **from £29.39** | £59.35 | £59.35 |
| **Pre-booked, standard** | **£35.28** | £59.35 | £59.35 |
| **Pay on the day** (if space allows) | **£40** | £65 | £65 |
| **Valet** (arena events only) | £75 pre-booked / £85 on the day | — | — |

The early-bird car rate is only available until three weeks before the event, and valet parking opens two hours before doors, with the car ready again as soon as the show finishes.

**Not coming to an event?** Car parks 2, 3 and 4 serve Outlet Shopping, Cineworld and Up at The O2 on ordinary hourly tariffs — from **£7** for up to two hours up to **£52** for a full day (18–24 hours). Car Park 2 is open 24/7. Spend £35 or more at Outlet Shopping and you get four hours free in car parks 2, 3 or 4; Cineworld customers get four hours free in car parks 2 and 3, validated at the parking office in Car Park 1.

Blue Badge spaces are available in all four of The O2's car parks (the badge holder must be travelling in the car), and there are several electric vehicle charging points on the BP Pulse network. The postcode for route planners is **SE10 0DX**.

### The other car park: North Greenwich station itself

Because there's only one station, there's no Wembley-style "park a stop out" option — but there is a genuinely separate, cheaper alternative right next to The O2's own car parks: the **North Greenwich Station Car Park**, on TfL land but run by Saba.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spaces** | 519 (509 standard, 6 motorcycle, 4 EV charging bays reserved for taxis) |
| **Arrive before 18:00** | **£18** for the day |
| **Arrive after 18:00** | **£27.50** for the day |
| **Height limit** | 1.9m |
| **Booking** | None — first come, first served, cashless (PayByPhone or machine only) |

It's not bookable in advance, so it isn't a guaranteed space, but on price alone it beats paying on the day at Car Park 1 — as long as you don't need to arrive after 6pm, since the after-6pm rate applies to almost every evening show.

### Free Blue Badge parking at Monro Way

A short walk from the station car park, on **Monro Way (SE10 0EJ)**, is a separate 19-space car park reserved entirely for Blue Badge holders: **free, with no time limit**, seven days a week, no booking and no payment machine at all. It's a better option than The O2's own paid accessible bays if you don't need to book ahead or want the walkway link to a specific car park.

## The cable car and the river: fine for arriving, rarely useful for leaving

### The cable car

The gondola across the river to the Royal Docks is still sold and signed in places as the **IFS Cloud Cable Car**, though TfL's own site now labels it simply the **London Cable Car** across its station page, hub page and ticket pages — a shift worth knowing if you're searching for it. Either way, it's the same service: a single crossing of the Thames from Greenwich Peninsula Terminal (a couple of minutes from North Greenwich station) to the Royal Docks Terminal, taking up to **10 minutes**, with cars every 30 seconds.

| Day | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| Monday–Thursday | 08:00–21:00 |
| Friday | 09:00–22:00 |
| Saturday | 09:00–23:00 |
| Sunday and bank holidays | 09:00–21:00 |

Booked online, a one-way adult ticket starts at **£7** and a round trip at **£13.50** (children roughly half price); contactless and Oyster pay-as-you-go are also accepted. **O2 ticket holders get 50% off a walk-up fare** on the day of their event.

> ⚠️ **It closes before most shows end.** Look at the hours again: Sunday to Thursday it shuts at 21:00, and even on Saturday — its latest night — it's closed by 23:00. A typical evening concert, comedy show or fight card finishes well inside that window on most nights of the week, which makes the cable car a good way to arrive early for a show with a river or Docklands detour, but not a realistic way to get home afterwards. It's step-free at both terminals, with lifts and boarding staff, though only single battery-powered wheelchairs up to 80cm wide can travel.

### The river

**Uber Boat by Thames Clippers** calls at North Greenwich Pier, on the East zone of the river network (you'll need a Central & East ticket to reach central London piers like Embankment or Westminster). A single fare bought online is **£13.50**; pay as you go by contactless or Oyster is **£13.70 at peak times** (which, on this stretch, means practically every evening — off-peak on the river is weekdays before 09:27 only, and every weekend and bank holiday counts as peak all day). The crossing to Blackfriars takes about **44 minutes**.

The frequency is good in the evening — sailings roughly every 20 minutes — but the **schedule itself stops running before most events finish**. Checked against the operator's own published timetable: the last westbound sailing towards central London leaves North Greenwich Pier at **21:45 on a weekday** and **22:22 at weekends**. A matinee or an early-finishing show can catch it; a normal 19:30 or 20:00 doors show usually can't. The pier is step-free and wheelchair accessible, with accessible toilets on the larger boats.

## Getting home

### Crowd management at the station

As covered above, TfL's own account of how it manages North Greenwich after events is to **hold and meter the crowd outside the station** rather than let everyone flood the ticket hall at once, working with The O2's own security team and the police under a standing local event plan. There's no published one-way system with named entrances the way Wembley Park has for Olympic Way and Bridge Road — North Greenwich has one main approach, not several — but do expect staff-managed queuing on the concourse for a big finish, and factor that into how long "getting to the platform" actually takes.

### The Night Tube changes the evening

The Jubilee is one of six Night Tube lines — alongside the Central, Northern, Piccadilly, Victoria and Windrush — confirmed directly by TfL as running **"on Friday and Saturday nights."** On those two nights, there's no last train to worry about: trains run all night, roughly every ten minutes, and you can simply wait out the worst of the crowd before walking onto a near-empty platform.

### Last trains, any other night

Outside the Night Tube, the last train leaves North Greenwich on the ordinary daily timetable — TfL does not extend it for an O2 event the way Chiltern Railways adds late trains for Wembley. Checked live against TfL's own station page on a Sunday when Evanescence was playing the arena:

| Direction | Last train from North Greenwich |
| --- | --- |
| **Towards Stanmore** (westbound) | **23:45** |
| **Towards Stratford** (eastbound) | **00:16** |

If your show is scheduled to finish later than that on a Sunday-to-Thursday night, plan on a bus, a taxi, or waiting it out — not a later train.

### Buses, taxis and the night bus

The **188 runs 24 hours** to and from Waterloo, and the **N472** night route starts at North Greenwich station and runs through Woolwich to Abbey Wood — both useful once the Jubilee has stopped for the night. There's a taxi rank at North Greenwich Bus Station, and The O2's own car parks stay open for you to return to once the crowd has cleared, though — as above — check the specific closing arrangements for your event, since only Car Park 2 is confirmed as open 24/7.

## Where to stay, and why the peninsula is the trap on a big night

Checked live on the hotels' own booking engines, on the day this article was written, the two hotels closest to The O2 both showed the same pattern Wembley's Novotel does: cheap most nights, expensive on a big one.

**The O2 arena was hosting Evanescence** on the night these prices were sampled — a normal single-headliner arena show, not an unusually large one.

| | Event night (Evanescence, Sun 13 Sep) | Ordinary Sunday, one week later (20 Sep) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **InterContinental London – The O2** (Classic Room) | **£465** | **£261** |
| **Holiday Inn Express London Greenwich** (Double Standard, breakfast included) | **£276** | **£68** |

*Same room type, one night, two adults, prices including taxes and fees, sampled on IHG's own booking engine on 13 September 2026.*

The swing gets bigger, not smaller, for a genuinely major event: over the same booking engine, the InterContinental's Classic Room price for the **Laver Cup weekend (25–26 September)**, a three-day international tennis event held at the arena, came out at **£610–£615** a night.

- **For a big night, book early or expect to pay a multiple of the ordinary rate.**
- **Or shift your stay to the night after** and travel home the next day, the way Wembley's article recommends for match nights.
- The gap is proportionally larger at the budget end: the InterContinental's event-night premium was roughly **1.8×** its ordinary rate; the Holiday Inn Express's was roughly **4×**.

### The hotels at the peninsula

**[InterContinental London – The O2](hotel:intercontinental-the-o2)** — 493 rooms and suites on Waterview Drive, with a spa (17-metre pool, jacuzzi, sauna), three restaurants and two bars. A private walkway connects the hotel directly to The O2, so the "commute" on a show night is a corridor. On-site parking is £52 a day. Check-in from 3:00pm, check-out by noon.

**[Holiday Inn Express London Greenwich](hotel:holiday-inn-express-greenwich-peninsula)** — the budget option on Bugsby's Way, about 15 minutes' walk from The O2 and the station, with breakfast included in the rate (which the InterContinental charges extra for). On-site parking is £35 a day.

To stay central and travel out instead, our [guide to the best areas to stay in London](/articles/best-areas-to-stay-in-london/) covers the Jubilee line neighbourhoods that put you a single change from North Greenwich.

## The hours before doors

**Up at The O2** is the obvious pre-show activity: a guided, 90-minute climb over the top of the dome's fabric roof, reaching 52 metres above ground with a 360-degree view of London — up to 15 miles on a clear day. Pricing runs from **£37 for a weekday daytime climb** up to **£44 for a weekend sunset climb**, with twilight and sunset slots priced in between. Book well ahead of a big event, since a climb slot and a doors time rarely both fit comfortably into the same evening.

The **Design District**, a cluster of 16 low-rise studio buildings by eight different architects, sits opposite the arena, about a minute from the station — worth a look if you want something other than the retail-heavy Entertainment District, and it has its own canteen for a quieter meal before a show.

**Outlet Shopping at The O2** wraps around the dome with over 60 discount and outlet stores, and keeps its own hours separate from the arena's — check its site directly if shopping is part of the plan, since it doesn't simply mirror event timings.

## Eating and drinking in the Entertainment District

Unlike Boxpark at Wembley, The O2's Entertainment District doesn't close to the public or convert to a ticketed fan zone on an event day — it's part of the venue's own estate rather than a separate leased business, and it runs on its ordinary public hours regardless of what's on in the arena that night.

The complex's general hours are **10:00–22:00 daily**, though individual restaurants, bars and leisure venues set their own, often later, times. The current line-up, direct from The O2's own listing, runs to more than 40 places, including **Gordon Ramsay Street Burger, Nando's, Wagamama**-style chains, **Five Guys, KFC, German Doner Kebab, Gaucho, Haidilao Hot Pot, TGI Fridays, PizzaExpress, Zizzi, Las Iguanas** and **Frankie & Benny's**, plus dedicated bars including **All Bar One** and **Slug & Lettuce**.

One reliably late option: **Wetherspoon's pub here, The Stargazer**, is open **10am to 12:15am daily**, with food served **10am to 11pm** — the closest thing on the peninsula to a guaranteed late kitchen and a late pint after a show.

Inside the arena itself, bars include the **American Express Lounge, Aperol Spritz Bar, London Essence Cocktail Bar, O2 Bluebar, O2 Blueroom, Smirnoff Bar** and **The Budweiser Bar**. Both The O2 arena and indigo at The O2 are **cashless**.

## The bag problem

If your checkout is at noon and doors are hours later, you can't carry a bag around all day.

| | The O2 arena | indigo at The O2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bag limit** | One bag per person, **no bigger than A4** or a standard handbag | Same policy applies |
| **Cloakroom** | **None** | **Yes** — a cloakroom is available |
| **Paid storage** | **£10 per bag**, just outside the main entrance | — |

Backpacks, travel cases, laptop bags, camera bags and large tote bags aren't permitted inside the arena at all — folding one down smaller doesn't count. **American Express cardholders get free bag drop** through a dedicated Amex lane. Tote bags bought at The O2's own merchandise stands are allowed in, subject to search.

> ⚠️ **Check the storage closing time for your show.** The O2's own pages don't fully agree with each other: its bag-policy and security pages both say storage "closes 1 hour after" the show ends, while its coming-to-an-event page says 30 minutes after. Treat 30 minutes as the safer number to plan around and collect your bag promptly.

The prohibited items list is broader than a simple bag-size rule: no weapons or bladed items, no fireworks, flagpoles or large inflatables, no large umbrellas, no glass or hard drinks bottles (an empty collapsible bottle is fine), no flammable liquids, no laser pens, no professional camera with a detachable lens, no illegal drugs or alcohol brought in, and no animals other than service and assistance dogs.

## Step-free access, Blue Badge parking and accessible booking

**Getting there:** North Greenwich station has low-level ticket machines, wheelchair-accessible gates, an induction loop, step-free access to every platform via lifts, and step-free bus stops. The cable car terminals are step-free with lifts and boarding staff, though only single battery-powered wheelchairs up to 80cm wide can travel on it. North Greenwich Pier is step-free and wheelchair accessible, with accessible toilets on the larger boats.

**Parking:** Blue Badge bays are available in all of The O2's own car parks (from about £21 pre-booked), or **free with no time limit** at the dedicated 19-space Blue Badge car park on Monro Way. An accessible drop-off point for cars and coaches sits about 150m from the main entrance, clearly signposted.

**Inside the venue:** the main concourse is level and step-free, with accessible lifts across all levels. Accessible seating is available on every level — aisle seats, minimal steps nearby, no obstruction, and close to accessible toilets — and viewing platforms are offered for guests who find standing difficult at events where the crowd tends to stand. Accessible toilets are unisex, on every level, and use a RADAR key; there's also a Changing Places unit on Level 1. If you bring your own wheelchair, it can be stored at the Customer Service Desk near block 101. Assistance dogs can stay with you in wheelchair bays — email **access@theo2.co.uk** ahead of your visit to let the team know. Assistive Listening Devices are available at the Level 1 Customer Service desk (a form of ID is held as a deposit while you use one).

**Getting a wheelchair to you:** call **0208 463 2211** once doors open on the night, and a wheelchair will be brought to the main entrance — you'll need your own companion to push it. That line is only staffed on event nights.

**Booking accessible tickets:** The O2 uses the **Nimbus Disability Access Card** system. A Personal Assistant ticket requires a valid Access Card carrying the "+1" symbol, booked at the same time as your own ticket; for more than one PA, call the dedicated access line on **020 8463 3359**. BSL interpreting is available through SignVideo, **10am–2pm, Monday to Friday** (Typetext 18001 020 8463 3359).

## Small print that catches people out

- **Postcodes:** The O2 itself and its car parks, **SE10 0DX**; North Greenwich station and its car park, **SE10 0PH**; the Blue Badge car park on Monro Way, **SE10 0EJ**.
- **Pre-booked parking closes 24 hours before the event** — after that, you're paying the on-the-day rate if space allows.
- **The cable car and the river boat are arrival options on a normal evening, not departure options after a show** — both stop running well before most doors-to-curfew times.
- **Both The O2 arena and indigo at The O2 are cashless.**
- **Event doors, show times and curfews are published per event, not as a house rule** — check the specific listing for your date rather than assuming a standard time.
- **Outlet Shopping at The O2 keeps its own hours**, separate from the arena and from indigo.
