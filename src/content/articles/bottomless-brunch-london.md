A bottomless brunch is a fixed price for a food dish plus unlimited drinks inside a set window, and in London that window is ninety minutes far more often than two hours. The two things worth checking before you book are both routinely buried: **when the clock starts, and which drinks the word "bottomless" actually covers.** At Flight Club it covers the pizza and not the drinks. At Megan's it covers the drinks and not the food. Everything below was read off each venue's own website or booking page and **checked on 3 September 2026**.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Big Easy** is the best value in London — around £41.50 buys two hours of unlimited drinks *and* unlimited barbecue. **Las Iguanas** at £39.95 gives you two full hours and a main course. **The Breakfast Club's Mayor's Boozy Brunch** at £45 is the pick for cocktails. **No 32 The Old Town** is the best in Clapham at £39. And **Megan's £25 headline is drinks only** — a main meal is compulsory on top.

> ⚠️ **Ninety minutes rarely means ninety minutes of drinking.** Revolution and Big Easy both stop taking drinks orders fifteen minutes before the end. All Bar One, Revolution and BrewDog all start the clock at your booking or order time, not when you settle in. Assume you have about three-quarters of the advertised window.

## The best bottomless brunches in London

**Big Easy** is the clearest value in the city and it is not close. The weekend Boozy Brunch is £35.50 for **unlimited** home-smoked barbecue — ribs, chicken, pulled pork, cornbread, beans and slaw — with one drink included, and £5 to £6 more upgrades that drink to unlimited Prosecco, beer, frozen mojito or wine for two hours. Nothing else on this page gives you unlimited food and unlimited drink for the same money. It runs Saturday, Sunday **and Monday** until 4pm, with a maximum table of twelve and a last seating at 3.45pm. Two honest caveats: you pick one drink and stay with it, no mixing, and drinks orders stop fifteen minutes before your two hours are up. Canary Wharf, Covent Garden and Chelsea.

**Las Iguanas** wins on time. Almost every rival gives you ninety minutes; Las Iguanas gives two hours and says so pointedly on its own page. For £39.95 you get a main dish and a choice of twenty-five drinks including cocktails, beer, fizz and spirits, which at £0.33 a minute is better than several venues charging less. It is pre-booking only with no walk-ins, and the whole table has to take part. **The one trap is location-specific**: bottomless brunch is not available at what the company calls event locations on event days, and that list includes the Royal Festival Hall on the South Bank, The O2 and Wembley. Check the date before you book the South Bank one.

**The Breakfast Club's Mayor's Boozy Brunch** is the pick if you want cocktails rather than fizz. £45 buys two hours at The Mayor of Scaredy Cat Town, the speakeasy behind the Spitalfields branch, with bottomless fizz, beer **and** cocktails plus a brunch dish each, every Saturday. The group's cheaper Soho version at Backseat Becky is £35 for the same two hours but drops the cocktails for fizz, mimosas and beer. At £0.38 and £0.29 a minute respectively, both undercut most ninety-minute cocktail brunches on price per minute while giving you a third more time.

**No 32 The Old Town** is the best thing in Clapham and the clearest menu structure anywhere here. Ninety minutes, Saturday and Sunday, 11am to 4pm, groups of any size, and four tiers that are genuinely different rather than cosmetic: **£39** for Prosecco, Pravha and mimosas, **£49** adding five cocktails, **£59** adding Whispering Angel and **£79** adding Veuve Clicquot. Every tier includes a shared hummus starter and your choice of brunch dish, which is more food than most £39 packages provide.

**Sticks'n'Sushi** is the one to book when the food matters more than the drinking. £70 for ninety minutes is the most expensive per minute on this page at £0.78, but it buys a real sharing menu — beef tataki, karaage, gyoza, tuna tartare, maki, nigiri, grilled sticks and a mango dessert — rather than a burger and chips. Every Saturday, noon to 5pm, last sitting 3.30pm, and it takes bookings from one person up to twelve, which makes it the rare bottomless brunch that works for a couple. **Budget for the 12.5% service charge on top**, which takes £70 to £78.75; it is the only venue here that publishes its service charge on the brunch page at all.

**Cottons** does the best-value two-course brunch outside the chains. £34.50 buys ninety minutes of bottomless rum punch, Prosecco, Cottons Lager or Caribbean Pimm's alongside a starter and a main, to a reggae soundtrack, in Camden, Shoreditch, Notting Hill or Vauxhall. One thing to know: **the whole £34.50 is taken at the time of booking**, not as a deposit. And the site's own marketing copy elsewhere promises "up to 2 hours" while the booking block says ninety minutes — book on the ninety.

**Revolution** is the most transparent operator in London, which is a strange thing to praise a chain bar for, but its terms page tells you everything the others hide. Two hours from £35 Sunday to Friday and £37.50 on Saturday, with unlimited Prosecco, mimosas, pints of Pravha, Kopparberg and a long cocktail list, plus a brunch dish. It then states in writing that the clock starts when your order is taken, that your current drink must be finished before the next is ordered, and that last orders come fifteen minutes before the end. **Everyone does this; Revolution is one of the few that admits it.**

**Flight Club** deserves a place here for the opposite reason. £33 for two hours across Bloomsbury, Islington, Shoreditch and Victoria, with bottomless sharing pizza and an hour of darts, is a good afternoon. But **the drinks are not bottomless at all**: each guest picks a bottle of Prosecco, four bottles of beer, a bottle of 0% Prosecco or four 0% beers. It is a fixed allocation. If that is what you want it is well priced; if you booked expecting free-flowing drinks it is not.

## Cheap bottomless brunches, under £35

The cheapest headline numbers in London are almost all drinks-only packages, and the food you are obliged to buy alongside them is what closes the gap.

**Megan's Classic at £25 for ninety minutes** is the most-advertised cheap option in the city and the most misread. The terms say it plainly: bottomless is for drinks only and must be paired with a main meal. Add a main and you are realistically at £40. What you do get is unlimited Prosecco, mimosas, bellinis, spritzes and draught beer, served all day every day rather than in weekend sittings, across Clapham, Islington, Notting Hill and a dozen more.

**The Little Yellow Door in Notting Hill runs the same model more cheaply** — buy any brunch dish and add ninety minutes of bottomless drinks for £23.50, every Saturday. At £0.26 a minute for the drinks alone it is the lowest number here. For groups of ten or more it switches to a family-style set menu with drinks included at £38.50 a head, which is the version worth booking for a birthday.

**Slug & Lettuce is the best genuinely cheap all-in deal**: from £33.95 for **two hours** with a brunch dish included, seven days a week, at Waterloo, Clapham Junction, Islington and Fulham. At £0.28 a minute with food it beats most things costing more. A fizz upgrade is £5. Prices do vary by site and time of day, so check the branch.

**Two more worth knowing.** Las Iguanas runs a **student session at £24.95** for ninety minutes with a main dish. And **Mr Bao in Peckham prices its bottomless drinks by the hour at £24** — prosecco, mimosas, spicy Bloody Marys and Bloody Marias — which is the only per-hour model found in London and means you extend rather than get cut off. Food is separate.

**BrewDog sells ninety minutes of bottomless beer for £19.95** in its England bars, the lowest price of anything here. It comes with no food, it is limited to the Headliner range, and it is sold as a bank-holiday promotion rather than a standing weekly brunch — so treat it as an occasional bargain rather than something to plan a Saturday around.

## Best for a party, a hen do or a birthday

If the brunch is one part of a bigger day, the [hen do](/articles/hen-do-london/) and [stag do](/articles/stag-do-london/) guides price the activities either side of it, and list the bars that will not take a group at all.

**Ballie Ballerson** in Shoreditch is the biggest party of the lot, and priced accordingly: ninety minutes of prosecco, passion fruit martinis, picantes, beer and seltzers on tap, plus **120 minutes of unlimited pizza** and full ball-pit access. £45 on Thursday and Sunday, £49 on Friday, £65 on Saturday. Its **Sisterhood drag brunch runs at 3pm on Saturdays** with a resident drag queen, at £65 with pizza or **£35 for drinks only**. Note the payment structure: fifty per cent deposit at booking, the balance a week before, when you also confirm numbers.

**BOXPARK Shoreditch** is the best illustration on this page of the two clocks a bottomless brunch runs on. From £42.67 you get **four hours of entertainment** and a meal from a street-food trader — but **ninety minutes of bottomless** cocktails and bubbles. The venue keeps you all afternoon; the bar does not.

**The Piano Works** is the one for a group that wants to sing. £50 for one course or £55 for two, ninety minutes of bottomless drinks and **two hours of a live request band**, every Saturday at Farringdon and the West End. If you only want the room and not the package, there is a £10 entry-only ticket with no table.

**Bunga Bunga** in Covent Garden is now a nineties-themed karaoke bar, and its £52 Saturday and Sunday package is priced as an event rather than a brunch: a live karaoke host, a small plate, a salad and a twelve-inch pizza per two guests, ninety minutes of bottomless beers and cocktails, and a DJ. **A non-bottomless alternative at £26** gets you one drink and a whole pizza, which is the sensible option for anyone in the group who is not drinking.

**Barrio** runs four sittings a day at noon, 2pm, 4pm and 6pm across Shoreditch, Soho, Covent Garden, Angel and Brixton, with themed weekends from Broadway musicals to drag. £30 Sunday to Thursday and at Saturday noon, £37.50 for Friday and the later Saturday slots. Food arrives as a birdcage between two, so a six is served three. Deposits are low with the balance paid on the day, which is the friendliest structure here for a group whose numbers might move.

**Swingers** bundles the drinking with something to do: £50 for ninety minutes of bottomless drinks, street food, a reserved area and thirty minutes of unlimited retro arcade play at the West End and City sites. For a group that would otherwise run out of conversation at the sixty-minute mark, that is money well spent — and it pairs naturally with our guide to [competitive socialising in London](/articles/competitive-socialising-london/).

**Queen of Hoxton** runs a Drag & Waffle bottomless brunch every Saturday over two sittings, with bottomless fizz, waffles and drag bingo. It is one of the best-regarded party brunches in east London, though the price is only shown once you reach the ticket page.

## How to read a bottomless brunch offer

Six things decide whether a deal is what it looks like.

**When the clock starts.** All Bar One's terms say the ninety minutes commences from the time of ordering. Revolution's say the two hours run from when your order is taken. BrewDog's say the ninety minutes runs from the start of your booking, which means a late arrival is your loss. **Turn up on time and order immediately.**

**When it really stops.** Revolution takes last orders fifteen minutes before the end. Big Easy requires drinks orders fifteen minutes before the two-hour table limit. A stated two hours is 105 minutes of ordering.

**One drink at a time.** All Bar One limits you to one drink per person at a time. Revolution requires your drink to be finished before the next is ordered. This is the single biggest brake on a bottomless brunch — **it makes the pace of service, not the price, the thing that determines value.** It is also the root of the most common complaint in recent reviews across every venue here: slow top-ups once the room fills, with waits of half an hour or more for a first drink reported at the busiest sittings. The venues rated best on this are consistently the ones where reviewers name a specific server.

**Whether food is included.** Megan's, The Little Yellow Door and Mr Bao all sell drinks-only packages with food charged separately. Barrio and Dirty Martini include food, but as one sharing board between two people. Flight Club inverts it entirely and makes the pizza bottomless while capping the drinks.

**Who else has to join in.** Megan's, Turtle Bay, Las Iguanas and Revolution all require the whole table to take the same package. Megan's is the one operator that publishes an exception, saying it makes allowances for groups with pregnant or dry members. Ask, rather than assuming.

**What gets added at the end.** Only Sticks'n'Sushi publishes its service charge on the brunch page — 12.5%, taking £70 to £78.75. Most London venues add 12.5% to 15% and mention it nowhere until the bill. **Add an eighth to every number on this page and you will not be surprised.**

Two structural tips. Book the **first sitting of the day** — the bar is fully staffed, the room is not yet three deep, and top-ups arrive. And check the deposit model before you commit: Revolution wants £5 a head, Ballie Ballerson wants fifty per cent up front, Cottons wants the entire £34.50, and Turtle Bay requires card validation for any booking of five or more.
