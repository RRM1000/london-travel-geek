Mobile data makes modern travel easier: it powers maps, tickets, hotel messages, translation, transport apps and banking alerts. You can use your home network's roaming plan, install a travel eSIM, or buy a physical SIM from a local network.

> **The short version:** check your existing roaming allowance first. If it is expensive, an **eSIM** is normally the easiest option for a compatible unlocked phone. A **local physical SIM** may provide better value, a local telephone number or more generous data, but it takes longer to obtain and replaces the SIM already in your phone unless the device supports two SIMs.

## Your four main options

| Option | Main advantage | Main disadvantage | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home-network roaming | Nothing new to install | Can be expensive or have small allowances | Short trips with a good roaming bundle |
| Travel eSIM | Buy and install before arrival | Often data-only; compatibility varies | Most short holidays and multi-country trips |
| Local eSIM | Local-network pricing without a plastic SIM | Registration or local payment may be required | Longer visits and destinations with good eSIM support |
| Local physical SIM | Often includes a local number and competitive data | Requires a shop, SIM swap and unlocked phone | Long stays, local calls and devices without eSIM |

There is no universally best option. Compare the **complete cost**, included countries, data allowance, speed, validity period, calls, texts, tethering and support.

## What is an eSIM?

An eSIM is a rewritable SIM built into a compatible device. Instead of inserting a plastic card, you download a network profile—commonly through an app, QR code or activation details.

The GSMA maintains the technical standards used by compliant consumer eSIM systems. An eSIM is not simply a normal app: the downloaded profile securely identifies the device to a mobile network.

Many travel eSIMs provide mobile data only. WhatsApp, FaceTime, iMessage and other internet services can still work, but the plan may not include a local telephone number, ordinary calls or SMS.

## Check these before buying

1. **Does the phone support eSIM?** Check the manufacturer's specification for the exact model and country of purchase.
2. **Is it network-unlocked?** A carrier-locked phone may reject another provider's SIM or eSIM.
3. **Which countries are included?** “Europe” or “Asia” plans do not necessarily include every country in that region.
4. **When does validity begin?** It may start at purchase, installation or first network connection.
5. **Is the plan data-only?** Do not assume it supplies a telephone number or verification texts.
6. **Is tethering allowed?** Some “unlimited” products restrict hotspots.
7. **Is 5G included?** Coverage and local partner networks determine the real speed.
8. **Can it be topped up?** Some profiles can be extended; others require a new installation.

## A safe setup sequence

- Check roaming prices with your home network.
- Confirm that the phone is unlocked and compatible.
- Buy from the network or provider's real website or app.
- Save the QR code and installation instructions somewhere accessible offline.
- Install the eSIM while you still have reliable Wi-Fi.
- Label the lines clearly, such as “Home” and “Travel”.
- Select the travel line for mobile data.
- Turn off data roaming on the home line unless your provider explicitly requires or includes it.
- Enable roaming on the travel line if its instructions require it.
- Prevent automatic mobile-data switching if that could use the expensive home line.
- Test essential services and keep support details offline.

Apple advises travellers to check roaming with their existing network first and documents how a home line and travel eSIM can be used together. Android menu names vary by manufacturer, so follow the device maker's current instructions.

## Keeping your normal number

A dual-SIM phone may keep the home line active for calls and messages while using the travel eSIM for data. This is useful for verification codes, but an incoming call, outgoing call or SMS on the home line can still create roaming charges.

Before travelling:

- confirm how the home network charges calls and texts abroad;
- enable Wi-Fi calling only if you understand its roaming treatment;
- choose the correct default line for calls and data;
- do not remove or deactivate a line that is needed for banking access without a backup method.

Messaging services do not all behave alike. WhatsApp normally remains associated with the existing account number unless you deliberately change it. iMessage and FaceTime settings should be checked after adding or removing a line.

## How much data should you buy?

For a one-week city break, **3–5 GB** is a sensible starting point for maps, messaging, bookings and light browsing. Choose **10 GB or more** when using social video, video calls, frequent uploads or a hotspot. See [How Much Mobile Data Do You Need When Travelling?](/articles/how-much-travel-data-do-you-need/) for a fuller estimate.

Download offline maps, music, video and translation packs over Wi-Fi before departure. Turn off automatic photo backup and application updates on mobile data if the allowance is limited.

## Security and reliability

- Use the official provider app or website rather than a QR code from an unknown seller.
- Protect the provider account and email account with strong authentication.
- Avoid conducting sensitive transactions on an unknown public Wi-Fi network.
- Keep the home SIM and eSIM activation details private.
- Know how to contact the bank without relying on a single mobile number.
- Keep offline copies of tickets, accommodation and emergency information.

An eSIM does not automatically make the internet connection private. It provides cellular access; normal website, app and device security still matters.

## Which option should you choose?

Choose **home roaming** when it is already included and the fair-use allowance is sufficient. Choose a **travel eSIM** for quick installation, short trips or several countries. Choose a **local SIM/eSIM** for a longer stay, a local number or the strongest local-network deal.

Do not buy any product merely because it says “unlimited”. Read the fair-use, speed, hotspot, validity and refund conditions first.
