Lunar New Year falls on **Saturday 6 February 2027**, and Chinatown marks it with a fortnight of decorations, lion dances and New Year menus, peaking on the day of the parade from Trafalgar Square.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **Lunar New Year is Saturday 6 February 2027, the Year of the Fire Goat.** The festival traditionally runs **15 days, to the Lantern Festival on 20 February**. The big day is a **Sunday parade** of lions and dragons from Trafalgar Square up Charing Cross Road to Shaftesbury Avenue, starting at **10am** in 2026, with lion dances through Chinatown from noon and stage shows until 5.30pm. It is free. Arrive before 10am for a kerbside spot, and eat early or book: **Four Seasons** and **Shu Xiang Ge** take bookings, **Dumplings' Legend** and **Wong Kei** are walk-in. Use **Tottenham Court Road** or **Covent Garden** rather than Leicester Square. Greenwich's **National Maritime Museum** has a free Lunar New Year day on **Saturday 13 February**.

## Year of the Fire Goat

The Goat is the **eighth animal of the Chinese zodiac**, and 2027 pairs it with the Fire element. The New Year follows the lunisolar calendar, so it moves each year between 21 January and 20 February, and the celebration traditionally lasts **15 days**, ending with the Lantern Festival. Chinatown London's [Chinese New Year page](https://chinatown.co.uk/en/experience/whats-on/chinese-new-year-in-chinatown/) covers the neighbourhood's celebrations.

## The parade

The parade is organised by the **London Chinatown Chinese Association** and held on a Sunday; in 2026, when the New Year fell on a Tuesday, it was the Sunday after. In 2026:

- **10am:** lions and dragons set off from the **east side of Trafalgar Square**, a few metres from Charing Cross station.
- **The route:** up **Charing Cross Road** to **Shaftesbury Avenue**, finishing at the bottom of **Wardour Street** at about 11.45am.
- **From noon:** a lion dance toured Chinatown along **Gerrard Street, Macclesfield Street, Wardour Street, Leicester Street, Leicester Place and Lisle Street**.
- **Until 5.30pm:** performances on the stages, including **Trafalgar Square** and **Leicester Square**.

The Saturday before brought lion dances around Chinatown for anyone who wanted to start early.

**Where to stand.** To see the whole procession, be on the route before 10am. The parade is over by about midday, so a late arrival gets the lion dances in Chinatown rather than the procession.

## Trafalgar Square

The square is where the parade starts and where the main stage show runs after it, so standing here keeps you in one place for both: in 2026 the procession left at 10am and the performances carried on into the afternoon. It is free and unticketed. The National Gallery on the square's north side is free to enter; our [museums guide](/articles/best-museums-london/) has its hours.

## Chinatown on the day

Chinatown is centred on **Gerrard Street** and also covers **Lisle Street, Wardour Street, Newport Place** and part of **Shaftesbury Avenue**. On parade day the lion dances reach its lanes from noon.

- **Not tied to parade day?** Chinatown London says the New Year brings festive decorations, special menus and cultural activities across the neighbourhood, so any day in the fortnight has the atmosphere. Weekday lunch is Chinatown's quiet time all year.
- **Book for dinner.** Chinatown London advises booking ahead at busy periods such as Chinese New Year; many of the best-known places take no bookings at all, so for those go early, around 5.30pm, or after 9pm.
- **Gerrard Street was completed in 1685** and pedestrianised in the late 1980s, when the first gates went up. The largest gate, on Wardour Street, opened in 2016; our [Soho and Chinatown walk](/articles/soho-chinatown-walk/) has the history.

## Where to eat

All from our [Chinese and East Asian restaurants](/articles/best-chinese-east-asian-restaurants-london/), [dim sum](/articles/best-dim-sum-london/) and [hot pot](/articles/best-hot-pot-london/) guides, which have the full entries. ### Four Seasons, Gerrard Street

*££ · 12 Gerrard Street · open from noon · books by phone*

The **roast duck hanging in the window** is the order. It takes bookings, so make one for the New Year weekends.

### Tao Tao Ju, Lisle Street

*£££ · 15 Lisle Street · open daily from noon*

**Dim sum made on site each day**, in towering bamboo baskets: order the steamed **har gau** and **siu mai**, then the grilled and fried baskets. A quieter, smarter room than the street outside.

### Plum Valley, Gerrard Street

*£££ · Gerrard Street · three minutes from Leicester Square*

Family-run, black-fronted, and **more than sixty handmade dim sum** made fresh daily. Smarter than most of Chinatown.

### Dumplings' Legend, Gerrard Street

*££ · 15–16 Gerrard Street · walk-in*

The **xiao long bao** are folded behind a glass wall in front of the queue: Shanghainese soup dumplings, pleated by hand and steamed to order. **No bookings**, so on parade day go at an odd hour.

### Wong Kei, Wardour Street

*£ · 41–43 Wardour Street · walk-in*

Around **five hundred covers over several floors**, roast meats over rice and wonton soup, and a decades-old reputation for brusque service. Fast and loud.

### Shu Xiang Ge, Gerrard Street

*£££ · book a few days ahead*

Sichuan hot pot in a **nine-grid pot**, one broth split into nine compartments so each ingredient cooks in the grid that suits it. A yuanyang split pot is there for anyone who cannot take the mala heat. Book a few days ahead.

### Haidilao, Leicester Square

*£££ · the Trocadero, off Piccadilly Circus*

London's most-cited hot pot, with an apron, a hair tie and a bag for your coat at the table, and noodles stretched by hand. **Tables are held back for walk-ins** and staff text you when one is ready, so you need not stand in the queue.

### Chinatown Bakery, Newport Place

*£ · 7 Newport Place · 10.30am to 9pm daily*

**Egg tarts warm from the tray** and **bolo bao**, the sugar-crusted "pineapple" bun with no pineapple in it, made in the window. There is usually a queue.

> 💡 **Lunch beats tea for dim sum.** Most of Chinatown's dim sum kitchens stop at about 4pm, so on parade day eat before the afternoon crowd or switch to hot pot for dinner.

## Other Lunar New Year celebrations

**The National Maritime Museum, Greenwich** holds a [Lunar New Year day](https://www.rmg.co.uk/whats-on/national-maritime-museum/lunar-new-year) on **Saturday 13 February 2027, 10am to 4pm**. It is free, and the 2026 edition had family workshops, objects from the museum's East and Southeast Asian collections and live performances. It suits families who want the celebration without the West End crowd; our [Greenwich area guide](/articles/greenwich-area-guide/) covers the rest of the day.

## Getting there

- **Leicester Square** (Northern and Piccadilly lines) is two minutes from Chinatown and the closest station, which makes it the most crowded on parade day.
- **Piccadilly Circus** (Bakerloo and Piccadilly) is about five minutes' walk, **Covent Garden** (Piccadilly) about seven.
- **Tottenham Court Road** (Central, Northern and Elizabeth lines) is eight to ten minutes' walk, and the Elizabeth line runs there direct from Heathrow.
- **Charing Cross** is beside the parade's start at Trafalgar Square.
- **Buses** stop on Shaftesbury Avenue and Charing Cross Road, which are the parade route, so expect diversions on the morning of the parade.
