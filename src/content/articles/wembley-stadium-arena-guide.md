Wembley is the easiest big venue in London to reach and one of the easiest to get wrong. Up to 90,000 people arrive at three suburban stations, the streets for up to two miles around are closed to your car from eight in the morning, and the place you were planning to eat in may be ticketed and shut all day.

This page is for one person: somebody with a ticket for **Wembley Stadium** or **OVO Arena Wembley**, a fixed date and a fixed kick-off, who has never done it before.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** Come by train if you can — **Wembley Park** is ten minutes down Olympic Way and has **33 ticket gates**, which is why it swallows the crowd. If you drive, **do not park on the street**: Brent's event-day zone reaches **3.26km** from the ground, runs **8am to midnight**, there is **no permit a visitor can buy**, and the higher-rate penalty is **£130**. Official parking was **£50 plus a £2.50 fee** at every stadium event checked. The cheap trick is to **park a stop out** — **Preston Road £10** all day on an event day, **Northwick Park £3** and free at weekends — but **only off-street**, and **not at Neasden or Stonebridge Park**, which do not work at all. Getting home: on a **Friday or Saturday the Jubilee Night Tube runs every ten minutes all night**, and the car parks stay open until **03:00**, so you can let the whole thing empty out. Any other night you are chasing a last train around **midnight**. And book a bed early or not at all — the Novotel was **£426.60** on a match Tuesday against **£131.40** an ordinary one, while **Waterloo did not move**.

## Getting there by train

Three stations serve the ground, they are not interchangeable, and which one you want depends on where your seat is and where you are coming from.

| Station | Lines | Walk to the stadium | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wembley Park** | **Jubilee, Metropolitan** | **10 minutes** down Olympic Way | Everything — the default, and the one for the arena |
| **Wembley Stadium** | **Chiltern**, from Marylebone | **5–10 minutes** across the White Horse Bridge | Seats in the **south and east** of the ground |
| **Wembley Central** | **Bakerloo**, Overground, Southern, LNR | **15–20 minutes** | The **west and south-west**, and anyone coming from Paddington, Euston, Watford or south London |

### Wembley Park: the one the stadium is designed around

Two stops from Baker Street on the Metropolitan, and on the Jubilee for everywhere else. You come out of the station at the top of **Olympic Way** — Wembley Way to everyone who has been — and walk straight down it to the Olympic Steps and the north concourse. It is pedestrianised, it is where the crowd goes, and it is impossible to get wrong. Both the stadium and the estate call it ten minutes; the straight-line distance is 803m.

The old 1970s concrete pedway that used to carry people up to the stadium has gone, replaced by the **Olympic Steps**, and Olympic Way itself has been widened by 50 per cent and extended to meet Engineers Way. Four lifts now run from ground level to the stadium concourse, each taking three wheelchairs.

The reason this station matters more than the other two is capacity: **33 ticket gates across two ticket halls**, against 12 at Wembley Central and none at all at Wembley Stadium station. It is also the station for the arena — **614m**, slightly nearer than the stadium — for Boxpark, and for the London Designer Outlet.

> ⚠️ **One live caveat on the lifts.** TfL's own notice for Wembley Park says that from March until **autumn 2026** there is **no lift between the street and the ticket hall** on the Wembley Way side, and that step-free access is instead via the **Bridge Road entrance**, which is level from street to ticket hall. Lifts to all platforms are unaffected. Three operators publish three different end dates for the work, so check TfL's own station page on the morning of your event rather than taking any of them on trust.

### Wembley Stadium station: an event station, not a commuter one

One stop from **Marylebone** on Chiltern, and quick — Chiltern says under ten minutes, Quintain says nine, and the live board on an event day showed 10 to 15. Chiltern puts the walk to the ground at five minutes and the stadium's own help centre says ten; the straight-line distance is 452m and the walk crosses the White Horse Bridge, so the truth sits between them.

Understand what this station is. On an ordinary Tuesday it is **unstaffed, with no ticket office, no ticket gates, no toilets and two unheated shelters**. On event days Chiltern staffs it, runs extra trains and sorts the crowd into a colour-coded queuing system. Oyster and contactless work, and it is Zone 4.

> ⚠️ **The lifts between the platforms and the footbridge were out of order** when this was checked on 9 September 2026, and the step-free route between platforms is via the street in any case — about 300m. Chiltern asks for at least two hours' notice to book assistance: **03456 005 165**, 07:00 to 20:00 daily. There are Help Points on both platforms.

### Wembley Central: the long walk with the best connections

The furthest of the three and the only one where the walk is a real walk. The stadium's help centre says 20 minutes; the estate says around 13. At 1.25km through streets with a crowd on them, **15 to 20 minutes** is the honest figure — and the stadium offers buses **18, 92 and 182** as an alternative, which tells you what it thinks of the distance.

It earns its place because of what it is on: the **Bakerloo line** and the **London Overground** from Watford Junction to Euston. From Paddington or Euston it can beat Wembley Park door-to-door.

> ⚠️ **Step-free here means street to platform, not platform to train.** Three separate operators make the same distinction in different words: there is a lift down to the platform, but there is a step up into the carriage on both the Bakerloo and the Overground. The lifts between platform 5 and the station entrance were also out of order when checked.

### Buses and bikes

Routes **18, 83, 92, 182, 206, 223, 297 and 483** serve the area, and the **N18 and N83 run all night** — which makes buses the fallback on a midweek night once the last tube has gone. Wembley Park's own accessibility page lists the 83, 182, 206, 223 and 297 as fully wheelchair accessible.

By bike, there is parking at street level in the **north-east corner of the stadium**, plus over 400 cycle spaces across Wembley Park, secure storage bookable through the Spokesafe app, and a repair station on Weaver Walk.

## Driving: the zone that costs £130

This is the part people find out about afterwards.

Brent Council operates the **Wembley Stadium Event Day Protective Parking Scheme**, and it is far bigger than anyone expects. Mapped from the council's own published boundary — 35 polygons — the furthest point is **3.26km from the stadium**: roughly 3km west, 2.7km east, 2.4km north and 1.8km south. It swallows Wembley Park, Wembley Central, Wembley Stadium station, Preston Road, Neasden, Stonebridge Park and Sudbury Town.

Inside it, on an event day, **only residents and businesses with an event-day permit may park on the street**. Not for a fee. Not in a pay-and-display bay.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **When it applies** | **8am to midnight** on main roads to the stadium; generally **10am to midnight** in residential streets outside controlled parking zones; **8am to midnight** in CPZs inside the scheme (zone 'SA' is 10am to midnight, zone 'T' is **24/7**) |
| **Who can park** | Residents and businesses with a **£15 event-day permit**, plus schools and places of worship inside the zone |
| **Can a visitor buy one?** | **No** |
| **Higher-rate penalty** | **£130** (band A) or £110 (band B) — **halved if paid within 14 days** |
| **Lower-rate penalty** | £80 (band A) or £60 (band B) |
| **Removal** | Possible **30 minutes** after a ticket is issued; some contraventions bring immediate removal |

Note the shape of those hours. For the Bon Jovi concert on 9 September 2026, doors opened at 5pm, **road closures went in from 3pm, and the parking restrictions started at 8am** — nine hours before anybody arrived.

The only lawful way a non-resident parks on a Wembley street on an event day is if a resident inside the zone hands over one of the **two paper visitor permits** their household is allowed. Anything sold to you online is not a Brent permit.

> 💡 **Check your exact address before you commit.** Brent publishes a postcode-searchable map that shows both the controlled parking zones and the event-day zone: [Brent's CPZ map](https://www.brent.gov.uk/parking-roads-and-travel/parking/where-you-can-park/controlled-parking-zones/cpz-map). It is the council's own tool and it settles the question in about ten seconds.

**Two things that are not loopholes.** Private and unadopted roads inside the zone are outside Brent's enforcement — but they are private property, so the landowner can clamp, tow or issue a charge of their own, and none of Brent's appeal rights apply. And **pop-up car parks**, which appear on event days, are warned against by name by both venues: the stadium's own wording cites insurance risk, car crime and poor lighting, and the arena says the same more briefly.

**Motorcycles are exempt.** Brent's own wording is that a motorcycle may be left in a parking space without charge or time limit, provided that part of the bay has not been suspended.

**ULEZ applies.** Wembley is in every London borough's worth of Ultra Low Emission Zone at **£12.50 a day**, though it is not in the Congestion Charge zone. The stadium flags a trap worth knowing: the charge runs midnight to midnight, so a late departure from an evening gig can cross the boundary and cost you two days.

## Where to actually park

### Official parking, at the ground

All parking for events at Wembley — including Blue Badge — is booked through **[Wembley Official Parking](https://www.wembleyofficialparking.com/)**. The car parks belong to Quintain, on the Wembley Park estate, not to the stadium. They are barrierless and run on ANPR, so there is no pay machine; your booking confirmation carries a QR code that is scanned on arrival.

Quintain publishes stadium parking as **from £40 per car**. What the booking site actually showed, checked on 9 September 2026 across three separate stadium events:

| | Stadium event | Arena event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Car** | **£50.00** | £22.50 (Spiritbox, 19 Sep) |
| **Blue Badge / wheelchair** | **£25.00** | £18.50 |
| **Minibus or limo** | £75.00 | £40.00 |
| **Coach** | £120.00 | £40.00 |
| **Booking fee** | £2.50 car and Blue Badge, £1 motorbike, £3.50 minibus, £5 coach | as stadium |

Arena parking is published as starting from **£16.25**. Both are per event rather than a standard tariff, and the "from £40" wording means a smaller fixture can be cheaper — so treat £50 as what you should expect, not as the price.

For contrast, on a **non-event day** the same Red and Pink car parks charge **£28 for 24 hours**, and £11.50 for six to eight. Event pricing is a different tariff, not a surcharge on the normal one.

| Car park | Postcode | Walk | Height limit | Takes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blue** | HA9 0TU | **6 min** | 2.6m | Car, **Blue Badge**, wheelchair, EV (10 Pod Point bays, level 2) |
| **Pink** | HA9 0HX | 8 min | 2.10m | Car, minibus, **coach**, EV (levels 1–3) — **no Blue Badge bays** |
| **Red** | HA9 0FD | 9 min | 2.45m | Car, **motorbike** (level 2), **Blue Badge**, EV (6 bays, level 1) |
| **Gold** | — | 10 min | **2.00m** | Car, Blue Badge — the **Hilton's** car park, off Lakeside Way. No charging |

**Blue Parking is the one with the bridge**, on level 3, straight into the stadium concourse. **Red** sits opposite the main entrance of the London Designer Outlet.

> ⚠️ **They sell out, and the accessible bays go first.** On 9 September, Blue Badge and wheelchair bays were **already sold out** for both the 12 September concert and the 26 September England international — seventeen days out. EV charging bays were sold out for all three stadium events checked. Pink car parking for the 12 September concert was down to **two spaces**. Refunds only exist for cancellations made **more than 72 hours** before, and never on the booking fee.

### Brent Civic Centre: the official car park nobody mentions

There is a **council** car park on **Engineers Way, HA9 0FJ**, 370m from the stadium and next door to the arena, that you do not book through Wembley Official Parking at all.

| Stay | Normal day | Stadium event day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 1 hour | £3 | **£3** |
| Up to 2 hours | £6 | **£6** |
| Up to 3 hours | £10 | **£10** |
| Whole day | £25 | **£50** |

The whole-day rate matches Quintain's £50, so it is no bargain if you are there all afternoon. The **short stays are the interesting bit**: three hours for £10 whether there is an event on or not. And **Blue Badge holders park free for up to three hours** in the designated bays, which is the only free disabled parking anywhere near the ground.

### The London Designer Outlet's £40 loophole

LDO applies a flat **£50 per car** on stadium event days, midnight to midnight, and **£20** on arena days from an hour before the event. But spend **more than £40** across its shops, restaurants or the Cineworld on an event day and, subject to availability, you pay the **normal tariff for the first six hours**. The ticket has to be validated at the **Red Car Park Office** before you leave, it is Red Parking only, and it excludes pre-booked parking. Spaces are first-come, first-served.

## Park a stop out

The good idea is to leave the car a stop or two down the line and take the tube in. It works — but **only off-street**, and not at most of the stations you would first think of.

The distinction that decides everything: **the event-day zone bans parking on the street, not in car parks**. Two of the Brent car parks inside the zone publish their own event-day tariffs, which is how you know the council expects event traffic to use them. Getting this wrong is the difference between a £10 ticket and a £130 penalty.

| Station | Line | To Wembley Park | Car park | Event-day cost | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Preston Road** | Metropolitan | **1 stop** | Brent, **164 bays** | **£10 all day** | **Best of the six.** Inside the zone, so street parking is out — but Brent opens a **second level for events** and sells an all-day event ticket. Three ticket gates, not step-free, and it will fill |
| **Northwick Park** | Metropolitan | 2 stops | Brent, 96 bays | **£3 all day — free evenings and weekends** | **Cheapest.** Charges apply **Mon–Fri 8am–6pm only**, so a Saturday match or an evening kick-off costs nothing. Outside the zone. 2m height limit, not step-free, and the hospital next door means real weekday demand |
| **Kingsbury** | **Jubilee** | 3 stops | Brent, 48 bays | £7.50 all day, **free Sundays** | **Works, and it is step-free.** On the Jubilee, so **Night Tube** on Friday and Saturday. Only 48 spaces |
| **Harrow-on-the-Hill** | Metropolitan, Chiltern | 3–5 stops | **TfL, 113 spaces** | **£5** (£2 Sat and bank holidays, £1.50 Sun) | **Works.** The only one with a proper TfL station car park, open 24 hours, 16 gates and 5 lifts. Furthest out at 4.75km, 1.9m height limit, and **no Night Tube** |

| **Stonebridge Park** | Bakerloo, Overground | — | **None** | — | **Does not work** |

> ⚠️ **Neasden is a trap, and it is a deliberate one.** The station sits inside the event-day zone, so the streets are out — and on a Wembley event day Brent caps the station car park at a **two-hour maximum stay**, with no all-day event ticket. You cannot leave a car there and go to a match. Stonebridge Park is simpler: it has no car park of any kind, and at 1.35km it is well inside the ring.

Brent car parks take **RingGo** (Preston Road reference 5005, Northwick Park 5096, Kingsbury 5002) and cash with a 50p surcharge. **Disabled badge holders park free for an unlimited period** in all of them. Note that these four car park pages were last updated in March 2023, so the tariffs are Brent's published figures but not fresh ones — check the machine.

## Getting home

Every operator here publishes an event-day queuing arrangement. Nobody hides them; they are just not where people look.

### The queue windows, in TfL's own words

For a sell-out concert, TfL publishes the actual window during which the station is running a managed queue:

| Station | Published busy window | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wembley Park** | roughly **22:30–23:40** | **Bridge Road becomes exit-only.** Everyone enters via Olympic Way, **including step-free entry by lift** |
| **Wembley Central** | roughly **22:40–23:30** | **The High Road entrance becomes exit-only.** Entry is via the **London Road events entrance, which is not step-free** — ask a member of staff |

That is about **seventy minutes of queuing at Wembley Park** and fifty at Wembley Central. Wembley Park's window is longer because far more people use it.

At **Wembley Stadium station**, Chiltern sorts the crowd by destination on the forecourt before anyone gets near a platform:

| Queue | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| **Blue** | **Marylebone only** |
| Pink | Haddenham & Thame Parkway, Bicester North and stations to Birmingham |
| Yellow | Denham and stations to Aylesbury via High Wycombe |
| Green | Bicester Village and the Oxford stations |
| Orange | Northolt Park, South Ruislip, West Ruislip |

If you are going to Marylebone, you want the Blue queue and nothing else.

> 💡 **The fastest exit is the one nobody queues for.** No operator publishes a ranking, but on their own numbers Wembley Park clears fastest, then Wembley Stadium station, then Wembley Central. The crush concentrates on Olympic Way and Wembley Park because that is where the signage points — which is precisely why walking the twenty minutes to Wembley Central, or taking the Chiltern train if Marylebone suits you, is so often quicker in practice.

### The Night Tube changes the entire evening

**Friday and Saturday nights, the Jubilee line runs all night through Wembley Park, roughly every ten minutes.** The Metropolitan runs no Night Tube at all.

Sampled from TfL's own journey planner for the small hours of Saturday 13 September 2026: **01:34, 01:44, 01:54 … 03:34, 03:44, 03:54**. Fares are off-peak, and step-free access is the same as during the day.

This is the single most useful fact on the page for a Friday or Saturday event. **You do not have to join the crush at all.** You can sit somewhere for two hours and walk onto a train at one in the morning.

Any other night, you are chasing a last train.

### Last trains

| Night | Last Chiltern to Marylebone | Last tube from Wembley Park |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Event night, Wed 9 Sep** (concert) | **00:06**, arriving Marylebone 00:17 | **00:32** to Baker Street, then nothing until 05:00 |
| **Event night, Sat 19 Sep**, 17:00 kick-off | **23:44** | Night Tube — all night |
| Ordinary Thursday | 00:05 | — |
| Ordinary Sunday | 23:50 | — |

> ⚠️ **An event buys you frequency, not a later last train.** On the Wednesday concert night there were **fifteen Chiltern departures between 22:00 and the last one** — roughly one every six to eight minutes for two hours, against two or three an hour on a normal night. But look at the Saturday: after a 17:00 kick-off the last train to Marylebone was **23:44**, which is *earlier* than the 00:05 last train on an ordinary Thursday. Do not assume the event extends your evening at the end of the night.

Two more things. Chiltern pays for the enhanced Wembley service out of the rest of its network — on 9 September it cancelled four other services to release the trains — and **weekend engineering work can cut Wembley Stadium station back to a Marylebone shuttle only**, with no route from the High Wycombe direction at all. Chiltern publishes a page for each specific event with the amended timetable and that night's queue colours: [Chiltern's Wembley events page](https://www.chilternrailways.co.uk/wembley-events-travel-information) is the link to check against your own date.

### If you drove, you have until three in the morning

**Wembley Park's official car parks are open 07:00 to 03:00 on stadium and arena event days.** That is the strongest argument for driving that exists here: you can sit in a pub for two hours and let the entire place empty out before you touch the car.

Taxis: there is an **event-day taxi rank on Engineers Way**, next to the arena. Bear in mind that road closures were in place from 3pm for a 5pm doors time on 9 September, and Engineers Way sits inside that. The stadium's own advice is that public transport is the best way to get to Wembley, and that if you do need a cab you should only use a licensed one.

Coaches: **National Express** is the stadium's Official Coach Supplier, running Wembley Express services direct to the ground from over 50 UK locations, with a guaranteed seat home and — its wording — drivers who wait if the event overruns.

## Where to stay, and why Wembley is the trap on a match night

Start with the number, because it is the whole story.

**Novotel London Wembley, on Olympic Way, was £426.60 for the night of England v Czechia on Tuesday 6 October. The same hotel, one week later on an ordinary Tuesday, was £131.40.**

Both figures are Accor's own member rates for one room, two adults, taxes and fees included, read from Accor's booking engine within the same hour on 9 September 2026. Two Tuesdays a week apart removes the weekend effect and isolates the event.

| Novotel London Wembley | Event Tuesday, 6 Oct | Ordinary Tuesday, 13 Oct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cheapest room available** | **£426.60** | **£131.40** |
| **Room types available** | **1** | 6 |
| **The same 61m² suite** | **£426.60** | £222.30 |

Two separate things are happening. The **identical suite** goes from £222.30 to £426.60 — up **92 per cent**. And the cheap rooms **do not exist at all** on match night, so somebody who only wants a bed goes from £131.40 to £426.60, which is **3.25 times**.

Now the control. **Novotel London Waterloo** — same brand, same booking engine, and on the Jubilee line direct to Wembley Park, so a genuine stay-central-and-travel-out option:

| The same two nights | Wembley | Waterloo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Event Tuesday, 6 Oct** | **£426.60**, one room type | **£300.60**, seven room types |
| Ordinary Tuesday, 13 Oct | **£131.40** | £315.90 |

**Central London does not move for a Wembley event.** Waterloo was in fact **£15.30 cheaper** on the match night than on the ordinary Tuesday. The surge is a Wembley phenomenon, not a London one.

> 💡 **So the rule is: Wembley is the bargain on a normal night and the trap on an event night.** On an ordinary Tuesday, Wembley beats Waterloo by £184.50 and it is not close. On the match Tuesday, Waterloo is £126 cheaper *and* you get a choice of seven rooms instead of one. If you are booking late for a big fixture, price a central hotel on the Jubilee line before you assume Wembley is cheaper.

It gets starker at the top of the market. For **England v Spain on Saturday 26 September**, the Novotel was **sold out** when checked **seventeen days out** — Accor's engine simply returned "unavailable". Its own flexible-date strip around that Saturday:

| | Thu 24 | Fri 25 | **Sat 26 (England v Spain)** | Sun 27 | Mon 28 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **From** | £134 | £171 | **Sold out** | **£91** | £99 |

The night *after* an England international at Wembley costs **£91**. The night of it cannot be bought at all.

**What to do with that:**

- **For a big stadium fixture, book months out or do not bother.** By seventeen days before England v Spain there was nothing at any price.
- **Consider staying the night after instead**, and travelling home on the Sunday.
- **Neither Accor hotel publishes a minimum stay or an event-night policy.** We searched both hotel pages for minimum-stay, two-night, event-night and deposit terms and there are none. The event effect shows up purely as price and availability.
- **Luggage storage is worth more than the room on an event day** — see [the bag problem](#the-bag-problem) below.

### The three hotels at the ground

**[Novotel London Wembley](hotel:novotel-london-wembley)** — 235 rooms at 5 Olympic Way, on the approach itself, with parking, a restaurant, a bar and a gym. Accor puts it three minutes from the London Designer Outlet and two from the arena. Check-in from 15:00, out by 12:00. Its own guest rating is 4.5 out of 5 from 1,415 reviews. This is the one all the pricing above is drawn from.

**[Hilton London Wembley](hotel:hilton-london-wembley)** — the four-star on Lakeside Way, and the one with the indoor pool, an executive lounge and the rooftop **Sky Bar 9**, which takes walk-ins and no reservations. **Icons Bar, Grill and Terrace** is open daily 10:00 to 17:00, with afternoon tea Friday to Sunday 13:00 to 17:00 on 48 hours' notice — so it is an option before an evening event, not after one. Self-parking is **£15 per 24 hours in the Gold car park**, with **no in-out privileges**, which matters on an event day: you cannot take the car out and bring it back. No room rate is quoted here because Hilton's live rates sit behind a path its own robots.txt disallows, so none was taken.

**[ibis London Wembley](hotel:ibis-london-wembley)** — the budget option, on South Way on the south side of the ground, a minute from Wembley Stadium station. Online prices are currently unavailable.

If you would rather stay central and travel out, our [guide to the best areas to stay in London](/articles/best-areas-to-stay-in-london/) covers the Jubilee line neighbourhoods that keep the journey to a single train.

## The hours before doors

![The inside of Wembley Stadium during an evening England match, the pitch floodlit and the tiers full, seen from high in the upper bowl](../../assets/articles/wembley-stadium-arena-guide/wembley-stadium-interior.jpg)

*Ninety thousand people arrive over about two hours and try to leave in twenty minutes. That asymmetry is the whole reason this page exists.*

This is a ninety-minutes-to-kill problem, not a day trip. Everything here is within a few minutes of both venues.

**The Wembley Park Art Trail** is the best fit, and it is free. Twenty-six outdoor works across the estate, running to the end of 2028, which the operator's own FAQ puts at **30 minutes to an hour** depending on pace — almost exactly the gap you have. Start outside Wembley Park station and follow the numbers; there is a free CityTrotter app that navigates by GPS, and the trail is step-free throughout.

The ones worth the detour:

- **The Bobby Moore Statue** by Philip Jackson, six metres of bronze in front of the stadium. The classic pre-match photograph.
- **I'm Mr. Mercury**, Frank Styles' large-scale anamorphic Freddie Mercury on the Spanish Steps.
- **Ready For It** on the same steps — the Taylor Swift tapestry that got them renamed the Swiftie Steps — and the **Lana Del Rey mural** on Lana Lane beside them.
- **Shadow Wall** by Jason Bruges in the Royal Route Underpass, which is fully interactive and mimics people walking past it.
- **Flight**, Mark Anthony's red kite mural on the wall above platform 1 at Wembley Stadium station — best seen from platform 2, so look across while you wait.

**The Square of Fame** on Arena Square is the best five-minute filler there is if you have an arena ticket, because you are standing on it while you queue: bronze plaques set with the handprints of performers who have played the hall, from Dolly Parton and Shirley Bassey to Kylie and Status Quo.

Indoors and free, the **Brent Civic Centre** houses Wembley Library and public toilets, which makes it the genuinely useful wet-weather option. LDO has the other public toilets.

> ⚠️ **You cannot do the stadium tour on your match day.** The tour **does not run on stadium event days at all**, and it is suspended either side of them. Availability checked across three separate booking windows lined up exactly with the stadium's own calendar — every blank date was an event date, including a lone blank Tuesday that was England v Czechia. In the run-up, the full tour is replaced by a cut-down **Express Tour** which the operator states does not include the player areas or the dressing rooms. If you want both, that is two separate days, and ideally not consecutive ones.

For reference, when it does run: **£28 adult, £19 child, concession or accessible, £75 family, free under 5s and free carers**, about 90 minutes, and the operator suggests allowing two hours for the exhibition spaces. There is **no cloakroom** on the tour and large bags are not permitted.

The separately sold **VIP Matchday Tour Experience** is not what you want. It bundles a 45-minute behind-the-scenes tour with **a match ticket included** — so it is no use to anyone who already has one.

For something more active, **PLAYBOX** inside Boxpark is a glow-in-the-dark games room with pool, air hockey, beer pong and Mario Kart, 18+ after 20:00, and **Sixes Social Cricket** has batting cages in the same building. Both carry the same event-day warning as everything else in there, which is the next section.

## Eating and drinking, and the Boxpark trap

![Boxpark Wembley's black frontage seen from below through trees, the white BOXPARK sign along the roofline, EAST END lettering over the doors and crowd barriers set out in front](../../assets/articles/wembley-stadium-arena-guide/boxpark-wembley.jpg)

*The barriers are the tell. On stadium event days Boxpark runs ticketed Fanparks and closes to everyone else — all day, not just the evening.*

**Boxpark Wembley regularly closes to the public on stadium event days.** It becomes a ticketed **Fanpark** — and the closures are listed as **ALL DAY**, not just from the evening.

This is the single biggest trap in the area, because "we'll get there early and eat at Boxpark" is the plan almost everybody makes, and it fails on precisely the day you are going. On 9 September 2026, Boxpark's own closures page listed the Bon Jovi Fanpark on 9 September and the Diljit Dosanjh Fanpark on 12 September, both marked all day, both ticketed through Eventbrite — and both matching stadium event dates exactly. That page is a rolling list of the next few dates rather than a season calendar, so the only reliable move is to **check [Boxpark's closures page](https://www.boxpark.co.uk/venues/wembley/blog/closures) against your own event date** before you build an afternoon around it.

When it is open, Boxpark runs **09:00 to 23:00 seven days a week** with more than twenty kitchens — Zia Lucia, The Athenian, Neat Burger, Molo Fish & Chips, Tortilla, German Doner Kebab, The Cheese Wheel and the rest — plus a sports bar with QR-code table service.

> ⚠️ **And the happy hour is switched off on your night.** Boxpark's 17:00–20:00 weekday happy hour explicitly does not apply "during Fanparks, Arena dates or televised England games" — which is to say, on exactly the evenings our reader is there.

![The entrance to the London Designer Outlet at Wembley Park, with giant gold LDO letters standing on grass plinths in front of a red brick and glass frontage, Holland & Barrett and MOSS inside the doorway and an Amorino gelato shop to the right](../../assets/articles/wembley-stadium-arena-guide/london-designer-outlet.jpg)

*The London Designer Outlet, ten minutes from Wembley Park station. Over 70 stores, and the balcony banner above the doors names most of the restaurants — Las Iguanas, JRC Global Buffet, Frankie & Benny's, Afrikana, Big Moe's Diner.*

### The London Designer Outlet does not stay open for you

**LDO does not extend its hours for stadium events.** On the Wednesday of the Bon Jovi concert the centre shut at 20:00 as usual, and on the Saturday of the Diljit Dosanjh show it shut at 21:00 as usual. Only the restaurants trade later, and they run their own hours.

| Day | Centre hours |
| --- | --- |
| Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday | 10:00–20:00 |
| Thursday, Friday, Saturday | 10:00–21:00 |
| Sunday | 11:00–19:00 |

On Sundays the big stores split: **Skechers, Next and M&S open 11:00–17:00**, while **Nike, Adidas, Tommy Hilfiger, Calvin Klein and Guess open 12:00–18:00**.

### What is still open when you come out

A 19:45 kick-off finishes around 21:40. A concert curfew is usually 22:00 to 22:30. Almost everything here is built around shopping hours.

| Friday or Saturday | Until |
| --- | --- |
| **Pizza Express** | **23:30** — the latest kitchen in LDO |
| Las Iguanas, Wagamama, Zizzi, You Me Sushi | 23:00 |
| TGI Fridays, Big Moe's Diner | 22:30 |
| **Boxpark** | 23:00 — **but only if it is not a Fanpark day** |

| Midweek | Until |
| --- | --- |
| **Las Iguanas** | **22:30** — the latest thing in LDO on a weekday |
| TGI Fridays, Nando's, Wagamama, Pizza Express, Zizzi, You Me Sushi | 22:00 |
| Big Moe's Diner | 21:45 |

> ⚠️ **On a midweek England night the whole place is shutting as you walk out.** With a 19:45 kick-off you reach the concourse at about 21:40 and everything in the outlet closes at 22:00 except Las Iguanas, which you will be racing. **Eat before, not after** — or plan to get back into town first. Nando's is the reliable one, on the same hours every day of the week, and Subway opens at 06:00 if you are there stupidly early. Pret shuts by 18:30 midweek, so it is not a pre-doors option for an evening event.

Beyond LDO and Boxpark, Wembley Park claims over 50 eateries, among them **Bread Ahead**, **Pasta Remoli**, **Estadio Lounge**, **Five Guys**, **Black Sheep Coffee** and **Choppaluna** — none of which publish hours on the estate's directory, so check the operator's own site before you rely on one of them after an event.

### Inside the arena

OVO Arena Wembley's concourse kiosks do hot dogs, burgers, pizzas and nachos, with bars on cocktails, wine, craft beer and soft drinks, and bundle deals. It is **completely cashless and there are no cash machines in the building**. **Free water is available on the concourses.** You may bring confectionery; drinks and hot food are prohibited. Photo ID is required for alcohol if you look under 25. Wembley Stadium is cashless too — no kiosk there takes cash.

## Drinking on event days

**You can buy alcohol in Wembley on an event day. There is no blanket ban on shops**, and the Tesco Express, Sainsbury's Local and Co-op on the estate list their normal hours. What catches people out is narrower than the rumour, and it comes in two parts.

**Some shops will still refuse you, because their licence says so.** Brent's [licensing policy](https://www.brent.gov.uk/-/media/files/business-documents/licencing/statement-of-licensing-policy---2025-2030.pdf?rev=18ceab9f9de6419badbee8d6ec57a872) sets special conditions for Wembley on major event days: "no sale of alcohol one hour before the event, and one hour after", no off-sales to anyone wearing "game day paraphernalia" (a team shirt, in other words), and no more than four cans a customer. These go onto individual licences rather than applying to every shop, so one shop may serve you and the next may not, and a team shirt can be enough to get you refused.

> ⚠️ **And you cannot drink it on the street.** A [Public Spaces Protection Order](https://www.brent.gov.uk/nuisance-crime-and-community-safety/public-spaces-protection-orders) covers Wembley Park at all times until January 2029, and the same rule runs across the rest of Brent. An officer can make you stop drinking in public and hand over your alcohol, **sealed cans included**. Refusing is a **£100 fixed penalty**, or up to £500 in court. Brent enforces it as a No Street Drinking Zone on Olympic Way on big event days. A pub's own terrace or garden is exempt. So the classic plan of cans from the Tesco, drunk on Olympic Way, is the one that gets your drink taken off you.

**Inside the stadium, it depends on the fixture.** At concerts, NFL and rugby league the concourse bars serve as normal. At football nobody may drink in view of the pitch, and at competitive UEFA and FIFA games **alcohol is sold only in restaurants and hospitality, not on the level 1 and level 5 concourses**. That includes England's autumn 2026 Nations League games against Spain, Czechia and Croatia. You cannot bring any in: the only drink container allowed is one empty, clear plastic bottle of up to 500ml for the free water points, and not in the pitch standing area at concerts. The arena has no readmission, so stepping out for a drink ends your night.

### Where to go for a drink

**On football days, Brent allocates pubs to one set of fans or the other** by the side of the stadium your ticket is for, and pubs near the ground often let in only ticket holders for their team. The list changes with every fixture and goes up on [Brent's event-days page](https://www.brent.gov.uk/parking-roads-and-travel/parking/wembley-event-day-parking/event-days) ahead of big matches. There are no allocations for concerts.

| Where | Walk to the stadium | What to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The White Horse** (Fuller's), Arena Square | 5 min | Football: often one set of fans only, with tickets checked at the door. **No alcohol from an hour before kick-off to 15 minutes after**, drinks in plastic, and it does not show the match. Concerts: no allocation and no cut-off |
| **Sky Bar 9**, ninth floor of the Hilton | 7 min | Rooftop bar, walk-in only. Normally open until midnight, 01:00 on Fridays and Saturdays, but hours can change on event days and the 2-for-1 cocktail hour is off |
| **Boxpark**, 18 Olympic Way | 9 min | A ticketed Fanpark on some event days, all day (see above). 18+ after 20:00 with physical ID |
| **Feed the Yak**, 51 Olympic Way | 9 min | Craft-beer sports bar with 20 taps. Closed Mondays |
| **The Green Man**, Dagmar Avenue | 15 min | Pub and hotel at the top of Wembley Hill. It also sells event-day parking: £40 for the stadium, £20 for the arena, booked by phone |
| **The Torch** (Greene King), Bridge Road | 16 min | Beside Wembley Park station, handy if that is your way home |
| **J.J. Moon's** (Wetherspoon), High Road | 19 min | On the Wembley Central side |

*Walking times are TfL's.*

## The bag problem

This is the practical thing that ruins a Wembley day. If your hotel checkout is 12:00 and doors are at 18:30, you cannot simply carry a bag around with you.

| | Wembley Stadium | OVO Arena Wembley |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Limit** | **One small bag per person, no bigger than A4** — 297mm × 210mm × 210mm | **30cm × 40cm × 20cm** — take the stricter of the two figures its FAQ gives |
| **Storage on site** | — | **None. No cloakroom** |
| **Luggage** | — | **Refused entry** |

The stadium states in capitals that anyone carrying bags outside the criteria **will not be allowed in**, and that bags which are half full and folded over to reduce their size will not be accepted — it is the overall size of the bag that counts, not how much is in it. A handbag counts as your one small bag. The stadium also notes that its rules differ from the **NFL Clear Bag Policy**, which matters for the NFL fixtures.

The arena's own FAQ page gives two different size limits — one answer says 30 × 40 × 20cm, another says bags larger than 40 × 35 × 19cm plus all adult backpacks of any size — so work to the stricter one and check your specific event page. The arena points customers to Stasher for storage in the local area.

> 💡 **This is where a hotel earns its keep.** All three Wembley hotels in our data offer luggage storage, and on an event day that is worth more than the room. It is the difference between killing three hours comfortably and dragging a holdall up Olympic Way to be turned away at the turnstile.

## Step-free access, Blue Badge parking and the shuttle

**Blue Badge parking at Wembley is not free and it is not on the street.** It is booked per event through Wembley Official Parking, in the **Blue** and **Red** car parks, at **£25 plus a £2.50 booking fee** for stadium events — exactly half the standard car rate. Arena events were £18.50 in Red. Wembley Park has over 200 accessible spaces in total, and **Pink and Green Parking have no Blue Badge bays at all**.

> ⚠️ **Book the moment you have a ticket.** Blue Badge and wheelchair bays were **sold out** for both the 12 September concert and the 26 September England international when checked on 9 September.

**Free Blue Badge parking that does exist:**

- **Brent Civic Centre**, Engineers Way HA9 0FJ — free for up to **three hours** in the designated bays, no return within one hour; ordinary visitor bays if none are free.
- **All Brent Council car parks**, including Preston Road, Northwick Park and Kingsbury Road — free for an **unlimited period** with a valid badge.
- **Harrow Council** car parks and on-street bays — free for vehicles displaying a Blue Badge.

**The stadium runs a free shuttle.** Wembley Stadium operates a complimentary, wheelchair-accessible shuttle from **Wembley Park station to the junction of Rutherford Way and Engineers Way**, without stopping, carrying up to ten people, roughly **every 30 minutes**. It generally runs from **30 minutes before gates open to an hour after the event finishes**, though hours vary by event and the operator is Brent Community Transport rather than the stadium. The meeting point is **by the lift entry at Wembley Park station, adjacent to Olympic Way**; afterwards it collects from Rutherford Way and Engineers Way. There is also a pushing and chaperone service from the drop-off point, which is not pre-bookable and runs at every event.

**Station by station**, the picture is uneven. **Wembley Park** is step-free street to train by lift, subject to the current works noted above, and if you are coming from stations east of Westminster you should board the correct carriage for level access; the Jubilee needs a manual boarding ramp, which staff at your origin station will arrange. **Wembley Central** is step-free from street to platform but **not from platform to train**. **Wembley Stadium station** has a step-free route between the platforms that runs via the street, about 300m, and Chiltern asks for two hours' notice to arrange assistance.

Inside the ground: **four lifts** from ground level to the concourse as part of the Olympic Steps, taking twelve wheelchairs at a time, plus lifts at each entrance and to every level; **147 accessible toilets**, opened with a RADAR key or by asking a steward; wheelchairs available to loan; two relieving stations for service dogs beside turnstiles B and M; and **free BSL interpretation of every concert**. The stadium's disabled access line is **0800 093 0824**; the arena's accessible booking line is **020 8782 5629**.

## Small print that catches people out

- **The stadium's sat-nav postcode is not the one you would guess.** Wembley Park's own advice is to use **HA9 0FA**, or simply to follow signs for Wembley Stadium once you reach the A406 North Circular. Car parks have their own: Red **HA9 0FD**, Pink **HA9 0HX**, Blue **HA9 0TU**, Brent Civic Centre **HA9 0FJ**.
- **The car parks are barrierless.** ANPR reads your plate and your confirmation carries a QR code. You can change the registration on a car booking up to an hour before the event, and 24 hours before for a coach. Turn up without a booking reference and you pay the full daily rate.
- **Coaches must be pre-booked**, go to Pink Parking, and are subject to a random mandatory security check. No alcohol on board and **no hold luggage without exception** — coaches that fail either are turned away with no refund.
- **No under-2s are permitted inside the stadium.**
- **The arena box office opens on event days only**, no earlier than an hour before doors, and closes about 15 minutes after the main act goes on stage. It is at the front right of the building on Arena Square; anything uncollected after that goes to Reception at the Door One entrance.
- **The arena does not publish stage times**, only doors and curfew, because stage times are set by the touring party. Events finish no later than the published curfew.
- **Residents inside the event-day zone can ballot for free tickets** to Wembley Stadium events through Brent — which is the council's way of buying back some goodwill for the 8am parking restrictions.
