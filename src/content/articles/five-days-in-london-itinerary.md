Five days is the first length at which London stops being a race. Three days covers the essentials; the fourth and fifth days are what turn a sightseeing trip into a visit.

The structure here is deliberate: **the central landmarks first while your energy is highest, the river on day four, and a whole day in one neighbourhood at the end**, when you know enough about the city to choose which one.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** Days 1–3 are the [three-day plan](/articles/three-days-in-london-itinerary/) — Westminster, the Tower and South Bank, and one museum district. **Day 4** is Greenwich, reached by river. **Day 5** is one neighbourhood, chosen by market day.

## Days 1 to 3: the central essentials

These follow the [three-day itinerary](/articles/three-days-in-london-itinerary/) exactly, and that guide has the walking times and booking detail.

* **Day 1 — Westminster and the West End.** The Abbey, St James's Park, Buckingham Palace, The Mall, Trafalgar Square, then Soho or a show.
* **Day 2 — The Tower and the South Bank.** The Tower first thing, Tower Bridge, Borough Market, then the continuous river walk past the Globe, Tate Modern and the Southbank Centre.
* **Day 3 — One museum district.** South Kensington or Bloomsbury. Both free, both enormous.

With five days you have one advantage the three-day version does not: **you can move a day if the weather turns.** Day 3 is entirely indoors. Swap it forward on the wet day and push the river walk back.

## Day 4: Greenwich, and go by river

*Half a day minimum, a full day comfortably.*

**Take the boat, not the train.** The DLR is faster and the river is the point — the approach past Wapping and the Isle of Dogs, with Greenwich opening up ahead of you, is one of the best things you can do in London for the price of a fare.

Boats run from **Westminster, Embankment and Tower** piers and take **around an hour** from Westminster. Contactless works and Oyster gets a discount. Our [river boats guide](/articles/how-to-use-london-river-boats/) covers the services and which pier to use.

### What to see

* **Cutty Sark** — the last tea clipper, and you can walk underneath the hull, which is the bit that surprises people.
* **The Old Royal Naval College** — Christopher Wren's, and the **Painted Hall** inside is the reason to go. Ten years of ceiling painting, and the closest thing London has to the Sistine Chapel.
* **National Maritime Museum** — **free**, and much better than it needs to be.
* **Greenwich Park and the Royal Observatory** — the climb is short and steep, and **the view from the top is free**. The Observatory itself, with the meridian line, is ticketed.
* **Greenwich Market** — covered, and good for lunch.

See our [Greenwich guide](/articles/greenwich-area-guide/) for the detail.

### Getting back

**Walk under the river.** The **Greenwich Foot Tunnel** beside the Cutty Sark is free, open at all hours, and comes up at Island Gardens with the best view back at Greenwich there is. From there the DLR runs north through the docks on a viaduct — sit at the front, there is no driver.

That also puts you at [Canary Wharf](/articles/canary-wharf-area-guide/), which is now worth an hour or two rather than the twenty minutes it used to be.

## Day 5: one neighbourhood, properly

The day people remember. Pick **one** and stay in it.

> **Choose by market day, not by reputation.** Several of these are transformed by whether their market is running.

### Notting Hill and west

**Portobello Road Market is Saturday** — that is the day it is at full stretch, and also the most crowded. Friday is a good compromise.

Add the pastel streets, **Kensington Gardens**, and either Little Venice's canal or the Design Museum. See [Notting Hill](/articles/notting-hill-area-guide/).

### Shoreditch and Spitalfields

**Sunday is the day** — Brick Lane, Columbia Road Flower Market and Spitalfields all run together and the whole area is at its best.

Street art, the markets, a curry or a beigel on Brick Lane, and a destination dinner. See [Shoreditch](/articles/shoreditch-area-guide/) and our [street art guide](/articles/london-street-art/).

### Bloomsbury and King's Cross

The quiet option, and the best in bad weather. The **British Library**, the Regent's Canal towpath, **Coal Drops Yard**, and the squares in between. See [King's Cross](/articles/kings-cross-area-guide/).

### Camden and Regent's Park

**Camden Market runs daily.** Combine it with the canal walk to Little Venice, or Regent's Park and Primrose Hill for the skyline view. See [Camden](/articles/camden-area-guide/).

### Hampstead

The furthest from central and the most unlike it. The Heath, the ponds, Kenwood House, and pubs that predate everything else on this list. See [Hampstead](/articles/hampstead-area-guide/).

## Spread the evenings out

Five nights is enough for variety, and the mistake is stacking the good ones at the end when you are too tired.

* **A West End show** — book before you fly.
* **One proper dinner** — see [special occasion restaurants](/articles/special-occasion-restaurants-london/). Lunch at the same restaurant is dramatically cheaper for the same kitchen.
* **A view at dusk** — Horizon 22 or the Sky Garden, both free with booking. See [the views guide](/articles/best-views-london/).
* **Live music** — from the [100 Club](/articles/best-live-music-venues-london/) to a jazz late set.
* **A quiet pub** — [historic pubs](/articles/historic-pubs-dining-rooms-london/) has the genuinely old ones.

Do not schedule the big dinner after the longest walking day.

---

## Should you take a day trip instead?

Windsor, Oxford, Cambridge, Bath and Brighton are all reachable, and on a five-day trip **they cost you a London neighbourhood you have not seen yet**.

Our view: on five days, don't. Greenwich gives you the change of scene, the river journey and a completely different kind of London without leaving the fare zone. Save the day trips for a second visit or a longer one.

---

## Common mistakes

1. **Taking the DLR to Greenwich because it is faster.** The river is the experience. The DLR is the way back.
2. **Saving the neighbourhood day for last and then being too tired to walk it.** Keep Day 4 light if you need to.
3. **Ignoring market days.** Portobello on a Tuesday and Columbia Road on a Saturday are different places entirely.
4. **Booking a day trip out of London.** On five days it is a poor trade.
5. **Stacking every special evening at the end.**
6. **Doing both museum districts.** Day 3 is one of them. The other keeps.
