**Nearly every London restaurant closes on Christmas Day and no public transport runs.** The places that do open sell a fixed menu at a fixed price, paid for in advance and rarely refundable. Book early: the people who end up disappointed are the ones who left it until December.

Here is what actually opens, what each price band buys, and the two constraints that should decide your booking before the menu does.

---

## Book near where you are sleeping

**TfL runs no services at all on 25 December**: no Tube, buses, Overground, DLR, Elizabeth line, trams or river boats. What moves is black cabs, private hire, Santander Cycles and rental e-scooters, and cab fares are well above normal, assuming you can get one.

**Pick somewhere within walking distance of where you are staying, then choose the menu.** Most of what opens is in Mayfair, Westminster, the West End and along the South Bank, which is walkable from a lot of central hotels. See the [getting around guide](/articles/getting-around-london-transport-guide/) for how the rest of the festive week works.

---

## What actually opens

- **Hotel dining rooms.** The bulk of it. Hotels have guests who need feeding, so their restaurants open when nothing else does, which is why Christmas Day dining skews expensive.
- **Rooftops and destination restaurants**, usually inside or attached to a hotel.
- **A handful of pubs**, mostly larger ones with a proper kitchen, and mostly in zones 1 and 2.
- **A few chains.** Hard Rock Cafe, Browns and similar: considerably cheaper.

Essentially every independent restaurant in London, every food market and nearly everything outside the centre is closed.

---

## What each price band buys

| Band | Roughly | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chains and pubs** | £45–£90 | Three courses, a drink on arrival, a fixed sitting time. By far the best value with children. |
| **Hotel dining rooms and rooftops** | £110–£180 | Four to six courses, champagne on arrival, a decorated room, and a longer table booking. |
| **Grand hotels** | £275–£450 | Five or more courses, unlimited champagne in some cases, live music, and a two-and-a-half to three-hour sitting. |
| **The very top** | £460–£670 | The Ritz: £460 for dinner, £670 for lunch. |

**Children are usually priced separately and much lower**, often between a third and a half of the adult price. Check whether the children's price includes the same number of courses; it frequently does not.

## Booking: the part that catches people out

**Full prepayment, non-refundable, is normal on Christmas Day.** The Ritz wants the whole £670 before it will hold the table, and others take a substantial deposit on the same terms. A no-show on 25 December cannot be resold, so a booking is a purchase, not a reservation: read the terms before you pay.

**Book as early as you can.** DesignMyNight's booking data puts the average Christmas Day reservation at about 59 days in advance, which lands in the last week of October. The best-known dining rooms go well before that, and the Ritz takes bookings a year out.

Confirm these when you book, because they vary and are rarely on the listing:

- **The sitting time and how long you have the table.** Christmas Day almost always runs on fixed sittings, and a 12.30 lunch may need to be out by 3.
- **Whether drinks are included.** "Champagne on arrival" means one glass. A £130 menu can become £200 a head with wine.
- **The children's menu and price**, including what age the child rate stops at.
- **Dietary requirements**, which usually have to be given days in advance on a set menu.

## Festive menus through December

For a Christmas meal out but not on the day itself, the rest of December has far more choice, lower prices and no transport problem.

**Festive set menus run from late November to Christmas Eve** at a large share of London restaurants, typically two or three courses for £40 to £85, and often with a group minimum. The good rooms book out for Thursday and Friday evenings in the first three weeks of December; a Monday or Tuesday booking is easier to get and frequently cheaper.

**Christmas Eve is the quiet opportunity.** Restaurants are open with normal service, and it is far easier to book than either the party weeks before it or the day after.

For a meal out that is not tied to a festive menu, the [special occasion restaurants guide](/articles/special-occasion-restaurants-london/) covers the rooms worth booking any time of year, and the [Sunday roast guide](/articles/best-sunday-roast-london/) covers the closest thing to a Christmas dinner you can get in January.

## If you would rather not sit down to a full lunch

**Christmas afternoon tea** runs from early November to the first week of January, costs £70 to £130, and is a much easier booking. Claridge's, the Landmark's Winter Garden, Fortnum & Mason and the Ritz all run Christmas tea menus. The [Christmas guide covers those](/articles/christmas-in-london/#afternoon-tea-at-christmas), and the [full afternoon tea guide](/articles/best-afternoon-tea-london/) has every price.

A few serve it on 25 December itself, at a premium. [The Ritz](https://www.theritzlondon.com/christmas-afternoon-tea/) charges £125 per adult and £83 per child from 14 November 2026 to 6 January 2027, rising to £140 and £92 on Christmas Day.
