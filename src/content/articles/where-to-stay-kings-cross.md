Most people staying at King's Cross are here for a train. The Eurostar goes from St Pancras next door, six Tube lines meet beneath the two stations, and the East Coast Main Line to Edinburgh, Thameslink to Gatwick and the Luton train all start here. So the question worth answering is narrow: **how much does a room next to the terminus actually save you, and what do you give up for it?**

The timings are what decide it. **Eurostar asks Standard and Plus passengers to be inside St Pancras 75 minutes before departure, and the ticket gates close 30 minutes before.** The first southbound Victoria line train from King's Cross is 05:29 on a weekday, and on a Sunday the first Northern line train is 07:23. For an early departure the Tube is not an option at all, and that is when a room you can walk from is worth paying for.

What you give up is the neighbourhood. **The hotels are on the Euston Road side of the stations, and the part of King's Cross people actually visit is on the other side** — Granary Square, the fountains, Coal Drops Yard and Gasholder Park, all about ten minutes north of the concourse. There is almost nowhere to stay up there, so you book near the platform and walk.

> 💡 **The Short Version:** **St Pancras London** at about £350 is the only hotel inside the terminus, and the Chambers rooms are the ones in the actual 1873 building. **The Standard** at about £249 is the design answer, in a Brutalist council annexe with a red lift running up the outside. **The Megaro** at about £180 is the independent, two minutes from the platform, and its cheaper Essential rooms are *bigger* than its design ones. **Point A** at about £95 is the sensible booking for a train you cannot miss. **Kabannas** — the former YHA St Pancras, under a new operator — starts at £50 for a pod. And if you would rather not pay for the address, **Bloomsbury** is ten minutes' walk south and cheaper on every tier — **[Generator London](hotel:generator-london)** is £26 a bed.

## Which part of King's Cross and St Pancras

The name covers two mainline stations, a Tube station serving six lines, and a redeveloped goods yard to the north that feels like a different place. Which side of the tracks you end up on makes a real difference.

**Euston Road, Argyle Street and Belgrove Street** — the frontage facing the stations across the traffic — is where every hotel in this guide above £90 sits. You are two minutes from a platform, and on the busiest road in the area. Ask for a room at the back.

**Gray's Inn Road and King's Cross Road**, running south-east, is the cheap end: hostels, Point A, ordinary shops, and about ten minutes on foot to the concourse. It is plain rather than unpleasant, and it is noticeably cheaper.

**Granary Square, Coal Drops Yard and the canal**, north of the stations in N1C, is the King's Cross people mean when they say it has changed — squares, fountains, a university in a Victorian granary, the Regent's Canal towpath. **It has almost no accommodation at all**, so treat it as a ten-minute walk from wherever you sleep rather than somewhere to stay.

**There is no Elizabeth line here.** Six Tube lines meet at King's Cross St Pancras — Circle, Hammersmith & City, Metropolitan, Northern, Piccadilly and Victoria — and the one everybody now wants is not among them. Farringdon, one stop east, is the fix.

## The six places to sleep

### St Pancras London — the Midland Grand, and the only hotel inside the terminus

*About £350 · 207 rooms and 36 signature suites · Euston Road, NW1 2AR · Attached to the Eurostar concourse*

George Gilbert Scott's Midland Grand opened in May 1873, lost money for decades, shut as a hotel in 1935, and stood empty for almost twenty years after British Rail moved out in 1985. Betjeman, who led the campaign that saved it from the 1960s planners, called the demolition plan "a criminal folly". It reopened on 5 May 2011, 138 years to the day after the original.

**The thing to get right is which wing you book.** Rooms split between **Chambers**, the 1873 Gothic building, and **Barlow House**, the 2011 extension behind it. Barlow House is fully renovated and perfectly comfortable, and it is also not what you are paying £350 for. Some Chambers Junior and Grand Junior Suites look directly over the train shed.

**The suites carry a "Seat to Suite" service with Eurostar** — staff move you between room and platform — on a minimum of 72 hours' notice. There is a free QR-code self-guided tour of the building for guests, and **Booking Office 1869 downstairs takes non-residents on a £29 set menu**, which is how to see the interior without the room rate. Since 3 June 2025 the hotel has traded as St Pancras London, Autograph Collection, and a great many booking sites and guides still file it under St Pancras Renaissance.

### The Standard, London — the Brutalist one, with the lift on the outside

*About £249 · 266 rooms and suites · 10 Argyle Street, WC1H 8EG · St Pancras 2 min*

A 1974 Camden Council office annexe, kept rather than knocked down, directly opposite St Pancras. The old council library on the ground floor is now **The Library Lounge**, which still has the books, and which matters more than it sounds: it serves **from 7am to midnight Sunday to Wednesday and to 2am Thursday to Saturday**, so there is somewhere civilised to sit when you arrive off a late train.

**Decimo, the tenth-floor Mexican restaurant, is reached by a red lift on the outside of the building**, and it is the reason a lot of people book. It closes on Sundays and Mondays — worth checking before you plan dinner around it, because those are common nights to be here for a Monday-morning train. The eleventh-floor Rooftop is a summer proposition and runs to 9pm, Sweeties opens Wednesday to Saturday, and the gym is open 24 hours to guests.

**Read the room name before you book.** The entry grade is the **Standard King Interior**, and "interior" means what it says. Dogs stay free.

### The Megaro — two minutes from the platform, and the cheaper rooms are bigger

*About £180 · 1 Belgrove Street, WC1H 8AB · King's Cross and St Pancras both 2 min*

The independent in a field of chains, unmistakable from the street for the mural covering its whole front, and two minutes from both concourses by its own measure. **Six room themes**, individually designed, from a red-and-gold Pop Diva suite to corner suites fitted out by Diesel.

**The floor areas are published and they invert what you would expect.** A **Signature Design Room is 20–23 sq m** and a **Megaro Essential is 23–30 sq m** — the plainer, cheaper grade is the larger one. Above them the **Megaro XL runs to 35 sq m** — near enough double the 18 sq m a Standard room gets you at Broadwick Soho for two and a half times the money, as our [Soho and West End guide](/articles/where-to-stay-soho-west-end/) sets out. Book the Essential unless the theming is the reason you came.

Downstairs, **Spagnoletti** serves Italian from breakfast through to dinner and **Hokus Pokus** is a basement cocktail bar done as a Victorian apothecary. The hotel is dog friendly, and massages, facials and personal training can be booked into the room through the concierge.

### Point A Hotel King's Cross — the sensible booking for a train you cannot miss

*About £95 · 324 Gray's Inn Road, WC1X 8BU · 0.3 km to both stations · Air conditioning*

Small, clean, quiet, air-conditioned rooms with mood lighting and self check-in, 0.3 km from both concourses — six to eight minutes on foot by the hotel's own reckoning. The rooms are compact and the views are nothing, and for a night bolted onto a train neither is a real cost.

**Three practical things decide whether it works for you.** Breakfast is **complimentary if you book direct with free A List membership**, and it runs **from 06:30 Monday to Friday but only from 07:00 at weekends and on bank holidays** — too late for an early Eurostar, so plan to eat at the station. Rooms are **cleaned after three nights**, not daily. And **left luggage is £3 a bag but cannot be kept overnight**: bags must be collected before midnight, which rules out the classic late-flight manoeuvre.

Early check-in from noon is £15 and late check-out to 3pm is £25. Snacks and drinks are available 24 hours; the irons live in rooms on the second and third floors rather than in your room.

### Kabannas London St Pancras — the old YHA, under new management, from £50

*From £50 a pod · 79–81 Euston Road, NW1 2QE · Directly across Euston Road from St Pancras · Open 24 hours*

**This was YHA London St Pancras and it is not any more.** The hostel has left the YHA network and now trades as Kabannas, in the same purpose-built block facing the station across the road — so any listing still selling it under the YHA name is out of date, and so is any expectation of YHA membership rates.

The format is graded rather than dormitory-and-nothing-else. **Pods from £50** are a private berth inside a shared room; **Cabins from £60** are compact private rooms with their own lockable door and shared bathrooms; **Nests from £130** are private en-suite rooms with air conditioning; a family Tribe room sleeping four to six is listed as coming. Female-only pod rooms are available and the team is on the desk 24 hours.

**Two catches worth knowing before you book a pod.** It has **no lockable door of its own** — the room is secured by digital key, not the berth — and **no food or drink is allowed in pod rooms**, water excepted. Check-in is 15:00, check-out 11:00, and the ground-floor Commons does coffee, pastries, pizza and natural wine.

### Clink 261 — the hostel that is open, because the famous one is not

*261–265 Gray's Inn Road, WC1X 8QT · Ten minutes' walk · Kitchen, bar and café*

Start with the correction, because it is the single most out-of-date fact about budget beds in King's Cross. **Clink78 — the Victorian magistrates' court where The Clash stood trial, with beds in the old cells — is closed for renovation and its own site says it will not reopen until 2027.** Do not book it, and be careful with listings that still sell it.

Clink 261, ten minutes' walk from the station on Gray's Inn Road, is the one taking bookings. It runs **mixed dorms sleeping 1 to 18, female-only dorms of 4 to 10, pod beds with privacy screens, twin rooms and private rooms for up to six**, with a full self-catering kitchen — the thing not one of London's [dedicated capsule hotels](/articles/pod-hotels-london/) has — plus a bar and a café doing pizza and coffee.

**Check-in is 4pm and check-out 10am**, an hour earlier than Kabannas, with late check-out to noon charged. Dorm lockers need your own padlock. Booking direct saves 15%.

## The arithmetic that actually decides this

This is the part worth doing on paper before you compare room rates, because on an early departure it removes most of London from the list.

**Eurostar's recommended arrival at St Pancras is 75 minutes**, on a Eurostar Standard or Eurostar Plus ticket. It is 45 minutes on Eurostar Premier and 60 minutes for accessible travel. **The ticket gates close 30 minutes before departure** — 15 for Premier — and that one is not advice. Boarding is called about 20 minutes out, and a full train carries up to 894 people through those gates, so the queue is a real variable rather than a theoretical one. The EU's Entry/Exit System has been phasing in at the border here since 12 October 2025, which adds a step for non-EU passports.

**Now put the Tube against it.** The first southbound Victoria line train from King's Cross St Pancras is **05:29** Monday to Friday; northbound it is 05:42. The Northern line's first is **05:50** — and **07:23 on a Sunday**. The Circle line's first Sunday train is 06:57. Add a journey and a walk to any of those and **anything leaving St Pancras before about seven in the morning is out of reach of the first train of the day** from most of London.

So the premium is worth paying on a narrow set of mornings: **roughly £100 a night when you have an early train, and nothing when you do not.** If your Eurostar is at 10am, you can sleep somewhere cheaper and nicer and still make it comfortably.

**Two nights either side is usually the wrong shape.** Book the night before the train here, and the rest of the trip somewhere you would rather be.

## Getting in and out

**Six Tube lines meet here, but not the Elizabeth line.** Circle, Hammersmith & City, Metropolitan, Northern, Piccadilly and Victoria all serve King's Cross St Pancras. For the Elizabeth line, **Farringdon is one stop east** on the Circle, Metropolitan and Hammersmith & City — that is the change to make for Canary Wharf, Bond Street and the quickest route to Heathrow.

**Night Tube, and the trap in it.** The Victoria and Piccadilly lines both run through King's Cross all night on Fridays and Saturdays, and **the Piccadilly runs right through to Heathrow Terminals 2 and 3 and Terminal 5** — Terminal 4 is not served overnight. **The Northern line Night Tube does not stop here**, because it runs via Charing Cross rather than Bank, so a late night out that ends on the Northern line ends at Euston or Leicester Square and a walk.

**Gatwick is direct.** Thameslink runs from St Pancras to Gatwick in about 45 minutes for £10.70 off-peak — no changes, and better than anything involving Victoria. **Luton is 32 minutes** on the Luton Airport Express plus the DART shuttle, from £10 booked ahead. The Thameslink platforms are well below the main concourse, so allow for the descent with cases.

**The two stations are not one station.** King's Cross is the East Coast Main Line to York, Newcastle and Edinburgh. St Pancras is Eurostar, East Midlands and Thameslink. They are two minutes apart and going to the wrong one is the most expensive mistake available here.

## What is open when you get in at eleven at night

A late arrival is the normal case in an area built around trains, and it is worth knowing which front doors are still open.

**Booking Office 1869**, in the station's original 1869 ticket hall, serves seven days a week from 7am until late, with drinks and snacks from noon onwards and a DJ on Saturdays from 9pm. It takes non-residents, so it works whether or not you are staying upstairs.

**The Library Lounge at The Standard** runs to midnight Sunday to Wednesday and 2am Thursday to Saturday, and its bar, Double Standard, to 1am at the end of the week. **Hokus Pokus** under The Megaro is a late bar by design. **Point A** sells snacks and drinks around the clock, alcohol from 11am, and its luggage room shuts at midnight. **Kabannas** is staffed 24 hours.

**What this really buys you is the walk.** Arriving at 11pm into a hotel two minutes from the platform means no Tube, no bus and no bag-wrangling — and that, rather than the design, is what the £249 rooms on Argyle Street are selling.

## Where to stay just outside instead

For anyone without a train to catch at dawn, this is the more useful half of the guide. Everything below is ten minutes' walk or a handful of stops from St Pancras.

If you have not settled on an area at all, our guide to the [best areas to stay in London](/articles/best-areas-to-stay-in-london/) sorts the whole city by what the trip is for.

### Bloomsbury — ten minutes south on foot, and cheaper on every tier

The single best answer here. Bloomsbury starts about ten minutes' walk south of the stations and is the calmest run of streets in Zone 1 — garden squares, the British Museum, university buildings — while still being close enough to walk to a Eurostar with a case.

**[Generator London](hotel:generator-london)** is about **£26 a bed** at 37 Tavistock Place, in a former police building, with dorms of four to ten, female-only dorms, private rooms and a 24-hour reception. **It is a party hostel and says so**, with a bar built into a bus: the right choice if meeting people is the point of the trip, and the wrong one the night before a 06:00 train.

**[Zedwell Tottenham Court Road](hotel:zedwell-tottenham-court-road)** is **£81 to £206** for a windowless private room, carved out of a disused car park beneath Great Russell Street — London's first entirely underground hotel, two minutes from the British Museum. Sealed, soundproofed and totally dark, which is either the best or the worst possible preparation for a 5am alarm. Our [windowless rooms guide](/articles/windowless-hotel-rooms-london/) has the full comparison.

### Farringdon — one stop, and the Elizabeth line King's Cross does not have

One stop east on the Circle, Metropolitan and Hammersmith & City, and the fix for the single real weakness of a King's Cross base. **Farringdon is the interchange where the Elizabeth line meets Thameslink**, which means Heathrow without changing, Bond Street in minutes, Canary Wharf direct, and the same Thameslink trains to Gatwick and Luton Airport Parkway that call at St Pancras.

The area itself is Clerkenwell: Smithfield, Exmouth Market, a dense run of restaurants and the tail end of the City. **It is a business district first, which works in your favour at weekends.** Hotels there sell mainly to people who go home on Friday, so they discount on Saturday nights while tourist areas do the opposite. Price the same Saturday in Farringdon and beside the station before you book either.

The honest downside is that it empties out. Clerkenwell is a working district first, and on a Sunday a good deal of it simply does not open.

### Islington and Angel — one stop north, and somewhere open on a Sunday

One stop on the Northern line, or about twenty minutes' walk up Pentonville Road. Angel is what King's Cross is not: **a proper high street with restaurants, pubs, cinemas, theatres and antiques at Camden Passage**, running the length of Upper Street and busy seven days a week.

**This is the answer if you are staying more than two nights.** King's Cross gives you trains and a shopping yard that keeps retail hours; Upper Street gives you dinner on a Sunday and somewhere to go afterwards, one stop from your platform. Nowhere else this close to St Pancras is a neighbourhood in that sense rather than a transport interchange with hotels attached.

**Check the Northern line detail before you commit**, though, because it bites twice here. There is **no Night Tube through King's Cross on this line**, and the first Sunday train from King's Cross is **07:23** — so a Saturday night out and an early Sunday train are a bad combination from Angel.

### Marylebone — three stops down the Victoria line, if you want quiet

Three stops to Oxford Circus and a four-minute walk, which puts Marylebone about fifteen minutes door to door from St Pancras and in a completely different register: wide streets, a village high street, Regent's Park at the top.

**[Treehouse Hotel London](hotel:treehouse-hotel-london)** is about **£250** at 14–15 Langham Place, next to BBC Broadcasting House, and occupies the **top floors of the building**, so every room looks out over something rather than into a light well. Magic 8 balls, cuckoo clocks and Paddington Bears in the rooms without tipping into gimmick; Green Key Gold certified; Time Out gave it five out of five. The Nest rooftop bar and Madera, its Mexican restaurant, both take non-residents.

At roughly the same money as The Standard you are trading two minutes from the Eurostar for a room with a view and a neighbourhood that is pleasant to be in — which is the right trade on every trip that does not start with a 6am departure.
