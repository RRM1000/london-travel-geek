Gatwick has one railway station but several competing trains. The best choice depends much more on your London destination than on the word “Express”: Gatwick Express and Southern go to Victoria, while Thameslink crosses central London through London Bridge, Blackfriars, Farringdon and St Pancras.

> **The short version:** take **Southern** for Victoria and Clapham Junction, **Thameslink** for London Bridge, Blackfriars, Farringdon or St Pancras, and **Gatwick Express** only when its nonstop Victoria journey and extra simplicity are worth the premium. Buy a separate National Rail ticket if travelling south to Brighton or west to Reading rather than assuming London Oyster rules continue beyond the airport.

## Gatwick transport at a glance

Prices and normal journey times below were checked on **28 July 2026**. Rail times are approximate from Gatwick Airport station. Always check a live fare for the exact train, time and London destination.

| Option | Typical adult cost to central London | Approximate time | Comfort | Best for |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Southern to Victoria | About £10.70 off-peak or £19.20 peak with Oyster/contactless | 32–40 minutes | 3/5 | Victoria, Clapham Junction and the best-value direct Victoria train |
| Thameslink | About £10.70 off-peak or £19.20 peak with Oyster/contactless to a common Zone 1 destination | 30–50 minutes, depending on station | 4/5 | London Bridge, Blackfriars, Farringdon and St Pancras |
| Gatwick Express | About £24.10 for a standard single before the direct-online discount | About 31 minutes to Victoria | 4/5 | The simplest nonstop Victoria journey |
| Coach | Advance fares can start around £6 | Roughly 75–120 minutes | 3/5 | A low advance fare, Victoria Coach Station or overnight travel |
| Airport taxi or pre-booked private hire | Obtain a live quote; central London is often £100 or more | Roughly 60–120 minutes | 4/5 | Door-to-door travel, groups, limited mobility and substantial luggage |

The £10.70 and £19.20 figures are useful examples, not a universal Gatwick fare. The charge changes with destination, route and time. Check the [TfL Single Fare Finder](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/single-fare-finder) and compare it with a National Rail ticket immediately before travelling.

For these rail journeys, peak pricing generally applies on weekdays during the morning commuter period, commonly **06:30–09:30**, with some evening peak rules depending on the direction and ticket. Weekends and public holidays are normally off-peak. The exact ticket restriction matters more than a general rule, so check the journey offered by the retailer before paying.

## Which route is best for your hotel?

| Hotel area or destination | Usually the best first choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Victoria, Belgravia or Pimlico | Southern; Gatwick Express if simplicity matters more than price | Both are direct to Victoria, but Southern is normally much cheaper |
| Westminster or St James's | Southern to Victoria, then walk, bus, Tube or taxi | Victoria is the most convenient London terminal |
| Chelsea or Sloane Square | Southern to Victoria or Clapham Junction, then a local connection | Avoids crossing central London unnecessarily |
| Battersea or Clapham Junction | Southern | Direct trains stop at Clapham Junction |
| East Croydon | Southern or Thameslink | Frequent direct trains and a short journey |
| London Bridge, Borough or Tower Bridge | Thameslink | Direct to London Bridge |
| Blackfriars, the South Bank or St Paul's | Thameslink | Blackfriars station spans the Thames and has entrances on both banks |
| Farringdon, Clerkenwell or the City | Thameslink | Direct, with an easy Elizabeth line interchange |
| King's Cross, St Pancras or Bloomsbury | Thameslink | Direct to St Pancras International |
| Paddington, Bond Street or Oxford Street | Thameslink to Farringdon, then Elizabeth line | Usually a useful step-free interchange with luggage |
| Liverpool Street, Shoreditch or Canary Wharf | Thameslink to Farringdon, then Elizabeth line | Avoids going west to Victoria before travelling east again |
| Waterloo | Thameslink to London Bridge, then Jubilee line; compare Southern via Clapham Junction | Choose according to disruption and luggage |
| Brighton | Southern, Thameslink or a southbound Gatwick Express service | Direct, but buy a separate rail ticket because Oyster does not cover the journey south |
| Reading or west of London | Great Western Railway | Direct trains avoid travelling into London; buy a National Rail ticket |

Check the hotel's actual address and nearest station. A property described as “central London” may be easier from Farringdon than Victoria, and the faster airport train can become the slower door-to-door journey after an unnecessary Underground transfer.

## Thameslink

Thameslink is the best default for a large part of central and north London. Trains run directly from Gatwick through:

- East Croydon;
- London Bridge;
- Blackfriars;
- City Thameslink;
- Farringdon;
- St Pancras International;
- stations farther north towards Bedford, Peterborough and Cambridge.

The route is especially useful with luggage because it can remove an Underground change. Farringdon provides a straightforward interchange with the Elizabeth line for Paddington, Bond Street, Liverpool Street, Canary Wharf and Heathrow. St Pancras is convenient for Eurostar, King's Cross and northbound rail services.

Thameslink trains normally have air conditioning, toilets and space around the doors, although airport services can become busy and dedicated luggage racks are not guaranteed. Check the destination display: not every train continues to every northern branch.

### Thameslink cost and payment

Oyster and contactless can be used between Gatwick and participating London stations. A typical adult pay-as-you-go journey to a Zone 1 Thameslink station is about **£10.70 off-peak** or **£19.20 peak**, but the exact fare depends on the destination and route.

Use the same physical card or the same device at both ends. A bank card loaded into a phone is treated separately from the physical card. Gatwick is outside the ordinary London fare zones, so special airport fares and caps apply.

A paper or digital National Rail ticket may be cheaper when:

- you have a Railcard;
- children are travelling;
- three or more adults qualify for GroupSave;
- you need a return rather than two unrelated singles;
- you are continuing beyond the Oyster area.

Contactless charges an adult fare and cannot apply an ordinary National Rail child or Railcard discount. Compare rather than assuming tapping is always cheapest.

## Southern

Southern is usually the best-value direct route to **London Victoria**. Many trains call at East Croydon and Clapham Junction before Victoria, making Southern more useful than Gatwick Express for south and southwest London.

The fastest Southern trains can reach Victoria only a few minutes later than the nonstop Gatwick Express. They may make more stops and can be busy with commuters, but the price difference—especially off-peak—often outweighs the small time saving.

Choose Southern for:

- Victoria without paying the Express premium;
- Clapham Junction, Battersea and useful southwest-London connections;
- East Croydon;
- destinations where a Southern through ticket continues beyond London.

### Southern cost and payment

Oyster and contactless are accepted between Gatwick and Victoria, Clapham Junction and other participating stations. The common Gatwick-to-Zone-1 pay-as-you-go example is about **£10.70 off-peak** or **£19.20 peak**.

At Victoria, use the normal Southern platforms—typically platforms **15–19**—for the lower Southern fare. Passing through the dedicated Gatwick Express gates at platforms **13 and 14** can trigger the higher Express fare even when the origin and destination are identical.

Southern offers child fares and eligible National Rail discounts. Gatwick Airport currently advertises a **£2 child fare with a paying adult** on qualifying Southern journeys and **GroupSave savings of one third** for eligible groups of three or more adults. Check the current terms before relying on either offer.

## Gatwick Express

Gatwick Express runs nonstop between Gatwick Airport and London Victoria, with a normal advertised journey of about **31 minutes**. Some branded trains continue south of Gatwick, but the airport–Victoria section is the one most visitors mean.

![The seating inside a Gatwick Express train.](../../assets/articles/gatwick-airport/gatwick-express-interior.jpg)

*Inside a Gatwick Express train. Photo: [Ameerali786](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2_Gatwick_Express_interior.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).*

The service has air-conditioned trains, luggage space and a simple nonstop journey. It is comfortable, but it shares the same railway corridor as Southern and arrives at the same London terminal.

### Gatwick Express fares

| Ticket or payment method | Typical adult price | Important point |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Standard single | About £24.10 | Check the live fare before travel |
| Direct online or app purchase | 10% off the standard fare | Buy through Gatwick Express to obtain its advertised web discount |
| Oyster or contactless | Premium Gatwick Express pay-as-you-go fare | Touch through the dedicated Express gates; no Railcard or child discount on ordinary contactless |
| Return, Web Duo, child, Railcard or First Class | Varies | Compare the appropriate ticket rather than paying two adult singles |

Prices and promotional products change, so use the [official Gatwick Express ticket page](https://www.gatwickexpress.com/tickets) for the journey date.

### Is Gatwick Express worth it?

**Usually not for price alone.** Southern reaches the same Victoria station, often only a few minutes later, and can cost considerably less. Thameslink is more convenient for much of central, east and north London because it avoids travelling to Victoria at all.

Gatwick Express can still be worthwhile when:

- Victoria is your final station or your hotel is very close;
- you want the simplest possible nonstop journey after a long flight;
- a direct-online, child, group or return offer makes the total competitive;
- the next Southern service is disrupted, crowded or much later;
- you particularly value luggage space and a premium-style airport train.

Compare the **next actual departures**, not only the timetable duration. Waiting 15 minutes for an Express can erase its small journey-time advantage over a Southern train already at the platform.

Gatwick Express, Southern, Thameslink and Great Northern transferred into public ownership on **31 May 2026**. Their familiar brands, tickets and passenger journeys continued, so this administrative change does not require visitors to use a different ticket.

## Great Western Railway, coaches and local buses

Great Western Railway runs direct trains from Gatwick towards **Redhill, Guildford and Reading**. This can be far easier for destinations west of London, Oxford connections or the Thames Valley than travelling into central London and back out. Oyster is not valid for a Gatwick–Reading journey, so buy a National Rail ticket.

Coaches serve both Gatwick terminals, including National Express services towards Victoria Coach Station and other UK destinations. Advance fares can start around **£6**, but the cheapest tickets are tied to an operator and departure.

Coach facilities can include Wi-Fi, USB charging, air conditioning and a toilet. The disadvantages are road congestion and less predictable arrival times. A coach is most attractive when:

- you found a low advance fare;
- Victoria Coach Station is convenient;
- your flight arrives after the normal rail service;
- you are travelling directly to another city.

At North Terminal, coach stops are on the lower level outside International Arrivals. At South Terminal, they are on the lower forecourt. Some routes serve only one terminal, so check the stop before waiting.

Local Metrobus and other bus routes connect Gatwick with nearby towns, including Crawley and Horley. They are useful for local hotels and staff travel, not usually for central London. Gatwick announced additional overnight trains and eight 24-hour bus routes for the 2026 summer period; check the live timetable because the precise service and seasonal dates can change.

## Taxis, private hire and hotel transfers

Use the official taxi desk or rank, or pre-book a licensed private-hire vehicle. Gatwick's official taxi partner offers quoted journeys, while Uber and similar operators use specified app pickup areas.

Central London is around 25 miles away. Traffic can turn the road journey into 90 minutes or more, and a live quote can easily exceed **£100**. For one traveller, rail is normally much cheaper. A suitable vehicle can make sense for:

- three or four people sharing the total;
- a hotel poorly connected to Victoria or Thameslink;
- several large suitcases;
- limited mobility;
- arrival after the last practical train or coach.

Confirm the complete price, pickup terminal, parking or waiting charges, child-seat arrangements and luggage capacity. Never accept an unsolicited ride from someone approaching inside arrivals.

Drivers dropping passengers at the terminal currently pay **£10 for the first 10 minutes**, then £1 for each additional minute up to 20 minutes, with a maximum stay of 30 minutes. For a free alternative, use the Long Stay car park, which currently provides up to two hours free parking and a shuttle to the terminal.

## Understanding Gatwick's terminals

Gatwick has two passenger terminals: **North Terminal** and **South Terminal**. The railway station is at **South Terminal**, about a five-minute signed walk from departures and arrivals.

| Terminal | Railway access | Important layout point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| South Terminal | Direct access to Gatwick Airport railway station | Follow signs between the concourse, arrivals, departures and station |
| North Terminal | Free inter-terminal shuttle to South Terminal, then follow signs to the station | Allow time for the shuttle, walking and waiting |

The automated inter-terminal shuttle is free, operates 24 hours a day and takes roughly **two minutes**. Luggage trolleys may be taken onto it. At South Terminal, follow the signs for North Terminal; at North Terminal, follow the signs for trains and South Terminal.

Do not guess the terminal from the airline. Assignments can change, and an airline may use different terminals for different flights. Check the boarding pass, airline app or Gatwick's [live flight information](https://www.gatwickairport.com/) before travelling.

If you accidentally reach the wrong terminal, use the shuttle rather than trying to walk outside between terminals. Include at least 15–20 minutes in your plan for finding it, waiting, travelling and reaching the correct check-in area.

## Travelling to Gatwick for a departure

First verify:

1. the departure terminal;
2. the airline's check-in and bag-drop deadline;
3. live flight status;
4. engineering work or disruption on the chosen railway;
5. the time needed for the North Terminal shuttle, if required.

Use Gatwick's [live flight information](https://www.gatwickairport.com/) and your airline app before leaving the hotel. Gatwick does not make general flight calls, so watch the airport screens after arrival.

Gatwick advises passengers to check their airline's instructions and recommends arriving at least two hours before departure. Its general guide suggests check-in may open around four hours before long-haul flights, three hours before European flights and two hours before UK and Ireland flights. Airline rules take priority.

Check [National Rail live departures](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/live-trains/) and the relevant operator before setting off. Overnight services have improved for summer 2026, but Sunday engineering work and late-night schedules can still alter the route.

A delayed departure does not necessarily move the check-in or bag-drop deadline. Do not deliberately arrive late unless the airline explicitly tells you to.

## Arriving at Gatwick: SIM cards and Wi-Fi

For a full comparison of airport shops, local mobile stores and installing an eSIM before flying, read [Should You Buy a SIM Card at the Airport?](/articles/should-you-buy-sim-card-at-airport/).

Gatwick provides free, unlimited airport Wi-Fi at up to 50 Mbps. Connect to **Free Gatwick Wi-Fi Vinci Airports** and enter an email address. A paid 100 Mbps option is also advertised for £5 for 24 hours or through myGatwick.

Physical SIM cards are sold in airport shops, including WHSmith and Excess Baggage locations in the terminals. Airport availability is convenient, but it is not automatically the cheapest option.

Before buying:

- check whether the existing mobile plan includes UK roaming;
- check that the phone is unlocked;
- compare the data allowance, speed and validity period;
- consider installing a UK or travel eSIM before flying;
- retain access to the number used for bank or login verification codes.

For a short visit, an eSIM installed before departure is often the simplest choice. For a longer stay, compare airport offers with prepaid plans from UK mobile shops, supermarkets and convenience stores after reaching London. Free airport Wi-Fi is enough to load directions or contact a hotel before deciding.

## Useful apps to prepare

- **Airline app:** check-in, boarding pass, terminal changes and disruption.
- **National Rail:** live departures, platform information and rail journey planning.
- **Southern or Thameslink app:** tickets and service updates for the non-Express trains.
- **Gatwick Express app:** only necessary if buying or managing an Express ticket.
- **TfL Go:** onward London routes and live transport status.
- **Citymapper or Google Maps:** compare the complete journey to the hotel.
- **Uber or another licensed operator's app:** optional for a pre-booked vehicle.

Download the boarding pass, hotel address and essential directions before flying. Keep an offline screenshot in case roaming or airport Wi-Fi is unavailable at the moment you need it.

## Luggage and accessibility

Gatwick Airport station has lifts and escalators, but platforms and trains can be busy. Thameslink, Southern and Gatwick Express services vary by rolling stock, so do not assume a particular luggage layout.

If you need assistance:

- request assistance from the airline and rail operator before travel;
- allow extra time for the North–South terminal shuttle;
- check lift availability for the complete London route;
- avoid a tight central-London interchange with several large cases;
- use a suitable taxi or pre-booked vehicle if the final address is awkward by rail.

Gatwick publishes an [assisted-travel guide](https://www.gatwickairport.com/passenger-guides/assisted-travel.html). National Rail's Passenger Assist service can arrange help at the station and with boarding.

## Common mistakes

- Paying the Gatwick Express premium without checking the next Southern train to the same station.
- Taking a Victoria train when Thameslink serves a station much closer to the hotel.
- Going to North Terminal without allowing time for the shuttle from the railway station.
- Using a phone to touch in and the corresponding physical bank card to touch out.
- Assuming a normal London Travelcard automatically covers Gatwick, which is outside the fare zones.
- Passing through Gatwick Express gates at Victoria when intending to pay the lower Southern fare.
- Using Oyster for travel south to Brighton or west to Reading.
- Buying an airport SIM before comparing roaming, eSIM and London prices.
- Treating a flight delay as permission to miss the original check-in deadline.
- Allowing no time for immigration, baggage reclaim, disruption or the terminal transfer.
