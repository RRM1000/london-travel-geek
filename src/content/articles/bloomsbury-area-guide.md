Bloomsbury is the quietest central district in London and the one with the most books in it. Between the British Museum, the University of London and a run of Georgian garden squares, it holds the country's densest concentration of libraries, bookshops and blue plaques.

Almost everyone comes for the British Museum and leaves immediately afterwards. The squares three minutes north are empty by comparison.

## Why visit — and who should skip it

**Come here if** you want the British Museum, or you want somewhere calm to walk in Zone 1. Bloomsbury is the only part of central London where you can sit in a garden square in silence and still be ten minutes from Covent Garden.

**Skip it if** you want evening life. Bloomsbury largely closes after office and university hours. Come during the day and eat in Soho or Covent Garden afterwards.

## Top sights and activities

1. **The British Museum** — Around eight million objects, of which roughly 50,000 are on display. The Rosetta Stone, the Parthenon sculptures, the Sutton Hoo helmet and the Egyptian galleries. Free. Use the Montague Place entrance.
2. **The Great Court** — Norman Foster's glazed roof over the old reading room courtyard, and the largest covered square in Europe. Free to walk into even without visiting the galleries.
3. **The garden squares** — Russell, Bedford, Gordon, Tavistock and Bloomsbury Squares. Most are open to the public and almost all are empty.
4. **The Charles Dickens Museum** — The Doughty Street house where he wrote *Oliver Twist* and *Nicholas Nickleby*, kept as a home rather than a display.
5. **Lamb's Conduit Street** — A pedestrianised independent shopping street of menswear shops, a good bookshop and The Lamb, a Victorian pub with original snob screens.
6. **The Foundling Museum** — The story of Britain's first children's charity and its Hogarth collection. Small, moving and rarely busy.
7. **The Cartoon Museum and the Wellcome Collection** — Two small free museums on the edges. The Wellcome's medical curiosities are genuinely strange.

## Key streets and micro-districts

### Great Russell Street and the museum precinct
The British Museum frontage and the souvenir shops opposite. The busiest part of Bloomsbury by a wide margin.

### The garden squares
Russell Square is the biggest and has a cafe. Gordon and Tavistock Squares to the north-west are the quietest and carry most of the Bloomsbury Group plaques.

### Lamb's Conduit Street
East, towards Holborn. Independent shops, The Lamb, and Persephone Books.

### Store Street and Marchmont Street
Small local shopping streets north and west of the museum, with better cafes than anything on Great Russell Street.

### Museum Street
The short run of antiquarian bookshops and print dealers between the museum and Holborn.

## Getting there

**By Tube.** **Russell Square** (Piccadilly) is closest to the squares. **Holborn** (Central, Piccadilly) and **Tottenham Court Road** (Elizabeth line, Central, Northern) are both about seven minutes from the British Museum and better connected.

**Best exit.** For the British Museum, come from **Tottenham Court Road** and approach along Great Russell Street — or better, walk round to the **Montague Place** entrance on the north side, which is far quieter.

**On foot.** Twelve minutes to Covent Garden, twelve to Soho, fifteen to King's Cross St Pancras.

## How long to spend, and when to go

| If you have | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **Two hours** | British Museum highlights via Montague Place |
| **Half a day** | Add the garden squares and Lamb's Conduit Street |
| **A full day** | Add the Dickens Museum, the Foundling Museum and the Wellcome Collection |

**Best time:** Weekday mornings at opening. The British Museum's late Friday opening is also much calmer than a weekend afternoon.

**Avoid:** Weekend middays at the museum, when the Great Russell Street queue is at its longest.

## Suggested two-hour walking route

1. **Start:** Tottenham Court Road station. East along Great Russell Street.
2. **British Museum:** Go round to the **Montague Place** entrance. The Great Court and two galleries.
3. **The squares:** North to **Russell Square**, then **Gordon** and **Tavistock** Squares for the blue plaques.
4. **Lamb's Conduit Street:** East for the independent shops and **The Lamb**.
5. **Finish:** South to Holborn, or on to Covent Garden.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Using the main museum entrance at peak times.** Montague Place on the north side has the same access and a fraction of the queue.
2. **Trying to see the whole British Museum.** Pick two or three galleries. It cannot be done in a day.
3. **Eating on Great Russell Street.** The cafes opposite the museum are poor value. Store Street or Marchmont Street are three minutes away.
4. **Coming in the evening.** Bloomsbury empties after work. It is a daytime area.
5. **Missing the squares entirely.** Most visitors never walk the three minutes north, which is where the character is.
6. **Assuming the small museums are shut.** The Foundling, Cartoon and Wellcome are all open and mostly free.

## Where to stay

One of the best-value central bases in London: quiet, walkable and close to the Eurostar.

- **Russell Square and Bedford Place** — Georgian townhouse hotels around the squares. Central and calm.
- **Gower Street** — A long run of small hotels at lower prices, walkable to the West End.
- **King's Cross** — Fifteen minutes north, better transport, more open in the evening.
