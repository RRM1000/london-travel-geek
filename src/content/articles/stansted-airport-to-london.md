Stansted lies about 40 miles northeast of central London. Unlike Heathrow or Gatwick, your decision comes down to two main options: take the Stansted Express for a fast, reliable train link to Tottenham Hale or Liverpool Street, or opt for a coach if it drops off nearer your hotel or offers a significantly cheaper advance fare.

> **The short version:** take Stansted Express to **Tottenham Hale** for the Victoria line and north London, or remain on the train to **Liverpool Street** for the City and Elizabeth line. Consider a coach for Stratford, Victoria, Baker Street, Paddington, Waterloo or an overnight journey. Contactless is now accepted on Stansted Express, but **Oyster is not**.

## Stansted Express

Stansted Express is the fastest and most reliable default. Trains normally run every 15 minutes, seven days a week. Liverpool Street takes about 48 minutes and Tottenham Hale about 37 minutes.

The airport station is directly below the single passenger terminal, around a two-minute signed walk using lifts, escalators or ramps. Trains have air conditioning and luggage space, although peak commuter sections can be busy.

### Tickets, contactless and Oyster

Advance tickets can start around **£9.45 one way**. Buy early through Stansted Express once your plans are confirmed.

**Oyster is not valid to Stansted Airport.** If you buy a rail ticket, retain it until after the airport gates.

Tottenham Hale is the best interchange for the Victoria line. Liverpool Street connects with the Central, Circle, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan lines and is linked to the Elizabeth line station. Follow the signs carefully because the walk between platforms can be substantial.

## Coaches to London

Stansted's coach station is opposite the terminal, about a two-minute walk. Services operate 24 hours a day.

National Express routes serve combinations of Tottenham Hale, Stratford, Liverpool Street, Shoreditch, Waterloo, Victoria, Baker Street and Paddington. Flibco operates routes including Liverpool Street, Stratford and Finsbury Park. Stops and route numbers change, so select the exact stop when booking.

Advance coach fares can start around **£5**. Coaches offer luggage storage and commonly have toilets, charging and air conditioning. Their weakness is traffic: the same journey can take far longer during a weekday peak, roadworks or an event.

Choose a coach when it stops close to the hotel, the railway is closed, or your flight is outside normal train hours. Allow a generous buffer on the return trip; Stansted advises travellers using a London coach to plan very conservatively.

## Taxis and private hire

The official taxi provider has a desk in International Arrivals. Private-hire services, including app bookings, use designated pickup arrangements.

Stansted is too far from central London for a taxi to be economical for most solo travellers. Request a complete quote including airport pickup, parking and waiting time. A vehicle can make sense for a group, several large cases, limited mobility or an address requiring multiple changes.

Never accept an unsolicited ride from someone approaching in the terminal. Confirm the driver, registration and vehicle against the booking.

## Understanding Stansted Airport

Stansted has **one passenger terminal**, so there is no terminal number to choose. Check-in, security, arrivals, the railway and coach station all connect to the same main building.

The departure gates are spread across satellite buildings. Depending on the gate, passengers may walk or use the airport transit system after security. Allow time after the gate appears on the screen; “one terminal” does not mean every gate is beside the lounge.

## Travelling to Stansted for departure

Check the airline’s live flight information before leaving. Stansted’s general itinerary recommends arriving at least **three hours before departure**.

The first Liverpool Street trains normally leave between about **03:40** and **04:40** depending on the day, and the last around **23:25**. Airport departures normally run from about **05:30**, with later starts on some weekdays, until around **00:30**. These are only planning guides: check the [**current Stansted Express timetable**](https://www.stanstedexpress.com/travel-information/timetables) for the exact times.

## Wi-Fi, SIM cards and useful apps

Use the airport's free Wi-Fi to retrieve directions or contact the hotel before buying mobile service. Check UK roaming and eSIM options before purchasing an airport SIM; city-centre and online plans may offer better value.

Read [Should You Buy a SIM Card at the Airport?](/articles/should-you-buy-sim-card-at-airport/) before deciding, or start with the [complete travel SIM and eSIM guide](/articles/travel-sim-esim-guide/).

Useful apps include the airline app, Stansted Express or Greater Anglia, National Rail, TfL Go, Citymapper or Google Maps, plus the selected coach operator's app. Save the hotel address and booking details offline.

## Luggage and accessibility

The airport station is step-free to the terminal, and the coach station has assisted-travel facilities. Request airline assistance at least 48 hours ahead where possible and arrange Passenger Assist separately for the railway.

The rail route is generally easier with luggage than changing between a coach and the Underground, but a coach stopping near the hotel may be simpler overall.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to use an Oyster card.
- Buying the cheapest advance ticket without checking its restrictions.
- Remaining on the train to Liverpool Street when Tottenham Hale is the better interchange.
- Taking a Victoria coach during heavy traffic without a time buffer.
- Assuming your departure gate is close because Stansted has one terminal.
- Using a phone to touch in and its physical bank card to touch out.
