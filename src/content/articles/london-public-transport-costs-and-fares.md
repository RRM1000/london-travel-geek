> 💡 **The Short Version:** **A London bus costs £1.75** and the **daily bus cap is £5.25**; on **1 November 2026** they rise to **£1.85** and **£5.55**. **A Tube single in Zone 1 costs £3.10 at peak and £3.00 off-peak** (Zones 1–2: £3.60 and £3.10). **The Zones 1–2 daily cap is £8.90** across Tube, bus, tram, DLR, Overground and Elizabeth line, as long as you tap the same contactless card, phone or Oyster every time. A paper single costs £7.00, so never buy one. Under-11s travel free and 11–15s pay half. Tube caps are frozen until March 2027.

Contactless or Oyster pay as you go is normally the cheapest choice for an adult visitor, because eligible journeys are capped automatically.

> 🌟 **3 Golden Rules for London Transit.**
> 1. **Always use Contactless or Oyster:** Never buy single paper tickets at station ticket machines.
> 2. **Stick to one card or device:** A phone and a bank card count as two accounts, and capping stops working.
> 3. **Let daily capping work for you:** it is almost always cheaper than a paper Day Travelcard.

Not sure which payment method to choose? Read the [complete Oyster card and contactless guide](/articles/oyster-card-guide-london/), including the current card fee, where Oyster works and when your own bank card is the better option.

## London bus fares 2026

**A London bus or tram costs £1.75 a journey, and the Hopper fare makes every further bus or tram you board within one hour free; the daily cap is £5.25, the 7-Day Bus & Tram Pass £24.70 and the monthly pass £94.90.** From 1 November 2026 the fare is £1.85, the daily cap £5.55, the 7-Day pass £26.10 and the monthly pass £100.30. Buses and trams charge one flat fare at any time of day, however far you ride. They don't take cash: pay with contactless or Oyster.

| Ticket or cap | Adult price to 31 October 2026 | Adult price from 1 November 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Single pay-as-you-go bus or tram fare | **£1.75** | **£1.85** |
| Daily bus & tram cap | **£5.25** | **£5.55** |
| Weekly cap (Monday to Sunday) | **£24.70** | **£26.10** |
| 1-Day Bus & Tram Pass | **£6.00** | **£6.40** |
| 7-Day Bus & Tram Pass | **£24.70** | **£26.10** |
| Monthly Bus & Tram Pass | **£94.90** | **£100.30** |
| Annual Bus & Tram Pass | **£988.00** | **£1,044.00** |

The passes load onto an Oyster card; a contactless bank card pays the single fare and the daily cap. For boarding instructions, live arrivals, and key routes, read our complete guide to [How to Use London Buses and Trams](/articles/how-to-use-london-buses-and-trams/). The new prices are on [TfL's bus and tram fares page](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/bus-and-tram-fares).

### How the Hopper Fare works

The **Hopper Fare** allows you to make unlimited bus and tram journeys across London within **60 minutes** for the price of a single fare.

* **Touch in only:** Always tap your card or device on the yellow reader when boarding a bus or tram, but **never touch out** when alighting.
* **Use the same payment method:** You must use the exact same contactless card, phone, or Oyster card for every tap so TfL can automatically link your journeys.
* **60-minute window:** Any additional bus or tram you board within 60 minutes of your first touch-in is automatically free.
* **Tube transfers:** Taking the Underground between two bus rides does not cancel the Hopper window. The Tube fare is charged separately, and the second bus is still free if you board within 60 minutes of the first.

For detailed journey scenarios, read the [worked examples in our bus and tram guide](/articles/how-to-use-london-buses-and-trams/#mixing-buses-and-the-underground).

## Peak and off-peak times

Peak and off-peak pricing mainly affects the Underground, DLR, London Overground, Elizabeth line and National Rail journeys using pay as you go.

| Fare period | When it applies |
| --- | --- |
| Morning peak | Monday to Friday, **06:30–09:29** |
| Evening peak | Monday to Friday, **16:00–18:59** |
| Off-peak | Monday to Friday before 06:30, 09:30–15:59 and after 19:00 |
| Weekends and public holidays | Off-peak all day |

The time you **touch in at the start** normally determines whether a single rail fare is peak or off-peak.

There are important exceptions:

- Journeys starting outside Zone 1 between 16:00 and 18:59 and ending in Zone 1 are charged an off-peak fare.
- On the Elizabeth line Reading extension, the evening contra-peak rule applies more widely to journeys heading towards London.
- It does not apply in the same way on Southeastern high speed, Gatwick Express or Heathrow Express.
- Tube and Elizabeth line journeys to or from Heathrow that start, end or pass through Zone 1 are charged peak fares.

Because the exceptions are journey-specific, check unusual or expensive routes in the [Single Fare Finder](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/single-fare-finder).

## Tube daily and weekly caps

**The Zones 1–2 daily cap is £8.90 and the Monday–Sunday cap is £44.70.** A cap limits what you pay for eligible journeys when you keep using the same Oyster card or the same contactless card or device. The TfL day runs from **04:30 to 04:29** the following morning. Caps are frozen until March 2027.

| Zones used | Daily cap | Monday–Sunday cap |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Zones 1–2 | £8.90 | £44.70 |
| Zones 1–3 | £10.50 | £52.50 |
| Zones 1–4 | £12.80 | £64.20 |
| Zones 1–5 | £15.30 | £76.40 |
| Zones 1–6 | £16.30 | £81.60 |
| Zones 1–7 | £17.80 peak / £16.30 off-peak | £88.90 |
| Zones 1–8 | £21.00 peak / £16.30 off-peak | £104.90 |
| Zones 1–9 | £23.30 peak / £16.30 off-peak | £116.40 |

For Zones 1–6 the 2026 daily peak and off-peak caps are the same. Some outer-zone combinations have a lower off-peak cap; consult TfL's [complete 2026 caps table](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/adult-fares.pdf).

The weekly cap follows a fixed **Monday-to-Sunday** week, not a rolling seven days. If your trip starts mid-week, a 7-Day Travelcard may be better value.

Heathrow Express and Southeastern high-speed journeys are excluded from these caps and from Travelcards. River Bus and Cable Car fares are also separate.

## Comparing Daily Capping vs. 7-Day Travelcards

A **7-Day Travelcard** is only worth buying if you will travel a lot on 5 to 7 consecutive days and your trip starts mid-week, spanning two Monday-to-Sunday weeks. Otherwise pay as you go is cheaper.

* **Flexible Start Date:** Unlike contactless weekly capping (which strictly runs Monday to Sunday), a **7-Day Travelcard can start on any day of the week** you choose (e.g. Wednesday to Tuesday). It is valid for 7 consecutive days starting at 04:30 on your chosen start date, and can be purchased up to 30 days in advance.
* **Bonus River Discount:** a valid Travelcard takes **33% off** standard [Uber Boat River Bus fares](#river-bus-prices).

> **Quick Example (Zones 1–2).**  
> The daily contactless cap for Zones 1–2 is **£8.90**, while a 7-Day Travelcard costs **£44.70**. 
> - If you travel over **4 days**, contactless capping costs **£35.60** (saving you £9.10 compared to a Travelcard).
> - If you travel heavily for **5 full days**, contactless capping reaches **£44.50** (5 × £8.90), making a 7-Day Travelcard virtually identical in cost.

A 7-Day Travelcard costs the same as the Monday–Sunday cap for its zones.

> **Avoid paper Day Travelcards.** A Zones 1–2 Day Travelcard is **£16.60**, against the **£8.90** daily cap. Zones 1–6 is **£23.60** anytime or **£16.60** off-peak, against the **£16.30** cap. TfL has frozen Travelcard prices, and with them the caps, until March 2027.

## National Rail and airport rail fares

Trains inside London fall under two fare systems: **TfL zonal fares**, which count towards daily caps, and **National Rail operator fares**, which may carry their own rates or exclusions.

> ⚠️ **Key Rules for National Rail & Airports.**
> * **Oyster vs. Contactless Boundaries:** Contactless is accepted much farther out than Oyster. Both work to Gatwick, but only contactless works to Luton Airport Parkway, Stansted Airport (since March 2026) and Southend Airport.
> * **Excluded Premium Express Trains:** **Heathrow Express** and **Southeastern high-speed** trains are excluded from TfL caps and Travelcards. **Gatwick Express** is excluded from the Gatwick Airport pay-as-you-go cap.

To check exact pay-as-you-go rates, use TfL's [Single Fare Finder](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/single-fare-finder). For long-distance, return, or Railcard tickets, book via [National Rail](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/).

> 💡 **The one case where a paper rail ticket beats contactless.** If you are travelling into London on National Rail and plan to visit paid attractions, **keep the ticket** — it unlocks **2FOR1 entry** at the Tower of London, Kew, Westminster Abbey and more than a hundred other London offers through the Days Out Guide scheme. Contactless, Oyster, Freedom Passes and Travelcards bought at Tube stations do **not** qualify, and there is no way to print a TfL journey history as a substitute. Pay for the Tube with contactless, but keep the rail ticket out of the barrier. Full rules, the attractions that are only a third off, and the blackout dates are in our [National Rail 2FOR1 guide](/articles/national-rail-2for1-london-attractions/).

### Heathrow Airport Links

Heathrow has three rail options at very different prices: the Piccadilly line is the cheapest and counts towards your daily cap, the Elizabeth line is faster and also capped, and Heathrow Express is the fastest but is excluded from caps and Travelcards. Current fares, terminal breakdowns and route advice by destination are in the [Heathrow Airport Transport Guide](/articles/heathrow-airport-to-london/).

### Gatwick Airport & Other Airport Links

* **Gatwick (LGW):** Compare Thameslink (serving London Bridge, Blackfriars, and St Pancras) with Southern Trains and Gatwick Express to Victoria. Thameslink is often cheaper and more convenient for Central London. Read our [Gatwick Airport Transport Guide](/articles/gatwick-airport-to-london/).
* **Stansted, Luton & Southend:** trains to all three accept contactless, but **not Oyster**.
* **Complete Airport Overview:** Read our [London Airport Transport Overview](/articles/getting-around-london-transport-guide/#travelling-to-and-from-londons-6-airports) for all six London airports.

## River Bus prices

River Bus services in London are operated by **Uber Boat by Thames Clippers** under a separate fare structure from the Underground and buses.

> ⚓️ **Key Rules for River Bus Fares.**
> * **Caps & Capping:** Oyster cards and contactless payments are accepted at pier gates, but river journeys **do NOT count towards TfL daily or weekly caps**.
> * **1/3 Travelcard Discount:** Presenting a valid Travelcard (or having a 7-Day Travelcard loaded on your Oyster) gives you a **33% discount** on standard River Bus fares.
> * **Peak vs. Off-Peak Timing:** Uber Boat uses a unique time schedule: weekday mornings between **06:35 and 09:27** are billed as **Off-Peak**, while all other times—including weekends and bank holidays—are billed as **Standard/Peak**.

| **Central + East or West** | £11.40 | £13.70 |
| **East or West Zone Only** | £6.20 | £7.40 |
| **All Zones** | £19.30 | £22.90 |

### Day Passes & Hop-On Hop-Off Tickets

An adult **River Roamer 1-Day Hop-on Hop-off Pass** (unlimited river travel for a full day) costs **£29.30 online / in app** or **£32.60 at the pier**. Children aged 5–15 pay half, and under-5s travel free.

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **One-way flight** | **£7.00** | **£3.50** |

### Key Cable Car Rules & Discounts

* **Payment Methods:** You can tap at the turnstiles using a contactless card, phone, or Oyster card. You can't top up an Oyster at the terminals.
* **No Daily Capping:** Cable Car rides are billed separately and **do NOT count towards TfL daily or weekly caps**.
* **Online Booking Discount:** [Booking online](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/ifs-cloud-cable-car-fares) saves up to **30%**; the full 30% is only on off-peak round trips booked 7 days ahead.
* **The O2 Event Ticket Discount:** Show a same-day ticket for The O2, indigo at The O2 or Up at The O2 at the terminal ticket office for **50% off** walk-up one-way and round-trip tickets (details on [The O2's cable car page](https://www.theo2.co.uk/visit-us/getting-to-the-o2/cable-car)).
* **Multi-journey ticket:** **£20** for 10 one-way journeys, valid 12 months, sold only at the terminal ticket offices.

## Black cabs and private hire (Taxis & Uber)

### Official London Black Cabs (Metered)
Black cabs can be hailed on the street, picked up at designated taxi ranks (major train stations all have one), or booked through apps like Gett. Fares are strictly metered based on time and distance:

* **Minimum fare:** **£4.40** upon entering the cab.
* **1-mile journey:** Approx. **£8.40 – £13.20** (depending on traffic and time of day).
* **4-mile journey:** Approx. **£21.00 – £35.00**.
* **Heathrow Airport to Central London:** Approx. **£70.00 – £120.00**.

Every licensed black cab has been required to take card and contactless since 2016, with the reader fixed inside the passenger compartment — see our [money in London guide](/articles/money-in-london/) for what else does and doesn't take cash across the city.

### Private Hire & Ride-Hailing (Uber, Bolt, FreeNow)
Private hire vehicles must be pre-booked through a licensed operator or app. Unlike black cabs, private hire apps show an exact upfront fare quote before you confirm your ride. Prices rise with demand.

## Top 10 money-saving transport tips for London

1. **Always use contactless or an Oyster card:** a paper single costs £7.00 whatever the distance.
2. **Use the same card or device all day:** a phone and a bank card count as two accounts, and capping stops working.
3. **Travel off-peak when possible:** Avoid peak hours (06:30–09:29 and 16:00–18:59 on weekdays) for significant savings on longer Tube and rail trips.
4. **Use the Hopper Fare:** every further bus or tram you board within 60 minutes is free.
5. **Tap the pink card readers:** On a journey that avoids Zone 1, tapping the pink reader mid-journey proves it and cuts the fare. See [the pink readers](#what-are-the-pink-card-readers) below.
6. **Skip paper Day Travelcards:** a Zones 1–2 Day Travelcard is £16.60 against the £8.90 contactless cap.
7. **Show a Travelcard on the river:** it takes **33% off** Uber Boat River Bus fares.
8. **Load a Railcard onto your Oyster:** staff at a Tube station can add a 16-25, 26-30, Senior, HM Forces or Veterans Railcard for a third off off-peak pay-as-you-go fares. Other Railcards, such as Two Together, can't be added.
9. **Skip Heathrow Express:** the Elizabeth line and Piccadilly line cost a fraction of the price and count towards the daily cap.
10. **Claim child fares:** under-11s travel free and 11–15s pay half (see below).

## What are the pink card readers?

**Stand-alone pink card readers, on the platform rather than at a gate.** They exist for one job: to prove you travelled between two outer zones **without going through Zone 1**, which is cheaper.

Without it, the system assumes you went through Zone 1 and charges you for it.

**Tap the pink reader when you change trains, and only then.** You still touch the normal yellow readers at the start and end of the journey.

**Where they are:** on or between the platforms at interchange stations including Highbury & Islington, Stratford, Canada Water, Gospel Oak, Willesden Junction, Clapham Junction, Richmond and Wimbledon ([TfL's full list](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/touch-pink-card-reader-when-changing-trains)). If there is no pink reader where you change, there is nothing to do.

**It only helps if your journey skips Zone 1.** Going through the centre and tapping pink on the way does nothing.

## Child fares: what children and teenagers pay

**Under-11s travel free on almost everything, and 11–15s pay half.**

| Child's age | Tube, DLR, Overground & Elizabeth Line | Buses & Trams | Required Card / Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Under 5** | **Free** | **Free** | No card required; walk through wide luggage gates with adult. |
| **Age 5–10** | **Free** (up to 4 children per accompanying adult; Elizabeth line only as far as West Drayton) | **Free** | No card required for TfL lines; use wide staffed gates together. |
| **Age 11–15 (with Young Visitor Discount)** | **50% off** adult PAYG fares and daily caps | **Half fare (£0.85 to 31 October 2026)** | Staff add it to an Oyster or Visitor Oyster card at a Tube or Overground station or a Visitor Centre; lasts 14 days. |
| **Age 11–15 (with Zip Oyster Photocard)** | Child fares and caps | **Free** | Requires advance online application (mainly for UK residents). |
| **Age 16–17** | Adult fare *(unless holding 16+ Zip Photocard)* | Adult fare | Use contactless card or adult Oyster. |

> 🧒 **How to get the 50% Young Visitor Discount.**  
> Buy a standard Oyster card for each child aged 11–15 and ask TfL staff at a Tube or London Overground station, or at a Visitor Centre, to apply the **Young Visitor Discount**. The child must be present. It takes **50% off** adult pay-as-you-go fares and daily caps for up to 14 days (the Zones 1–2 daily cap becomes **£4.45**).

> 👴 **Senior & Overseas Concessions.**  
> Free senior travel passes (such as the 60+ London Oyster or Freedom Pass) are strictly reserved for London or UK residents. Overseas senior citizen cards, student IDs, or international disability badges are **not accepted** for discounts on TfL services. A visitor's 16-25, 26-30, Senior, HM Forces or Veterans Railcard can be added to an Oyster card (see tip 8).

## Penalty fares and incomplete journeys

Travelling without a valid ticket or failing to touch in and out can lead to extra charges:

### Official Penalty Fare: £100 (or £50 if paid early)
If a TfL Revenue Protection Inspector inspects your contactless card or ticket and finds you do not have a valid fare, failed to touch in, or used an Oyster card beyond its valid zone boundary (e.g. at Stansted or Luton):
* **Standard Penalty Fare:** **£100**
* **Discounted Rate:** Reduced to **£50** if paid within **21 days**.
* *(Fare evasion is a criminal offence and can lead to prosecution.)*

### Maximum fares for incomplete journeys
If you make a mistake and **forget to touch out** at the end of your trip (or forget to touch in at an un-gated station):
* TfL cannot calculate where you travelled, so it charges a **maximum fare** instead.
* A maximum fare **does not count towards your daily cap**.

> 💡 **How to claim a refund for a forgotten tap.**  
> Wait 48 hours, as most are refunded automatically. If yours isn't, claim through your [contactless and Oyster account](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/refunds/apply-for-incomplete-journey-refund) or on 0343 222 1234 within 8 weeks of the journey.
